# Involvement of the CCL21/CXCR3 Axis in Neuropathic Pain

The chemokine C‑C motif ligand 21 (CCL21, also known as secondary lymphoid‑tissue chemokine, SLC, or 6Ckine) is a potent immuno‑modulatory factor that has been classically associated with the homing of naïve T‑cells and dendritic cells to lymph nodes via its canonical receptor, CC‑chemokine receptor 7 (CCR7). However, a growing body of evidence has revealed an **alternative, pronociceptive signalling axis** in the central nervous system: following peripheral nerve injury, neuronal CCL21 is markedly upregulated and released in the spinal dorsal horn, where it acts not only on CCR7 but also—and critically—on the **C‑X‑C chemokine receptor type 3 (CXCR3)** , which is expressed on spinal microglia. Engagement of microglial CXCR3 by CCL21 drives a robust intracellular cascade involving Gαi‑mediated MAP kinase (ERK, p38) activation and NF‑κB nuclear translocation, which polarises microglia toward a pro‑inflammatory M1 phenotype. This microglial response fuels the release of tumour necrosis factor‑α (TNF‑α), interleukin‑1β (IL‑1β), interleukin‑6 (IL‑6), and brain‑derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF), thereby amplifying central sensitisation, suppressing inhibitory neurotransmission, and maintaining mechanical allodynia. Pharmacological blockade of CXCR3 with selective antagonists (e.g., AMG487) or genetic deletion of *Cxcr3* or *Ccl21* consistently attenuates pain behaviours in models of traumatic nerve injury, diabetic neuropathy, and chemotherapy‑induced peripheral neuropathy. The clinical development of CXCR3 antagonists for other inflammatory conditions provides a repurposing opportunity, although the dichotomous roles of CXCR3 in host defence and tissue repair require careful therapeutic targeting.

## 🧬 The CCL21/CXCR3 System: Ligand, Receptor, and Physiological Functions

*   **CCL21 (SLC/6Ckine)** : CCL21 is a homeostatic chemokine encoded by the *CCL21* gene. It is constitutively expressed in secondary lymphoid organs (lymph nodes, spleen) and, at lower levels, in the central nervous system by endothelial cells and neurons. Under pathological conditions, CCL21 is rapidly upregulated in injured peripheral nerves, dorsal root ganglion (DRG) neurons, and spinal dorsal horn neurons.
*   **Receptor promiscuity**: CCL21 binds with high affinity to **CCR7**, the receptor that mediates its classical immune‑trafficking functions. Additionally, CCL21 has been identified as a functional ligand for **CXCR3**, particularly the **CXCR3‑A** splice variant, which is prominently expressed on microglia and some neuronal populations in the spinal cord. While CCL21 can act as a weak agonist or antagonist at CXCR3‑B, it is the CXCR3‑A isoform that transduces the pronociceptive signals in neuropathic pain.
*   **CXCR3 receptor isoforms and signalling**:
    *   **CXCR3‑A**: The predominant isoform in immune cells and microglia. Couples primarily to **Gαi/o**, leading to inhibition of adenylyl cyclase, activation of phospholipase C (PLC), intracellular Ca²⁺ mobilisation, and stimulation of the MAPK (ERK1/2, p38) and PI3K/Akt pathways.
    *   **CXCR3‑B**: Expressed on endothelial cells and some neurons. Coupling can be to Gαs or Gαi; often mediates anti‑proliferative and pro‑apoptotic signals, and may act as a physiological brake on CXCR3‑A‑driven inflammation.
*   **Cellular distribution in the nociceptive pathway**:
    *   **DRG neurons**: CCL21 is upregulated in small‑ and medium‑sized nociceptors after injury. CXCR3 is expressed at low levels on DRG neurons but is robustly induced on infiltrating macrophages and satellite glial cells.
    *   **Spinal dorsal horn**: CXCR3 is predominantly expressed on **spinal microglia**, where it is upregulated following nerve injury. Neuronal CXCR3 expression is lower but may contribute to direct sensitisation. CCL21 is released from the central terminals of primary afferents and from dorsal horn neurons themselves.
    *   **Supraspinal regions**: CXCR3 is present on microglia in the anterior cingulate cortex, hippocampus, and thalamus, where it may modulate pain‑related affect and cognition.

## 🔬 Mechanisms of CCL21/CXCR3 in Neuropathic Pain

Following peripheral nerve injury, the CCL21/CXCR3 axis is rapidly engaged in the spinal cord, acting as an **early and potent initiator of microglial‑driven neuroinflammation**.

### 1. Injury‑Induced Upregulation and Release of CCL21

*   **Neuronal source**: In the days following chronic constriction injury (CCI), spared nerve injury (SNI), or spinal nerve ligation (SNL), CCL21 mRNA and protein are dramatically upregulated in the ipsilateral DRG and in spinal dorsal horn neurons. CCL21 is packaged into dense‑core vesicles and transported to central terminals, where it is released in an activity‑dependent manner.
*   **Astrocytic contribution**: Activated spinal astrocytes also produce and secrete CCL21 in the later phases of neuropathic pain, sustaining the inflammatory milieu.
*   **CXCR3 upregulation**: Concurrently, CXCR3 expression is increased on spinal microglia. The CXCR3‑A isoform is the dominant transcript, and its levels correlate with the degree of microglial activation (Iba‑1 expression) and the severity of mechanical allodynia.

### 2. Microglial CXCR3 Activation and M1 Polarisation

CCL21 binding to CXCR3‑A on spinal microglia triggers the following cascades:

*   **Gαi‑mediated MAPK activation**: The Gβγ subunit recruits PI3K, leading to the activation of **p38 MAPK** and **ERK1/2**. Pharmacological inhibition of p38 (with SB203580) or MEK (with U0126) completely abolishes CCL21‑induced microglial activation and cytokine release, confirming that both kinases are required.
*   **NF‑κB nuclear translocation**: Downstream of MAPK signalling, the inhibitor of κB (IκB) is phosphorylated and degraded, freeing NF‑κB (p50/p65) to translocate to the nucleus. NF‑κB drives the transcription of a battery of pro‑inflammatory genes, including *Tnf*, *Il1b*, *Il6*, *Ccl2*, *Cxcl10*, and *Nos2* (iNOS).
*   **Microglial M1 polarisation**: The CCL21/CXCR3 axis is a key determinant of the M1‑like phenotype in spinal microglia. It promotes glycolysis, the production of reactive oxygen species (ROS), and the release of **BDNF**, which is a critical downstream effector for disinhibition.
*   **Pro‑inflammatory cytokine release**: Microglial CXCR3 activation results in a rapid and sustained secretion of **TNF‑α**, **IL‑1β**, and **IL‑6** into the spinal parenchyma. These cytokines act on dorsal horn neurons and neighbouring astrocytes, amplifying central sensitisation and creating a feed‑forward loop of inflammation.

### 3. Contribution to Central Sensitisation and Disinhibition

*   **BDNF‑TrkB‑KCC2 pathway**: CCL21‑stimulated microglia release BDNF, which binds to TrkB receptors on lamina I projection neurons. This activates a signalling cascade that downregulates the potassium‑chloride cotransporter KCC2, collapsing the chloride gradient and converting GABA/glycine‑mediated inhibition into excitation (or reduced inhibition). This is a canonical mechanism of mechanical allodynia.
*   **Direct potentiation of glutamatergic transmission**: CCL21/CXCR3 signalling in microglia can indirectly enhance the function of neuronal NMDA receptors. The released TNF‑α and IL‑1β phosphorylate GluN2B subunits via Src and PKC, increasing NMDAR‑mediated calcium influx and promoting long‑term potentiation at C‑fibre synapses.
*   **Loss of inhibitory interneurons**: The sustained neuroinflammatory environment driven by CXCR3 promotes the excitotoxic death of parvalbumin‑positive (PV⁺) GABAergic interneurons, as described in previous sections on caspase‑3 and calcium overload. Thus, CCL21/CXCR3 is an upstream driver of the structural disinhibition that characterises chronic pain.

### 4. Crosstalk with TLR4 and Other DAMP Systems

*   **Synergy with TLR4**: CCL21/CXCR3 signalling can prime microglia to become hyper‑responsive to damage‑associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) such as HMGB1 and S100A8/A9, which act via TLR4. The combination of CXCR3 and TLR4 agonists produces a synergistic upregulation of IL‑1β and TNF‑α, vastly amplifying the neuroinflammatory response.
*   **Chemokine network amplification**: CCL21/CXCR3 induces the expression of other chemokines, including **CCL2**, **CXCL1**, and **CXCL10**, which recruit additional monocytes, neutrophils, and T‑cells to the spinal meninges and parenchyma, perpetuating the inflammatory cycle.

### 5. Supraspinal and Systemic Roles

*   **Plasma CCL21**: Elevated circulating CCL21 has been observed in patients with chronic pain. Systemically, CCL21 may act as a chemoattractant for CXCR3‑expressing immune cells, but its central effects—mediated by CXCR3 at the blood‑brain barrier or in the circumventricular organs—likely contribute to pain‑associated sickness behaviour and fatigue.
*   **Hippocampal CXCR3**: In chronic pain, microglial CXCR3 is upregulated in the hippocampus, where it drives neuroinflammation that impairs neurogenesis and long‑term potentiation, contributing to the cognitive deficits and depressive‑like behaviours that frequently accompany neuropathic pain.

## 🔬 Preclinical Evidence of Modulation

### 1. Pharmacological CXCR3 Antagonists

*   **AMG487**: A highly selective, orally bioavailable, allosteric antagonist of CXCR3. In the **CCI model** in mice, systemic or intrathecal administration of AMG487 (3 mg/kg, i.p., daily for 7 days) significantly:
    *   Reverses mechanical allodynia and thermal hyperalgesia in a dose‑dependent manner.
    *   Reduces the number of Iba‑1‑positive activated microglia in the ipsilateral spinal dorsal horn.
    *   Suppresses the expression of TNF‑α, IL‑1β, IL‑6, iNOS, and BDNF.
    *   Restores the expression of GABAergic markers (GAD65, GAD67) and partially reverses the downregulation of KCC2.
*   **NBI‑74330**: Another selective CXCR3 antagonist that has shown similar analgesic effects in rat models of SNI, reducing mechanical allodynia and spinal microgliosis.
*   **Mechanism specificity**: The analgesic effects of CXCR3 antagonists are **abolished in *Cxcr3*‑/‑ mice**, and they do not affect acute nociceptive thresholds in naïve animals, confirming that CXCR3 is specifically engaged in pathological pain.

### 2. Neutralising Anti‑CCL21 Antibodies

*   Intrathecal injection of a CCL21‑neutralising monoclonal antibody 1 hour before CCI or SNL surgery significantly delays and attenuates the development of mechanical allodynia and thermal hyperalgesia. When administered after the establishment of pain (day 7 post‑injury), a single intrathecal dose transiently reverses allodynia for approximately 48 hours, demonstrating that ongoing CCL21/CXCR3 signalling is required for pain maintenance.
*   Systemic administration of anti‑CCL21 antibody also partially reduces pain behaviours, indicating that a peripheral pool of CCL21 (likely from the injured nerve or DRG) contributes to the overall pronociceptive tone.

### 3. Genetic Models

*   ***Cxcr3*‑/‑ mice**: Mice lacking CXCR3 are viable, fertile, and display normal acute pain sensitivity. Following CCI or SNI, *Cxcr3*‑/‑ mice exhibit a **profound reduction in mechanical allodynia and thermal hyperalgesia**, which is accompanied by:
    *   A near‑complete absence of injury‑induced microglial activation in the spinal dorsal horn.
    *   Markedly reduced levels of TNF‑α, IL‑1β, IL‑6, and BDNF.
    *   Preservation of KCC2 expression and GABAergic inhibition.
*   ***Ccl21*‑/‑ mice**: These mice show a similar, albeit slightly less pronounced, analgesic phenotype, consistent with the fact that CCL21 also signals through CCR7, and some residual pronociceptive signalling may occur via other CXCR3 ligands (e.g., CXCL10).
*   **Conditional CXCR3 knockout**: Mice with CXCR3 deleted specifically in CX3CR1‑positive cells (microglia/macrophages) recapitulate the pain‑protective phenotype, confirming that **microglial CXCR3 is the critical cellular site of action**.

### 4. Targeting Downstream Pathways

*   **p38 MAPK inhibitors (SB203580)** and **MEK inhibitors (U0126)** : Intrathecal administration of these compounds blocks the intracellular signalling downstream of CXCR3 and reproduces the analgesic effects of CXCR3 antagonists, further validating the CCL21/CXCR3–MAPK signalling cascade as a therapeutic target.

### 5. Non‑Pharmacological Interventions

*   **Electroacupuncture**: Electroacupuncture at ST36 and GB34 has been shown to reduce spinal CCL21 and CXCR3 expression, suppress microglial activation, and alleviate mechanical allodynia in CCI rats. The analgesic effect is partially reversed by intrathecal administration of recombinant CCL21, confirming target engagement.

## 🩺 Clinical Implications and Therapeutic Opportunities

*   **CXCR3 antagonists as a novel analgesic class**: The robust preclinical efficacy of AMG487 and related compounds, combined with their oral bioavailability and good tolerability in early clinical trials for other diseases (e.g., psoriasis, rheumatoid arthritis), positions CXCR3 as a high‑value target for neuropathic pain. The selective blockade of an early, microglia‑specific initiator of the neuroinflammatory cascade offers the potential for disease‑modifying analgesia with minimal direct neuronal toxicity.
*   **Repurposing of AMG487**: AMG487 has completed Phase I and Phase II trials for inflammatory disorders. Although its development was halted for those indications due to insufficient efficacy, its well‑characterised human safety and pharmacokinetic profiles could accelerate its evaluation in a neuropathic pain clinical trial, particularly if pain is now considered a primary endpoint.
*   **Biomarker potential**: Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) levels of CCL21 have been reported to be elevated in patients with chronic neuropathic pain and correlate with pain severity. Serum CCL21 or the ratio of CCL21 to its scavenger receptor CCR7 could serve as a non‑invasive biomarker for patient stratification and monitoring of target engagement.
*   **Combination strategies**:
    *   **CXCR3 antagonist + KCC2 enhancer**: Synergistic restoration of GABAergic inhibition by simultaneously removing the BDNF‑driven suppression of KCC2 (via CXCR3 blockade) and directly enhancing KCC2 function.
    *   **CXCR3 antagonist + anti‑TNF biologic**: Dual blockade of the upstream chemokine‑driven and downstream cytokine‑driven arms of neuroinflammation.
    *   **CXCR3 antagonist + opioid**: CXCR3 antagonism could prevent opioid‑induced microglial activation and hyperalgesia, allowing lower opioid doses and sustained analgesia.

## ⚠️ A Note of Caution and Complexity

*   **Receptor redundancy with CCR7**: CCL21 also signals through CCR7, which is expressed on some T‑cells, dendritic cells, and possibly some glial populations. CCR7 activation can mediate both pro‑ and anti‑inflammatory effects depending on the context. Complete CCL21 neutralisation could block protective CCR7 signalling (e.g., regulatory T‑cell recruitment) and inadvertently impair resolution of inflammation. CXCR3‑selective antagonists avoid this redundancy and are therefore preferred.
*   **CXCR3 in anti‑tumour and anti‑viral immunity**: CXCR3 is expressed on activated T‑cells (especially Th1 and CD8⁺ cytotoxic T‑cells) and natural killer (NK) cells, where it is critical for their trafficking to sites of infection and tumours. Chronic systemic CXCR3 blockade carries a theoretical risk of increased susceptibility to viral infections (particularly herpesviruses) and impaired tumour immune surveillance. The safety of long‑term CXCR3 antagonism in a chronic pain population, which is often elderly and may have comorbidities, requires thorough evaluation. Intrathecal delivery or peripherally restricted antagonists could mitigate systemic immunosuppression.
*   **CXCR3 in remyelination**: CXCR3 signalling on oligodendrocyte precursor cells and Schwann cells can promote their migration and differentiation, contributing to remyelination after nerve injury. Complete, sustained CXCR3 blockade could potentially impair axonal repair and remyelination, an effect that would be counterproductive in the long‑term recovery from neuropathic pain. The therapeutic window for CXCR3 inhibition must be carefully timed—perhaps restricted to the early inflammatory phase while allowing later regenerative functions.
*   **CCL21/CXCR3 in stroke and neurodegenerative diseases**: The axis is also implicated in post‑stroke neuroinflammation and Alzheimer’s disease, where its blockade has shown neuroprotective effects. This shared pathophysiology could be therapeutically exploited, but it also means that systemic modulation could have broader, potentially unintended effects on other CNS disorders.
*   **Sex differences**: Preclinical studies have largely been performed in male rodents. Given the established sex differences in microglial biology and chemokine signalling, the contribution of the CCL21/CXCR3 axis to neuropathic pain in females may differ and must be explicitly investigated.
*   **Interactions with other pain pathways**: The CCL21/CXCR3 axis is a critical upstream node in the neuroimmune pain network, converging with and amplifying:
    *   **BDNF–TrkB–KCC2**: The primary effector of disinhibition.
    *   **TNF‑α, IL‑1β, IL‑6**: The major pro‑inflammatory cytokines driving central sensitisation.
    *   **TLR4**: Synergistic activation of innate immunity.
    *   **P2X4/P2X7 receptors**: ATP‑gated purinergic receptors on microglia, which are often co‑activated with CXCR3 and share downstream signalling pathways.
    *   **CCL2/CCR2 and CX3CL1/CX3CR1**: Other chemokine axes that act in parallel to sustain neuroinflammation.
    *   **NLRP3 inflammasome**: CXCR3‑mediated NF‑κB activation provides the priming signal for NLRP3 assembly and IL‑1β maturation.
