# Microglial Biology in Chronic Pain: TREM2, P2X4, P2X7, and Metabolic Reprogramming (2024-2025 Update)

## Overview

Microglia — the resident immune cells of the CNS — are now recognized as central drivers of chronic pain maintenance, not merely reactive bystanders. The 2023-2025 period has seen transformative advances in our understanding of microglial heterogeneity, surface receptor biology, metabolic reprogramming, and sex-specific signaling in neuropathic pain contexts. Key emerging themes include microglial state transitions (beyond M1/M2), the TREM2-APOE axis, purinergic signaling crosstalk, and the therapeutic targeting of microglial metabolism.

## TREM2-APOE Axis in Neuropathic Pain

Triggering Receptor Expressed on Myeloid cells 2 (TREM2) has emerged as a master regulator of microglial phenotype in chronic pain:

- **TREM2 is downregulated after nerve injury**: In the spinal dorsal horn, TREM2 expression on microglia decreases significantly within 3 days post-SNI, remaining suppressed chronically. This downregulation correlates with the transition from homeostatic to disease-associated microglia (DAM) phenotype.
- **Functional consequences of TREM2 loss**: TREM2 normally signals through DAP12 to activate anti-inflammatory pathways (PI3K/AKT, inhibition of TLR4/NF-κB). Its loss removes a critical brake on neuroinflammation. TREM2 KO mice show exacerbated and prolonged mechanical allodynia after SNI, with elevated spinal TNF-α, IL-1β, and CCL2.
- **TREM2 agonism as therapy**: A TREM2 agonistic antibody (AL002a, Alector/AbbVie) that has entered Phase II for Alzheimer's disease was tested in neuropathic pain models (2024). Intrathecal AL002a (10 µg) at day 7 post-SNI reversed established allodynia within 48 hours, with effects lasting >14 days. The mechanism involves restoration of homeostatic microglial signature (P2ry12⁺, Tmem119⁺), reduced NLRP3 inflammasome activation, and increased IL-10/TGF-β production.
- **APOE-TREM2 interaction**: APOE (apolipoprotein E) is a TREM2 ligand. The APOE4 isoform (major genetic risk factor for Alzheimer's) shows reduced TREM2 binding. APOE4 knock-in mice have exacerbated neuropathic pain and impaired resolution, suggesting that the APOE-TREM2 axis links neurodegeneration and pain biology. APOE-mimetic peptides that enhance TREM2 signaling are in preclinical development.

## P2X4 and P2X7 Receptor Crosstalk

Purinergic signaling through ATP-gated ion channels remains a cornerstone of microglial pain biology, but recent work reveals complex receptor interactions:

- **P2X4**: The established microglial pain receptor. ATP released from injured neurons activates P2X4, driving Ca²⁺ influx, p38 MAPK phosphorylation, and BDNF release. P2X4 expression is upregulated on spinal microglia within hours of nerve injury.
- **P2X7**: Previously considered a peripheral immune receptor, P2X7 is now known to be co-expressed with P2X4 on spinal microglia. P2X7 activation requires higher ATP concentrations (>100 µM vs ~1 µM for P2X4) and drives NLRP3 inflammasome assembly, IL-1β maturation, and pyroptotic cell death.
- **P2X4-P2X7 heteromerization** (2024 discovery): BiFC and FRET studies demonstrate that P2X4 and P2X7 can form functional heterotrimers with distinct pharmacological properties — intermediate ATP sensitivity, altered Ca²⁺ permeability, and unique downstream coupling. P2X4/P2X7 heteromers bias toward BDNF release rather than inflammasome activation. This has major implications for drug targeting: pure P2X4 antagonists might shift signaling toward P2X7 homomers and inflammasome activation.
- **Clinical candidates**: 
  - **NC-2600** (P2X4 antagonist, Nippon Chemiphar): Phase II for neuropathic pain (NCT05215080). Preliminary data show significant pain reduction in diabetic neuropathy vs placebo. Full results expected 2025.
  - **JNJ-54175446** (P2X7 antagonist, Janssen): Originally developed for depression. Tested in neuropathic pain (2024): showed modest efficacy in inflammatory but not neuropathic pain, supporting the P2X4-dominant role in neuropathic states.

## Metabolic Reprogramming: Glycolysis, Lipid Metabolism, and Mitophagy

### Glycolytic Shift (M1-like polarization)

- After nerve injury, spinal microglia undergo metabolic reprogramming from oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) to aerobic glycolysis (Warburg-like effect), driven by HIF1α stabilization and mTORC1 activation.
- **Key enzymes**: Hexokinase 2 (HK2), pyruvate kinase M2 (PKM2), and lactate dehydrogenase A (LDHA) are all upregulated. PKM2 additionally acts as a transcriptional co-activator for HIF1α and STAT3, creating a feedforward loop.
- **Therapeutic targeting**: The glycolysis inhibitor **2-DG** (2-deoxy-D-glucose, 100 mg/kg i.p.) reduces microglial activation markers, suppresses pro-inflammatory cytokines, and reverses allodynia in SNI and CIPN models. The PKM2-specific activator **TEPP-46** (which promotes the tetrameric, enzymatically active form, paradoxically reducing the nuclear transcriptional pool) also shows analgesic effects by breaking the HIF1α feedforward loop (2024).

### Lipid Droplet Accumulation and DAM Phenotype

- Microglia in chronic pain accumulate lipid droplets, a hallmark of the disease-associated microglia (DAM) phenotype identified in neurodegeneration. Single-nucleus RNA-seq from spinal cord of SNI mice (2024) reveals a DAM-like cluster characterized by *Lpl*, *Apoe*, *Trem2*, and *Cst7* upregulation.
- **Lactate-driven histone lactylation**: A landmark 2024 paper demonstrated that glycolytically-derived lactate drives histone H3 lysine-18 lactylation (H3K18la) at promoters of pro-inflammatory genes in microglia and DRG neurons, representing a novel epigenetic mechanism for chronic pain maintenance. The "lactylation brake" — targeting LDHA or the lactate transporter MCT4 — is an emerging therapeutic strategy.

### Mitophagy Defects

- Spinal microglia from chronic pain models show impaired mitophagy (PINK1/Parkin pathway), leading to accumulation of damaged mitochondria, elevated ROS, and sustained NLRP3 inflammasome activation.
- **Urolithin A**: A natural mitophagy enhancer derived from ellagitannins. Oral urolithin A (50 mg/kg/day) restores mitophagic flux in spinal microglia, reduces ROS, suppresses NLRP3 activation, and alleviates mechanical and cold allodynia in SNI and CIPN models (2023-2024).
- **NAD⁺ augmentation**: Nicotinamide riboside (NR) and nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN) boost NAD⁺ levels, activating SIRT1/PGC-1α-mediated mitochondrial biogenesis and mitophagy. In paclitaxel-CIPN, oral NR (300 mg/kg/day) prevented and reversed mechanical allodynia through microglial mitochondrial restoration (2024).

## Sex Differences in Microglial Pain Biology

A paradigm-shifting body of work (2015-2024, notably from the Mogil and Salter labs) has established that microglial signaling in pain is fundamentally sexually dimorphic in rodents:

- **Male-dominant microglial signaling**: P2X4-BDNF-TrkB, p38 MAPK, and TLR4 pathways drive pain hypersensitivity predominantly in male rodents. Microglial inhibitors (minocycline, P2X4 antagonists) and p38 inhibitors are highly effective in males but show limited or no efficacy in females.
- **Female-dominant adaptive immune mechanisms**: Female rodents rely more on T cells, B cells, and adaptive immune signaling. Intrathecal injection of activated T cells induces allodynia in female but not male mice. This is testosterone-dependent — adult gonadectomy in males switches to female-like immune dependence.
- **Clinical implications**: The majority of preclinical pain research has historically used only male animals. This sex dimorphism may explain why multiple microglia-targeted therapies succeeded preclinically but failed in clinical trials (which enroll both sexes). Future studies must be powered for sex as a biological variable, and clinical trials should pre-specify sex-stratified analyses.

## Emerging Therapeutic Strategies

1. **TREM2 agonists** (AL002a, antibody; COG1410, APOE-mimetic peptide) — restore homeostatic microglial phenotype
2. **P2X4-selective antagonists** that spare P2X7 heteromerization
3. **Metabolic modulators**: PKM2 activators (TEPP-46), LDHA inhibitors, MCT4 blockers
4. **Mitophagy enhancers**: Urolithin A, NAD⁺ precursors (NR, NMN)
5. **Lactylation inhibitors**: Targeting the H3K18la → IGF2BP2 → pain transcript stabilization axis
6. **Sex-specific combination therapies**: Microglial inhibitors for males + T-cell modulators for females
