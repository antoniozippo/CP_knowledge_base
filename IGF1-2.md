# Involvement of IGF1 and IGF2 in Neuropathic Pain

The modulation of insulin-like growth factors (IGF1 and IGF2) is increasingly recognized as a significant factor in neuropathic pain, though their roles are distinct and complex. Evidence from preclinical models consistently shows that both can influence pain processing, but they appear to do so through different mechanisms and sometimes with opposing effects—IGF1 can be both protective and pain-promoting depending on context, while IGF2 is more consistently associated with driving and sustaining chronic pain states.

### 🧬 Distinct Roles of IGF1 and IGF2

IGF1 is a neuroprotective factor that also has a complex, dual role in pain. While it can promote neuronal survival and resolve acute pain, its signaling in the spinal cord is often upregulated in chronic pain states, where it can paradoxically contribute to both pain relief and pain exacerbation.

In contrast, IGF2 appears to be more consistently pro-nociceptive in the context of chronic pain. Its expression increases in the spinal cord and dorsal root ganglia (DRG) after nerve injury, and this upregulation is directly linked to the development and maintenance of pain hypersensitivity.

### 🔬 Preclinical Evidence of Modulation

- **IGF1 – A Double-Edged Sword**: In animal models, the role of IGF1 depends on the context of its modulation.
    - **Pain-Relieving Effects**: Systemic or central administration of IGF1 has been shown to resolve inflammatory hyperalgesia, prevent chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy (CIPN), and reverse neuronal hyperactivity. A study on electroacupuncture found that its pain-preventive effect in CIPN was mediated by IGF1 signaling in the spinal dorsal horn.
    - **Pain-Driving Mechanisms**: Conversely, in a chronic pain model (CCI), spinal IGF1 derived from astrocytes was found to be upregulated. Neutralizing this IGF1 or blocking its receptor (IGF1R) reduced pain behaviors, suggesting that abnormal IGF1 signaling actively contributes to pain. A 2024 study further revealed that the protein follistatin, upregulated after nerve injury, drives pain hypersensitivity by binding directly to IGF1R on nociceptive neurons.

- **IGF2 – A Pain Promoter**: Multiple lines of evidence point to IGF2 as a key mediator of neuropathic pain. In the spared nerve injury (SNI) rat model, a standard model for neuropathic pain, spinal IGF2 levels increased significantly after injury. Treatments that reduced IGF2 expression provided significant pain relief:
    - **Pulsed Radiofrequency (PRF)**: Immediate PRF treatment inhibited neuropathic pain development and produced a lasting effect, which was associated with a down-regulation of IGF2 and inhibition of ERK1/2 activity, primarily in microglial cells.
    - **siRNA-Mediated Knockdown**: Directly silencing IGF2 with intrathecal small-interfering RNA (siRNA) significantly inhibited mechanical and cold allodynia, with the analgesic effect lasting for 10 days.
    - **Molecular Pathway**: A 2025 study uncovered a unidirectional signaling cascade where the neurotrophic receptor NTRK1, also upregulated after injury, drives IGF2-dependent endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress in the spinal cord to sustain chronic post-surgical pain. Inhibiting IGF2 alleviated both the ER stress and the pain.
    - **Histone Lactylation & m6A Modification**: Emerging epigenetic research adds another layer of complexity. A 2026 study demonstrated that histone lactylation in injured DRG neurons drives expression of **IGF2BP2**, an mRNA-binding protein that stabilizes transcripts, contributing to chronic pain. While IGF2BP2 is not IGF2 itself, it highlights how the broader IGF signaling network is being implicated in pain pathogenesis.

### 🩺 Clinical Implications and Future Directions

While the preclinical data for IGF2 is robust, clinical translation is still in its infancy. For IGF1, the clinical picture is mixed. Early human trials with recombinant IGF1 (mecasermin) showed promise for certain conditions, but some studies have not found consistent benefit, and the potential for paradoxical pain enhancement warrants caution.

The IGF2 pathway, particularly the NTRK1-IGF2-ER stress axis, represents a novel and promising therapeutic target. NTRK1 inhibitors and IGF2 biologics could offer a new class of non-opioid analgesics for conditions like chronic post-surgical pain, though their safety and efficacy in humans remain to be tested.

### ⚠️ A Note of Caution and Complexity

A key complexity lies in the **dual nature of IGF1 signaling**. While promoting IGF1 activity can be neuroprotective and analgesic in some contexts (e.g., preventing CIPN), overactive IGF1 signaling in the spinal cord drives chronic pain. This is reminiscent of the paradox with TNF, where inhibition can relieve pain but may, in rare cases, trigger it. The finding that FST, a protein known to antagonize TGF-β cytokines, promotes pain by activating IGF1R further illustrates the intricate and sometimes unexpected interplay between neurotrophic factors and pain pathways.
