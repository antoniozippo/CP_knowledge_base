# Involvement of CX3CL1/CX3CR1 in Neuropathic Pain

The fractalkine (CX3CL1)/CX3CR1 axis is a unique neuron–microglia communication pathway that plays a critical role in the pathogenesis of neuropathic pain. CX3CL1 is the sole member of the CX3C chemokine family and exists in both membrane‑anchored and soluble forms, while its exclusive receptor, CX3CR1, is predominantly expressed on microglia within the central nervous system[reference:0]. After peripheral nerve injury, the CX3CL1/CX3CR1 signalling pathway is profoundly dysregulated, contributing to microglial activation, neuroinflammation, and the development of pain hypersensitivity through multiple intracellular cascades[reference:1].

### 🧬 The CX3CL1/CX3CR1 System: Ligand, Receptor, and Physiological Roles

- **Ligand (CX3CL1/Fractalkine)**: CX3CL1 is a transmembrane chemokine constitutively expressed in the CNS, particularly in intrinsic neurons of the dorsal horn of the spinal cord[reference:2]. It can be cleaved by proteases—most notably **cathepsin S (CatS)** and **ADAM10/17**—to release a soluble fragment that acts as a chemoattractant and signalling molecule[reference:3].
- **Receptor (CX3CR1)** : CX3CR1 is a G‑protein‑coupled receptor expressed primarily on microglial cells in the CNS, as well as on circulating monocytes, dendritic cells, and natural killer cells[reference:4]. Its exclusive expression on microglia within the brain and spinal cord parenchyma makes the CX3CL1/CX3CR1 pair a dedicated neuron–microglia signalling axis[reference:5].
- **Physiological Functions**: Under normal conditions, CX3CL1/CX3CR1 signalling modulates synaptic pruning, neuronal survival, and microglial homeostasis. It participates in the regulation of basic physiological activities during development, adulthood, and ageing[reference:6].

### 🔬 Mechanisms of CX3CL1/CX3CR1 Dysregulation in Neuropathic Pain

Following peripheral nerve injury, the carefully maintained CX3CL1/CX3CR1 balance is disrupted, leading to a cascade of neuroinflammatory and pronociceptive events.

- **Cathepsin S–CX3CL1–CX3CR1 Positive‑Feedback Loop**: The primary mechanism of CX3CL1/CX3CR1‑mediated pain is a feed‑forward loop involving CatS. After nerve injury, activated microglia release CatS, which cleaves neuronal membrane‑bound CX3CL1 to generate soluble fractalkine. Soluble CX3CL1 then binds to CX3CR1 on microglia, further activating them and driving additional CatS release[reference:7][reference:8]. This loop is necessary for the full development of nerve injury‑induced tactile allodynia[reference:9].

- **Intracellular Signalling Cascades**:
    - **ERK5 Pathway**: In the spinal cord, CX3CL1/CX3CR1 signalling activates extracellular signal‑regulated kinase 5 (ERK5) in microglia. ERK5 phosphorylation drives NF‑κB‑dependent transcription of pro‑inflammatory mediators, and knockdown of ERK5 reverses both CX3CL1‑induced hyperalgesia and spinal microglial activation[reference:10].
    - **p38 MAPK Pathway**: CX3CL1 binding to CX3CR1 also activates p38 mitogen‑activated protein kinase (p38 MAPK), which leads to the release of CatS, interleukin‑1β (IL‑1β), and other pronociceptive factors[reference:11].
    - **Akt1 Pathway**: In paclitaxel‑induced peripheral neuropathy, CX3CR1 mediates Akt1 activation in the dorsal root ganglia (DRG) and spinal dorsal horn, contributing to pain‑related behaviour[reference:12].

- **Monocyte/Macrophage Recruitment**: In chemotherapy‑induced neuropathy (e.g., vincristine), CX3CL1 upregulation promotes the infiltration of CX3CR1⁺ monocytes into the sciatic nerve. At the endothelial–nerve interface, these monocytes are activated by CX3CL1 and produce reactive oxygen species (ROS), which in turn activate TRPA1 channels on sensory neurons, evoking pain[reference:13][reference:14].

- **Interaction with TNF‑α**: Tumour necrosis factor‑α (TNF‑α) upregulates CX3CR1 expression in microglia via NF‑κB activation, creating a positive‑feedback loop that amplifies neuroinflammation. Pharmacological blockade of this TNF‑α–CX3CR1 pathway (e.g., with PDTC) suppresses microglial activation and alleviates neuropathic pain[reference:15].

- **Opposing Neuroprotective Mechanisms**: Importantly, the CX3CL1/CX3CR1 pathway is not exclusively pronociceptive. Enhancing CX3CL1 signalling can reduce neuroinflammation and alleviate neuropathic pain through **TGF‑β/Smad2/3‑dependent** pathways, promoting neural repair and resolution of pain[reference:16]. This dual role—neurotoxic versus neuroprotective—is a defining feature of the CX3CL1/CX3CR1 axis.

### 🔬 Preclinical Evidence of Modulation

Manipulation of the CX3CL1/CX3CR1 pathway in animal models has validated its causal role in pain.

- **CX3CR1 Knockout and Antagonists**:
    - **CX3CR1‑deficient mice**: Several studies report that CX3CR1‑null mice fail to develop mechanical allodynia in certain neuropathic pain models[reference:17]. However, other studies show that CX3CR1‑KO mice can exhibit increased allodynia after spared nerve injury, highlighting the pathway’s context‑dependent role[reference:18].
    - **CX3CR1 neutralizing antibodies**: Inhibition of CX3CR1 with neutralizing antibodies prevents Akt1 activation in DRG and spinal dorsal horn and alleviates paclitaxel‑induced pain behaviour[reference:19].
    - **CX3CR1 antagonists**: Systemic or intrathecal administration of CX3CR1 antagonists reverses pain‑like behaviour in experimental rheumatoid arthritis and other models[reference:20].

- **Targeting CX3CL1 or its Shedding**:
    - **CX3CL1‑neutralizing antibodies**: Blocking CX3CL1 reduces paclitaxel‑induced macrophage recruitment to the DRG, suppresses neuronal apoptosis, and attenuates mechanical allodynia[reference:21].
    - **Inhibition of CX3CL1 shedding**: Blocking the proteolytic cleavage of CX3CL1 by inhibiting CatS or ADAM10/17 reduces the availability of soluble fractalkine, thereby interrupting the positive‑feedback loop and preventing pain development[reference:22].

- **Enhancing CX3CL1 Signalling**:
    - **CX3CL1 overexpression in transgenic mice**: CX3CL1‑Tg mice exhibit reduced neuroinflammation, enhanced TGF‑β/Smad2/3 signalling, and significantly alleviated neuropathic pain after chronic constriction injury (CCI). Overexpression effectively reversed CCI‑induced differentially expressed genes and restored neuro‑immune functions[reference:23].

- **Downstream Pathway Inhibition**:
    - **ERK5 antisense knockdown**: Intrathecal injection of ERK5 antisense oligonucleotides suppresses hyperalgesia and spinal microglial activation induced by nerve injury[reference:24].
    - **NF‑κB inhibition**: Intrathecal infusion of PDTC (pyrrolidine dithiocarbamate) inhibits NF‑κB‑dependent CX3CR1 upregulation, suppresses microglial activation, and attenuates mechanical and thermal hyperalgesia in CCI rats[reference:25].

### 🧠 Beyond the Spinal Cord: Supraspinal CX3CL1/CX3CR1

The role of the CX3CL1/CX3CR1 pathway is not limited to the spinal cord. In the brain, increased microglial expression of CX3CR1 has been reported in pain‑related areas such as the thalamus, amygdala, and anterior cingulate cortex[reference:26]. This supraspinal activation may contribute to the emotional, cognitive, and motivational dimensions of chronic pain. The CX3CL1/CX3CR1 axis also mediates orthopaedic surgery‑induced cognitive dysfunction, underscoring its broader involvement in neuroimmune signalling beyond classical pain pathways[reference:27].

### 🩺 Clinical Implications and Therapeutic Opportunities

- **CX3CR1 Antagonists as Analgesics**: Centrally penetrant CX3CR1 antagonists represent a novel class of analgesics targeting the neuron–microglia interface[reference:28]. Preclinical data strongly support their use, and early‑stage clinical development is anticipated.
- **Inhibition of CX3CL1 Shedding**: Targeting CatS or ADAM10/17 to prevent the release of soluble CX3CL1 could interrupt the feed‑forward loop driving neuroinflammation. This approach is being explored for chemotherapy‑induced neuropathic pain prophylaxis[reference:29].
- **Dual‑Action Approaches**: Given the dual neuroprotective/neurotoxic nature of CX3CL1 signalling, therapeutic strategies may need to shift the balance toward the protective TGF‑β‑dependent pathway while blocking the pro‑inflammatory ERK5/p38 MAPK cascades.
- **Biomarker Potential**: Elevated CX3CL1 levels in cerebrospinal fluid have been reported in chronic pain patients[reference:30]. CX3CL1 and CX3CR1 expression profiles in immune cells could serve as biomarkers for patient stratification or monitoring treatment response.
- **Sex Differences**: Emerging evidence indicates that CX3CR1‑mediated signalling may have sex‑dependent effects in neuropathic pain. For example, a 3′UTR element in the Nalf1 gene is required for neuropathic pain in female but not male mice, and HNRNP L‑mediated post‑transcriptional control interacts with these pathways[reference:31]. Sex‑specific targeting may therefore be required.

### ⚠️ A Note of Caution and Complexity

- **Dual and Opposing Roles**: The CX3CL1/CX3CR1 axis exhibits both neurotoxic and neuroprotective functions depending on the context, pain aetiology, and downstream signalling pathway engaged. Blocking the pathway may inadvertently eliminate protective TGF‑β‑mediated effects, while enhancing it could amplify pro‑inflammatory cascades[reference:32].
- **Model‑Dependent Outcomes**: CX3CR1‑KO mice show reduced allodynia in some models but increased allodynia in others, indicating that the pathway’s contribution varies with the type of injury[reference:33][reference:34]. Therapeutic strategies must be tailored to specific pain aetiologies.
- **Physiological Functions**: CX3CL1/CX3CR1 signalling is essential for normal microglial surveillance, synaptic pruning, and neuronal health. Complete and prolonged blockade could impair cognitive function, synaptic plasticity, and immune surveillance in the CNS[reference:35].
- **Compensatory Chemokine Networks**: In CX3CR1‑deficient mice, compensatory upregulation of CCL2/CCR2 signalling in monocytes has been observed, suggesting that blockade of one chemokine axis may shift the neuroinflammatory burden to another[reference:36].
- **Interactions with Other Pain Pathways**:
    - **BDNF/TrkB**: CX3CR1‑activated microglia release BDNF, which downregulates KCC2 and contributes to GABAergic disinhibition. The p38 MAPK pathway activated by CX3CL1 is the same cascade that drives BDNF secretion.
    - **P2X4 receptor**: The P2X4–BDNF–TrkB–KCC2 cascade and the CX3CL1/CX3CR1–CatS loop represent parallel, but potentially convergent, microglia‑mediated disinhibition pathways.
    - **TNF‑α**: TNF‑α induces CX3CR1 upregulation via NF‑κB, and CX3CL1/CX3CR1 signalling in turn promotes TNF‑α production, forming a self‑amplifying inflammatory loop.
    - **CCL2/CCR2**: The CX3CL1/CX3CR1 and CCL2/CCR2 chemokine systems are co‑regulated in the spinal cord after nerve injury, and cross‑talk between them has been demonstrated in chemotherapy‑induced pain models[reference:37][reference:38].
    - **TRPA1**: In vincristine‑induced neuropathy, CX3CR1⁺ monocyte‑derived ROS activate TRPA1 on sensory neurons, linking chemokine signalling to ion channel‑mediated nociceptor excitation[reference:39].
    - **NLRP3 Inflammasome**: CX3CR1‑mediated p38 MAPK activation can prime the NLRP3 inflammasome, promoting IL‑1β maturation and amplifying neuroinflammation.
