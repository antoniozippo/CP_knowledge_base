# Involvement of PPARγ in Neuropathic Pain

Peroxisome proliferator‑activated receptor gamma (PPARγ) is a ligand‑activated nuclear receptor that, beyond its canonical roles in adipogenesis and glucose homeostasis, functions as a powerful negative regulator of inflammation and oxidative stress in the nervous system. In neuropathic pain, PPARγ expression is significantly downregulated in the spinal cord, dorsal root ganglia (DRG), and peripheral nerves, removing a critical brake on neuroinflammation. Pharmacological activation of PPARγ with thiazolidinediones (pioglitazone, rosiglitazone), endogenous ligands (15d‑PGJ₂), or natural agonists (cannabidiol) consistently alleviates mechanical allodynia, thermal hyperalgesia, and pain‑associated cognitive dysfunction in multiple preclinical models. These analgesic effects are mediated through a combination of NF‑κB suppression, Nrf2‑dependent antioxidant enzyme induction, macrophage/microglial M1‑to‑M2 phenotypic switching, and the restoration of mitochondrial homeostasis. The recognition that the non‑psychoactive phytocannabinoid cannabidiol exerts part of its spinal antiallodynic effect via PPARγ has further broadened the therapeutic landscape. However, the clinical translation of PPARγ agonists for neuropathic pain faces challenges related to the side‑effect profiles of classical thiazolidinediones and the need for isoform‑ and tissue‑specific targeting.

## 🧬 PPARγ: Gene, Structure, and Normal Function

- **Gene and protein**: PPARγ is encoded by the *PPARG* gene on human chromosome 3p25.2. It belongs to the nuclear receptor superfamily and exists in two major isoforms, **PPARγ1** (widely expressed) and **PPARγ2** (predominantly adipose tissue), generated by alternative promoter usage and splicing. In the nervous system, PPARγ1 is the principal isoform.
- **Activation mechanism**: PPARγ functions as an obligate heterodimer with the retinoid X receptor (RXR). In the absence of ligand, the PPARγ/RXR heterodimer is bound to corepressor complexes and maintains target genes in a repressed state. Agonist binding induces a conformational change that releases corepressors and recruits coactivators (e.g., PGC‑1α, SRC‑1), enabling the complex to bind to **peroxisome proliferator response elements (PPREs)** in the promoters of target genes. PPARγ also exerts anti‑inflammatory effects through **transrepression**—physically interfering with the DNA‑binding or transcriptional activity of NF‑κB, AP‑1, and STAT transcription factors without binding directly to PPREs.
- **Endogenous ligands**: The physiologically relevant endogenous agonists include the prostaglandin D₂ metabolite **15‑deoxy‑Δ¹²,¹⁴‑prostaglandin J₂ (15d‑PGJ₂)** , nitrated fatty acids, and oxidised phospholipids. These are produced at low levels in health but can be increased in inflammatory environments.
- **Synthetic agonists**: The **thiazolidinediones (TZDs)** —pioglitazone, rosiglitazone, and troglitazone (withdrawn)—are potent, synthetic PPARγ full agonists. **GW1929** is a non‑thiazolidinedione, tyrosine‑based PPARγ agonist used preclinically.
- **Natural agonists**: **Cannabidiol (CBD)** is a notable phytocannabinoid that directly activates PPARγ, an effect independent of CB1/CB2 receptors. Some polyunsaturated fatty acids and their metabolites can also serve as low‑affinity PPARγ ligands.
- **Tissue distribution**: PPARγ is expressed in adipose tissue (highest), vascular endothelium, immune cells (macrophages, microglia, T‑cells), and, importantly for pain, in **neurons, astrocytes, microglia, and dorsal root ganglion (DRG) cells** of the central and peripheral nervous systems.
- **Physiological functions**: Canonical roles include the regulation of adipocyte differentiation, lipid storage, and insulin sensitisation. In the CNS, PPARγ contributes to the resolution of inflammation, the maintenance of mitochondrial biogenesis, and the protection of neurons from oxidative and excitotoxic injury.

## 🔬 Mechanisms of PPARγ Dysregulation and Action in Neuropathic Pain

Following peripheral nerve injury, PPARγ expression and activity are pathologically suppressed, contributing to the unchecked neuroinflammation that drives pain. Pharmacological PPARγ activation counteracts this at multiple mechanistic levels.

### 1. Downregulation of PPARγ in Pain States

- In models of traumatic nerve injury (e.g., chronic constriction injury, spinal nerve ligation), spinal cord and DRG PPARγ mRNA and protein levels are significantly reduced in a time‑dependent manner, correlating with the development of mechanical allodynia.
- Paclitaxel‑induced chemotherapy neuropathy similarly suppresses PPARγ expression in the spinal cord.
- The mechanisms underlying this downregulation include the suppression of PPARγ transcription by pro‑inflammatory cytokines (TNF‑α, IL‑1β) via NF‑κB‑mediated inhibition of the PPARγ promoter, and possibly enhanced proteasomal degradation of the receptor protein.
- This loss of PPARγ tone removes a critical anti‑inflammatory constraint, permitting the sustained activation of NF‑κB, STAT3, and AP‑1 pathways that drive gliosis, cytokine production, and central sensitization.

### 2. Anti‑Inflammatory Mechanisms: Transrepression of NF‑κB and MAPK Pathways

The dominant analgesic mechanism of PPARγ is the suppression of neuroinflammation through transcriptional transrepression.

- **NF‑κB pathway**: Ligand‑activated PPARγ can physically interact with the p65 (RelA) subunit of NF‑κB, inhibiting its DNA binding and promoting its nuclear export. PPARγ also induces the expression of **IκBα**, stabilising the NF‑κB inhibitor complex and preventing NF‑κB nuclear translocation. The net effect is a marked reduction in the transcription of pro‑inflammatory cytokines (**TNF‑α, IL‑1β, IL‑6**), chemokines (CCL2, CXCL1), inducible nitric oxide synthase (**iNOS**), and cyclooxygenase‑2 (**COX‑2**) in spinal microglia and astrocytes.
- **MAPK pathways**: PPARγ activation suppresses the phosphorylation of ERK, JNK, and p38 MAPK in the spinal cord and DRG after nerve injury, reducing both the production and the sensitising effects of pro‑inflammatory mediators.
- **STAT3 pathway**: PPARγ can directly bind to and inhibit STAT3, preventing its dimerisation and transcriptional activity. This provides a direct link between PPARγ and the JAK/STAT3‑driven glial activation that sustains chronic pain.

### 3. Nrf2‑Dependent Antioxidant Defence

PPARγ agonists activate the transcription factor **nuclear factor erythroid 2‑related factor 2 (Nrf2)** , a master regulator of the cellular antioxidant response.

- The PPARγ/RXR heterodimer can bind to PPREs in the *Nfe2l2* (Nrf2) gene promoter, enhancing its transcription.
- Additionally, PPARγ activation can stabilise Nrf2 protein by preventing its Keap1‑dependent degradation.
- Nuclear Nrf2 drives the expression of a battery of antioxidant and cytoprotective enzymes, including **heme oxygenase‑1 (HO‑1)** , **NAD(P)H quinone dehydrogenase 1 (NQO1)** , catalase, and superoxide dismutase.
- The resulting reduction in mitochondrial and cytosolic reactive oxygen species (ROS) prevents oxidative sensitisation of TRPV1 and TRPA1 channels, protects mitochondria from permeability transition, and reduces NLRP3 inflammasome activation.
- In paclitaxel‑induced neuropathic pain, pioglitazone’s ability to mitigate mechanical allodynia is directly dependent on Nrf2/HO‑1 signalling, as the Nrf2 inhibitor ML385 or the HO‑1 inhibitor zinc protoporphyrin IX abolish the analgesic effect.

### 4. Macrophage and Microglial M1‑to‑M2 Phenotype Switching

PPARγ is a pivotal determinant of macrophage and microglial polarisation.

- Nerve injury drives microglia and infiltrating macrophages towards a pro‑inflammatory **M1 phenotype**, characterised by the production of TNF‑α, IL‑1β, IL‑6, and ROS.
- PPARγ activation promotes a metabolic and transcriptional reprogramming that shifts these cells towards an anti‑inflammatory, pro‑resolving **M2 phenotype**, which secretes IL‑10, TGF‑β, and arginase‑1, and enhances phagocytosis of debris.
- This switch is mediated in part by the PPARγ‑dependent induction of genes that support oxidative phosphorylation and fatty acid oxidation, the metabolic programs required for M2 function.
- In the GDPD3‑deficiency neuropathic pain model, GDPD3 deficiency alleviates pain by shifting the macrophage phenotype from M1 to M2 through a PGE₂‑PPARγ‑dependent pathway, providing genetic evidence for this mechanism.

### 5. Mitochondrial Protection and Restoration of Autophagy

PPARγ activation counteracts the mitochondrial dysfunction that is a hallmark of neuropathic pain.

- PPARγ induces **PGC‑1α**, the master co‑activator of mitochondrial biogenesis, restoring mitochondrial number and electron transport chain function in injured neurons and glia.
- It promotes mitochondrial fusion and counteracts fission, preserving the mitochondrial network and reducing the release of mtDNA and cardiolipin, which are potent NLRP3 inflammasome activators.
- PPARγ activation also restores impaired autophagic flux. By reducing oxidative stress and normalising the activity of mTOR and AMPK, PPARγ facilitates the clearance of damaged mitochondria (mitophagy), thereby breaking the vicious cycle of ROS‑induced ROS release and inflammasome activation.

### 6. Direct Neuronal and Synaptic Effects

Beyond glial modulation, PPARγ exerts direct effects on neurons that contribute to analgesia.

- PPARγ activation can suppress the hyperexcitability of DRG neurons by modulating the expression and function of voltage‑gated sodium channels (Nav1.7, Nav1.8) and TRPV1, reducing ectopic firing.
- In the spinal dorsal horn, PPARγ agonists reduce the release of glutamate and CGRP from primary afferent terminals and dampen postsynaptic NMDA receptor‑mediated currents, inhibiting central sensitization.
- PPARγ in cortical and hippocampal neurons may contribute to the attenuation of pain‑related cognitive impairment. For example, cannabidiol’s ability to reduce memory deficits and depressive‑like behaviours in neuropathic pain models is mediated, at least in part, through PPARγ activation in the prefrontal cortex and hippocampus.

### 7. PPARγ and Neurosteroid Synthesis

There is emerging crosstalk between PPARγ and the synthesis of neurosteroids. PPARγ activation can upregulate the expression of enzymes involved in neurosteroidogenesis, potentially increasing the production of allopregnanolone and other positive allosteric modulators of the GABA<sub>A</sub> receptor. This mechanism may contribute to the restoration of GABAergic inhibitory tone that is lost in neuropathic pain.

## 🔬 Preclinical Evidence of Modulation

### 1. Thiazolidinedione (TZD) Agonists

- **Pioglitazone**: The most extensively studied PPARγ agonist in pain models. Systemic (oral, intraperitoneal) or intrathecal administration of pioglitazone consistently reduces:
    - Mechanical allodynia and thermal hyperalgesia in **chronic constriction injury (CCI)** , **spinal nerve ligation (SNL)** , and **spared nerve injury (SNI)** models.
    - Paclitaxel‑ and oxaliplatin‑induced mechanical allodynia (chemotherapy‑induced peripheral neuropathy, CIPN).
    - Streptozotocin‑induced diabetic neuropathic pain.
    - Spinal cord injury‑associated neuropathic pain.
- Mechanistic studies show that pioglitazone achieves these effects by restoring spinal PPARγ levels, activating the **Nrf2/HO‑1 antioxidant pathway**, suppressing NF‑κB‑driven inflammation, and shifting microglia from an M1 to an M2 phenotype. The analgesic and anti‑inflammatory effects are reversed by the PPARγ antagonist **GW9662**, confirming target specificity.
- **Rosiglitazone**: Similarly attenuates neuropathic pain in multiple models through PPARγ‑dependent mechanisms, though it has a more pronounced effect on peripheral glucose metabolism.
- **Troglitazone**: Early studies demonstrated analgesic efficacy, but its use is precluded by hepatotoxicity.

### 2. Natural and Dietary PPARγ Agonists

- **Cannabidiol (CBD)** : One of the most clinically significant findings is that the non‑psychoactive phytocannabinoid cannabidiol produces **spinal antinociception through the activation of PPARγ** (and 5‑HT<sub>1A</sub> receptors).
    - Systemic or intrathecal CBD reduces mechanical allodynia and thermal hyperalgesia in CCI, SNI, and paclitaxel‑induced neuropathy.
    - The spinal antiallodynic effect of CBD in female mice is mediated by PPARγ and 5‑HT<sub>1A</sub> receptors, as it is reversed by GW9662 and the 5‑HT<sub>1A</sub> antagonist WAY‑100635.
    - CBD’s PPARγ‑dependent effects extend to supraspinal regions, where it reduces pain‑related anxiety, depressive‑like behaviours, and cognitive impairments in neuropathic pain models.
- **15d‑PGJ₂**: The endogenous PPARγ agonist 15‑deoxy‑Δ¹²,¹⁴‑prostaglandin J₂, when administered intrathecally, reduces spinal neuroinflammation and mechanical allodynia in CCI models. Its short half‑life limits systemic utility.
- **Curcumin** and **resveratrol**: Both have been reported to activate PPARγ, among other targets, and contribute to their analgesic and anti‑inflammatory effects in neuropathic pain.

### 3. Genetic Approaches

- **PPARγ overexpression**: Viral vector‑mediated overexpression of PPARγ in the spinal cord or DRG protects against the development of mechanical allodynia and reduces neuroinflammatory gene expression after nerve injury.
- **PPARγ conditional knockout**: Mice with neuronal or glial‑specific deletion of PPARγ show exaggerated neuropathic pain and enhanced spinal inflammation, confirming the protective, tonic anti‑nociceptive role of endogenous PPARγ.
- **GW9662 antagonism**: The PPARγ‑selective antagonist GW9662 blocks the analgesic effects of pioglitazone, rosiglitazone, and cannabidiol, and when administered alone, can worsen pain behaviours, indicating that basal PPARγ activity is restraining pain.

### 4. Non‑Pharmacological Upregulation

- **Electroacupuncture**: Alleviates neuropathic pain in part by upregulating spinal PPARγ expression, promoting M2 microglial polarisation, and reducing neuroinflammation.

## 🩺 Clinical Implications and Therapeutic Opportunities

- **Drug repurposing with thiazolidinediones**: Pioglitazone and rosiglitazone are FDA‑approved for type 2 diabetes and have well‑characterised safety profiles. Small, retrospective clinical studies and case reports have suggested that diabetic patients on TZDs experience a lower incidence of neuropathic pain or report less severe pain. A small pilot trial of pioglitazone in patients with painful diabetic neuropathy showed a trend toward pain reduction, but larger, placebo‑controlled trials are lacking.
- **Cannabidiol (CBD) as a safe PPARγ activator**: CBD is already approved for specific epilepsy syndromes (Lennox‑Gastaut, Dravet) and is widely available as a nutraceutical. Its ability to activate PPARγ, combined with its actions at 5‑HT<sub>1A</sub>, TRPV1, and other targets, positions it as a multi‑mechanistic analgesic with a favourable safety profile. Clinical trials of CBD for neuropathic pain are ongoing, and its PPARγ‑mediated component may be especially relevant for pain with a prominent neuroinflammatory component.
- **Developing selective PPARγ modulators (SPPARγMs)** : A major limitation of classical TZDs is their full‑agonist profile, which drives the full array of PPARγ‑dependent effects, including those on adipogenesis, fluid retention, and bone resorption. **Selective PPARγ modulators (SPPARγMs)** are designed to differentially activate beneficial pathways (e.g., anti‑inflammatory, insulin‑sensitising) while avoiding the transcriptional programs responsible for adverse effects. Several SPPARγMs are in preclinical development for metabolic and inflammatory diseases and could be explored for neuropathic pain.
- **Biomarker potential**: PPARγ expression in peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) or the ratio of PPARγ to NF‑κB activity could serve as a biomarker for the degree of systemic and central inflammation, helping to identify patients most likely to benefit from PPARγ‑targeted therapy.
- **Combination therapy**: Because PPARγ activation dampens multiple parallel pain pathways—NF‑κB, NLRP3, oxidative stress, and glial activation—it may synergise with analgesics that target complementary systems:
    - With **CBD** or **5‑HT<sub>1A</sub> agonists** to enhance spinal inhibition.
    - With **KCC2 enhancers** to restore GABAergic tone.
    - With low‑dose **naltrexone** (TLR4 antagonist) to simultaneously suppress innate immune activation.
    - With **opioids**, as PPARγ agonists can prevent opioid‑induced microglial activation and tolerance.

## ⚠️ A Note of Caution and Complexity

- **Side‑effect profile of classical TZDs**: The use of full‑agonist TZDs for a non‑life‑threatening chronic pain condition is limited by their well‑documented adverse effects: fluid retention and oedema (due to PPARγ in the collecting duct), increased risk of congestive heart failure, bone fractures (by promoting marrow adipogenesis over osteoblastogenesis), and weight gain. These side effects are dose‑dependent and would likely preclude the high, chronic dosing required for sustained central nervous system penetration.
- **Blood‑brain barrier penetration**: Pioglitazone and rosiglitazone have limited CNS penetration, which raises the question of whether their analgesic effects are mediated predominantly by peripheral PPARγ activation (on immune cells, DRG) or whether sufficient spinal concentrations are achieved at clinically used doses. Higher‑dose intrathecal delivery or CNS‑penetrant SPPARγMs may be required for maximal efficacy in centrally driven pain.
- **PPARγ and neuropathic pain heterogeneity**: PPARγ expression is not uniformly suppressed in all pain conditions. The degree of PPARγ downregulation may vary depending on the aetiology (traumatic vs. metabolic vs. inflammatory), and patients with a more profound loss of PPARγ may be more responsive to its pharmacological restoration.
- **Interaction with the endocannabinoid system**: Some PPARγ agonists, including pioglitazone, can indirectly increase endocannabinoid tone by upregulating the expression of the endocannabinoid biosynthetic enzyme NAPE‑PLD. This introduces CB1/CB2‑mediated effects that may contribute to both analgesia and side effects, complicating the attribution of outcomes to PPARγ alone.
- **Sex‑specific effects**: In preclinical studies, spinal PPARγ‑mediated antinociception by CBD was specifically demonstrated in **female mice**, while male mice may engage different mechanisms. The role of sex hormones, particularly oestrogen which can crosstalk with PPARγ, in modulating PPARγ expression and function in pain states is an understudied area that may have clinical relevance.
- **Temporal dynamics**: PPARγ activation is most effective when initiated early in the course of neuropathic pain, consistent with its role in preventing the establishment of chronic neuroinflammatory loops. Its efficacy in reversing well‑established, long‑standing neuropathic pain in humans remains to be fully demonstrated.
- **Interactions with the wider pain network**:
    - **NF‑κB and NLRP3**: PPARγ directly transrepresses NF‑κB, shutting down the priming signal for NLRP3 inflammasome activation and reducing IL‑1β maturation. This places PPARγ upstream of the NLRP3–IL‑1β cascade.
    - **TNF‑α and IL‑1β**: PPARγ activation blocks the expression of these cytokines, interrupting the auto‑amplification loops that sustain neuroinflammation. In turn, the cytokine‑rich environment of injured nerve can downregulate PPARγ, creating a reciprocal relationship.
    - **BDNF–TrkB–KCC2**: By reducing microglial activation and the release of BDNF, PPARγ indirectly protects KCC2 from downregulation, preserving GABAergic/glycinergic inhibition.
    - **TLR4**: PPARγ activation can suppress TLR4 expression and signalling, interfering with the ability of DAMPs (HMGB1, S100A8/A9) to initiate innate immune responses.
    - **JAK/STAT3**: PPARγ can directly bind to and inhibit STAT3, providing a direct intersection with the IL‑6/JAK/STAT3 pathway that drives reactive gliosis.
    - **Wnt/β‑catenin**: PPARγ promotes β‑catenin degradation via a proteasome‑dependent mechanism, potentially countering the pathological activation of Wnt signalling that contributes to NMDA receptor potentiation and pain.
    - **Nrf2/HO‑1**: PPARγ is a major upstream activator of Nrf2, and many of its antioxidant and mitochondrial protective effects are mediated through this pathway. This links PPARγ to the resolution of oxidative stress and ferroptosis.
    - **CB2 receptor**: PPARγ and CB2 are functionally coupled; some of the anti‑inflammatory effects of CB2 agonists may be mediated through the secondary activation of PPARγ, and PPARγ agonists can potentiate CB2‑mediated analgesia.
