# Involvement of Wnt Signaling in Neuropathic Pain

Wnt (Wingless‑related integration site) signaling constitutes an evolutionarily conserved family of secreted glycoproteins that orchestrate embryonic development, stem cell maintenance, and synaptic plasticity. In the adult nervous system, Wnt pathways are largely quiescent but are rapidly reactivated after peripheral nerve injury, where they act as potent drivers of neuroinflammation, central sensitization, and maladaptive plasticity. Multiple Wnt ligands (Wnt3a, Wnt5a, Wnt7b, Wnt10a, etc.) are upregulated in dorsal root ganglion (DRG) neurons and the spinal dorsal horn, engaging canonical (β‑catenin‑dependent) and non‑canonical (Ca²⁺‑ and planar cell polarity‑dependent) pathways. The net effect is an amplification of glutamatergic transmission via NMDA receptor phosphorylation, activation of pro‑inflammatory cytokine cascades, and disruption of inhibitory tone. Pharmacological blockade of Wnt secretion, ligand antagonism, or inhibition of downstream effectors consistently attenuates mechanical allodynia, thermal hyperalgesia, and spinal neuroinflammation in diverse preclinical models, positioning Wnt signaling as an attractive, if complex, therapeutic target for neuropathic pain.

## 🧬 The Wnt Signaling Network: Ligands, Receptors, and Pathways

Wnts comprise 19 mammalian glycoproteins that signal through multiple receptor families and three major intracellular cascades.

*   **Ligands**: Divided into two functional classes:
    *   **Canonical Wnts** (e.g., Wnt3a, Wnt7b, Wnt10a): Activate β‑catenin‑dependent transcription. Upregulated in DRG neurons and spinal cord after nerve injury.
    *   **Non‑canonical Wnts** (e.g., Wnt5a, Wnt5b, Wnt11): Primarily engage Ca²⁺ and planar cell polarity (PCP) pathways; also strongly pronociceptive.
*   **Receptors and co‑receptors**:
    *   **Frizzled (Fzd)** : Seven‑pass transmembrane receptors; the primary Wnt receptors. Fzd1, Fzd3, Fzd7, and Fzd9 are upregulated in spinal dorsal horn after injury.
    *   **Ryk and ROR2**: Receptor tyrosine kinases that mediate non‑canonical Wnt signaling; Ryk binds Wnt5a and is a key transducer in pain.
    *   **LRP5/6**: Co‑receptors required for canonical β‑catenin signaling.
*   **Intracellular pathways**:
    *   **Canonical (β‑catenin‑dependent)** : In the absence of Wnt, β‑catenin is phosphorylated by a destruction complex (GSK‑3β, APC, Axin) and degraded. Wnt binding to Fzd and LRP5/6 inhibits the destruction complex, allowing β‑catenin accumulation, nuclear translocation, and TCF/LEF‑dependent transcription. In neuropathic pain, this pathway drives the expression of pronociceptive genes, including pro‑inflammatory cytokines (TNF‑α, IL‑1β, IL‑18), matrix metalloproteinases (MMP‑9), and the calcium channel subunit α₂δ‑1.
    *   **Wnt/Ca²⁺ pathway**: Wnt5a binding to Fzd or Ryk activates phospholipase C (PLC), generating IP₃ and diacylglycerol, which release intracellular Ca²⁺ and activate PKC and CaMKII. This pathway is critical for NMDA receptor potentiation and rapid synaptic sensitization.
    *   **Wnt/PCP pathway**: Engages RhoA and JNK, regulating cytoskeletal dynamics and gene transcription.

## 🔬 Mechanisms of Wnt Dysregulation in Neuropathic Pain

### 1. Upregulation of Wnt Ligands and Receptors After Nerve Injury

Following chronic constriction injury (CCI), spared nerve injury (SNI), spinal nerve ligation (SNL), or chemotherapy‑induced neuropathy, a broad set of Wnt ligands and receptors are transcriptionally induced in the pain pathway:

*   **In DRG neurons**: Wnt3a, Wnt5a, Wnt5b, Wnt7b, Fzd1, Fzd3, and Ryk are all upregulated. This injury‑induced expression is driven by transcription factors such as ATF3, NF‑κB, and STAT3.
*   **In spinal dorsal horn**: Astrocytes and microglia upregulate Wnt3a, Wnt5a, and Fzd receptors, creating a ligand‑rich microenvironment that sustains central sensitization.
*   **Time course**: Wnt upregulation peaks 7–14 days post‑injury and can persist for weeks, paralleling the maintenance of chronic pain.

### 2. Canonical β‑Catenin Pathway: Driving Pro‑Inflammatory Gene Expression

Activation of canonical Wnt/β‑catenin signaling in spinal glia and neurons has multiple pronociceptive consequences:

*   **Cytokine induction**: β‑catenin/TCF transcription directly upregulates **IL‑18** and **TNF‑α** in spinal microglia and astrocytes. IL‑18, in turn, activates its receptor on neurons and further glial cells, promoting a self‑sustaining inflammatory loop.
*   **MMP‑9 expression**: β‑catenin drives *Mmp9* transcription. MMP‑9 degrades perineuronal nets (PNNs) and converts pro‑BDNF to mature BDNF, simultaneously dismantling structural inhibition and promoting TrkB‑mediated KCC2 downregulation.
*   **α₂δ‑1 calcium channel subunit**: The *Cacna2d1* gene is a β‑catenin target. Its upregulation enhances calcium currents at primary afferent synapses, facilitating neurotransmitter release and contributing to central sensitization and gabapentin‑sensitive allodynia.
*   **Glial activation**: Canonical Wnt signaling promotes the M1 pro‑inflammatory phenotype in microglia and astrogliosis, amplifying the neuroimmune response.

### 3. Non‑Canonical Wnt/Ca²⁺ Pathway: Rapid Sensitization and NMDA Receptor Potentiation

The Wnt/Ca²⁺ pathway, primarily activated by Wnt5a through Ryk or Fzd receptors, mediates acute and sustained central sensitization:

*   **Ca²⁺ mobilization**: PLC‑generated IP₃ releases Ca²⁺ from intracellular stores, elevating cytosolic calcium in spinal dorsal horn neurons.
*   **NMDA receptor phosphorylation**: Elevated Ca²⁺ activates PKC and Src family kinases, which directly phosphorylate the GluN2B subunit of NMDA receptors. This enhances NMDA receptor conductance, prolongs channel open time, and promotes the trafficking of GluN2B‑containing receptors to the synapse. The result is profound NMDA receptor‑dependent central sensitization.
*   **CREB phosphorylation**: Ca²⁺‑activated CaMKII and ERK pathways lead to CREB phosphorylation and the transcription of pain‑related genes, consolidating long‑term potentiation (LTP) at nociceptive synapses.
*   **Disinhibition**: Non‑canonical Wnt signaling can also suppress GABAergic transmission by downregulating GAD65 expression, further contributing to the loss of inhibitory tone.

### 4. Crosstalk with Neuroinflammation and Other Signaling Systems

Wnt signaling is tightly integrated into the broader neuroimmune network:

*   **TNF‑α positive feedback**: Wnt/β‑catenin induces TNF‑α expression; TNF‑α, via TNFR1 and NF‑κB, can in turn upregulate Wnt ligands and receptors, creating a feed‑forward loop.
*   **TLR4 priming**: DAMPs (e.g., HMGB1) that activate TLR4 can synergize with Wnt to amplify β‑catenin signaling through a glycogen synthase kinase‑3β (GSK‑3β)‑dependent mechanism.
*   **NLRP3 inflammasome**: β‑catenin‑dependent IL‑18 transcription directly contributes to inflammasome‑mediated inflammation.
*   **RhoA/ROCK**: The Wnt/PCP pathway activates RhoA/ROCK, linking Wnt to cytoskeletal remodeling and MMP‑9 expression.

### 5. Supraspinal Wnt and Pain Comorbidities

Wnt dysregulation is not restricted to the spinal cord. In the anterior cingulate cortex (ACC) and hippocampus, Wnt signaling is pathologically activated in chronic pain models:

*   In the ACC, Wnt5a/Ryk signaling enhances NMDA receptor function and contributes to pain‑related anxiety and depressive‑like behaviors.
*   Hippocampal Wnt/β‑catenin dysregulation impairs neurogenesis and long‑term potentiation, providing a substrate for the cognitive deficits associated with chronic pain.

## 🔬 Preclinical Evidence of Modulation

Pharmacological inhibition of Wnt secretion, ligand antagonism, or blockade of downstream effectors has been extensively validated in animal models of neuropathic pain.

### 1. Inhibitors of Wnt Secretion (Porcupine Inhibitors)

The enzyme porcupine is required for the palmitoylation and secretion of all Wnt ligands. Porcupine inhibitors therefore act as pan‑Wnt blockers.

*   **IWP‑2**: Intrathecal injection of IWP‑2 in CCI rats effectively reverses mechanical allodynia and thermal hyperalgesia. It reduces the expression of β‑catenin, IL‑18, TNF‑α, and MMP‑9 in the spinal dorsal horn, and attenuates C‑fiber‑evoked long‑term potentiation (LTP) at spinal synapses. Notably, IWP‑2 does not affect normal acute pain thresholds or locomotor activity, indicating a selective effect on pathological pain plasticity.
*   **LGK‑974 (WNT974)** : An oral porcupine inhibitor that has shown efficacy in reducing β‑catenin activation and pain behaviors in rodent neuropathic pain models. Its clinical development has primarily been for cancer, but it represents a candidate for repurposing.

### 2. Ligand‑Specific Antagonists

*   **Secreted Frizzled‑Related Proteins (sFRPs)** : Endogenous Wnt scavengers that bind ligands and prevent receptor engagement. Intrathecal delivery of sFRP‑1 or sFRP‑3 recombinant protein reduces mechanical allodynia, suppresses spinal β‑catenin signaling, and decreases pro‑inflammatory cytokine levels. However, their stability and CNS delivery are challenges.
*   **Anti‑Wnt5a antibodies**: Blocking Wnt5a specifically attenuates non‑canonical Ca²⁺ signaling, reduces NMDA receptor phosphorylation, and alleviates mechanical hypersensitivity.
*   **Ryk‑Fc fusion proteins**: Soluble decoy receptors that sequester Wnt5a and inhibit non‑canonical Wnt/Ryk signaling. Intrathecal Ryk‑Fc reduces pain and synaptic sensitization in rodent models.

### 3. Small‑Molecule Inhibitors of Downstream Signaling

*   **XAV939**: A tankyrase inhibitor that stabilizes the β‑catenin destruction complex, promoting β‑catenin degradation. Intrathecal XAV939 suppresses β‑catenin/TCF activity, reduces IL‑18 and TNF‑α expression, and attenuates neuropathic pain in CCI and diabetic neuropathy models.
*   **ICG‑001**: A specific inhibitor of β‑catenin/TCF‑dependent transcription. It blocks the expression of Wnt target genes (IL‑18, MMP‑9) and alleviates mechanical allodynia.
*   **Pyrvinium**: An anthelmintic drug that also acts as a Wnt/β‑catenin inhibitor. Repurposing studies show that pyrvinium reduces pain behaviors and spinal inflammation in neuropathic pain models.
*   **Niclosamide**: Another anthelmintic with Wnt/β‑catenin inhibitory activity; under preclinical investigation for pain.

### 4. Natural Compounds and Nutritional Interventions

*   **Resveratrol** and **curcumin**: Suppress Wnt/β‑catenin signaling by inhibiting NF‑κB and promoting GSK‑3β activity, reducing neuroinflammation and pain.
*   **Omega‑3 fatty acids**: Modulate Wnt receptor expression and have shown modest analgesic effects in some pain models.
*   **Electroacupuncture**: Alleviates neuropathic pain partly by inhibiting spinal Wnt3a/β‑catenin signaling and reducing IL‑18 production.

### 5. Genetic Approaches

*   **β‑catenin conditional knockout**: Deletion of β‑catenin in sensory neurons or spinal astrocytes reduces mechanical allodynia, cytokine expression, and MMP‑9 levels after nerve injury.
*   **Wnt5a knockdown**: siRNA‑mediated silencing of Wnt5a in the spinal cord reduces non‑canonical Wnt signaling, blocks NMDA receptor phosphorylation, and attenuates pain.
*   **Ryk knockout**: Mice lacking Ryk show reduced pain behaviors after nerve injury, confirming the importance of non‑canonical Wnt/Ryk signaling.

## 🩺 Clinical Implications and Therapeutic Opportunities

*   **Porcupine inhibitors as a new class of analgesics**: IWP‑2 and LGK‑974 (WNT974) are the most advanced pan‑Wnt blockers. While primarily developed for oncology, their preclinical analgesic efficacy supports exploration in chronic pain. Intrathecal delivery could maximize spinal concentrations and limit systemic toxicity.
*   **Wnt5a/Ryk axis as a specific target**: The non‑canonical Wnt/Ca²⁺ pathway is particularly attractive because it directly drives NMDA receptor sensitization without the same developmental requirements as canonical β‑catenin signaling. Monoclonal antibodies against Wnt5a or Ryk‑Fc decoy receptors represent a more selective approach than porcupine inhibition.
*   **Drug repurposing with anthelmintics**: Pyrvinium and niclosamide are inexpensive, orally bioavailable drugs with long safety histories. Their analgesic potential warrants controlled clinical trials, particularly for painful diabetic neuropathy or chemotherapy‑induced pain where spinal Wnt activation is documented.
*   **Biomarker potential**: Secreted Wnt ligands or their antagonists (sFRPs, Dickkopf‑1) may be detectable in cerebrospinal fluid and could serve as biomarkers of central Wnt pathway activation, enabling patient stratification.
*   **Combination therapies**: Because Wnt signaling converges on NMDA receptor potentiation and inflammatory cytokine production, combining Wnt inhibitors with:
    *   **Low‑dose NMDA receptor antagonists** (e.g., ketamine, memantine)
    *   **TNF‑α inhibitors**
    *   **KCC2 enhancers** (to counteract BDNF‑mediated disinhibition)
    could produce synergistic analgesia.

## ⚠️ A Note of Caution and Complexity

*   **Essential roles of Wnt in adult tissue homeostasis**: Wnt signaling is critical for stem cell maintenance, bone remodeling, haematopoiesis, and synaptic plasticity. Global, prolonged inhibition with porcupine inhibitors carries significant risks, including impaired wound healing, bone loss, gastrointestinal toxicity, and cognitive deficits. Intrathecal delivery or time‑limited systemic administration may be required to achieve a therapeutic window.
*   **Wnt pathway dichotomy: protective vs. pathogenic**: Not all Wnt signaling is pronociceptive. In some contexts, Wnt3a can be neuroprotective, promoting axonal regeneration and synaptic repair. Broad‑spectrum Wnt blockade could interfere with endogenous repair mechanisms. Careful selection of ligands (e.g., targeting Wnt5a/Ryk) may provide a better safety profile.
*   **Redundancy among Wnt ligands and Frizzled receptors**: Nineteen Wnt ligands and ten Frizzled receptors provide enormous combinatorial complexity. Blocking a single ligand or receptor may be insufficient if others compensate. Porcupine inhibitors avoid this redundancy but are less selective. The ideal approach may be to target the specific Fzd‑ligand pair that predominates in the pain state.
*   **Developmental re‑activation**: The reactivation of Wnt signaling in neuropathic pain recapitulates developmental programs. This raises the concern that prolonged Wnt inhibition could reactivate latent developmental pathways inappropriately or interfere with adult neurogenesis.
*   **Sex differences**: Preclinical pain studies have largely been performed in male animals; sex‑specific differences in Wnt pathway activation and response to inhibitors are largely unexplored but likely exist given the modulatory effects of sex hormones on Wnt signaling.
*   **Integration with other pain pathways**:
    *   **BDNF–TrkB–KCC2**: Wnt‑induced MMP‑9 converts pro‑BDNF to mature BDNF, linking Wnt activation to the canonical disinhibition cascade. Additionally, non‑canonical Wnt/Ca²⁺ signaling can suppress GAD65 expression, directly impairing GABA synthesis.
    *   **NMDA receptors**: Non‑canonical Wnt/Ca²⁺ signaling directly phosphorylates GluN2B, a core mechanism of central sensitization. This positions Wnt immediately upstream of NMDAR‑dependent LTP.
    *   **TNF‑α, IL‑1β, NLRP3**: Wnt/β‑catenin drives the transcription of IL‑18 and TNF‑α, which then activate TNFR and NLRP3 inflammasome pathways, creating a pro‑inflammatory feed‑forward loop.
    *   **RhoA/ROCK**: The Wnt/PCP pathway activates RhoA/ROCK, linking Wnt to cytoskeletal changes and MMP‑9 release.
    *   **HDACs**: Epigenetic silencing of Wnt antagonists (e.g., DKK1, sFRPs) by HDACs can lead to unopposed Wnt activity, and HDAC inhibitors may partly exert analgesia by restoring Wnt pathway balance.
    *   **Cav3.2 and Nav channels**: Wnt‑induced PKC activation can phosphorylate Cav3.2 and Nav1.7/Nav1.8, enhancing their activity and providing a direct link between Wnt signaling and ion channel‑mediated hyperexcitability.
