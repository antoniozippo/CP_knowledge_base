# A Quadruple-Pronged Therapeutic Strategy to Reverse Established Neuropathic Pain: Targeting Convergent Mechanisms of Spinal Disinhibition

---

## 1. BACKGROUND AND SIGNIFICANCE

### 1.1 The Clinical Burden of Established Chronic Pain

Chronic neuropathic pain — arising from a lesion or disease of the somatosensory nervous system — afflicts 7–10% of the global population (>350 million individuals) and results from traumatic nerve injury, diabetic neuropathy, chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy (CIPN), post-herpetic neuralgia, spinal cord injury, and complex regional pain syndrome (CRPS) (van Hecke et al., 2014; Colloca et al., 2017). Its cardinal feature, mechanical allodynia (pain from innocuous touch), renders everyday activities such as dressing, walking, or even the pressure of bed sheets excruciating. Patients also experience ongoing spontaneous pain, thermal hyperalgesia, and cold allodynia, with significant comorbidities of anxiety, depression, and sleep disruption that compound disability and dramatically reduce quality of life.

Current pharmacotherapy for neuropathic pain is strikingly inadequate. First-line treatments — gabapentinoids (gabapentin, pregabalin), serotonin-noradrenaline reuptake inhibitors (duloxetine, venlafaxine), and tricyclic antidepressants — provide clinically meaningful relief (≥50% pain reduction) in only one in three to four patients, with a number needed to treat (NNT) of approximately 4–7 (Finnerup et al., 2015). Opioid analgesics, still widely prescribed despite limited evidence of long-term efficacy in neuropathic pain, carry well-characterized risks of tolerance, dependence, respiratory depression, and diversion, contributing substantially to the ongoing opioid public health crisis. Critically, all existing pharmacotherapies are purely symptomatic: none targets or reverses the underlying disease mechanisms sustaining chronic pain.

A fundamental and underappreciated reason for treatment failure is that the vast majority of preclinical pain research has focused on preventing the *development* of pain hypersensitivity rather than reversing it once established. The molecular and circuit-level mechanisms that initiate pain in the hours following nerve injury — early inflammatory cascades, acute transcriptional responses, and initial synaptic plasticity — are partly distinct from those that maintain pain weeks, months, or years later. Chronic pain involves stable, self-reinforcing pathological states: sustained microglial activation, irreversible or slowly reversible loss of inhibitory interneurons, epigenetic reprogramming of sensory neurons, and maladaptive structural remodeling of spinal and supraspinal circuits. This proposal directly targets the convergent mechanisms sustaining spinal disinhibition in the established, treatment-resistant phase, with all interventions tested at chronic timepoints (≥21 days post-injury) rather than at the initiation of pain.

### 1.2 The Spinal Disinhibition Paradigm

In the superficial spinal dorsal horn (laminae I–III), fast-spiking parvalbumin-expressing (PV+) GABAergic interneurons provide feedforward inhibition gating Aβ mechanoreceptive tactile input, preventing innocuous signals from activating lamina I nociceptive projection neurons (Petitjean et al., 2015; Boyle et al., 2019). After peripheral nerve injury, these PV+ interneurons are compromised through five convergent mechanisms, producing spinal disinhibition in which tactile input pathologically drives nociceptive pathways:

**Mechanism 1 — PNN Degradation via MMP-9.** Perineuronal nets (PNNs) — ECM structures of CSPGs, hyaluronan, HAPLN1, and tenascin-R — enwrap PV+ interneurons, providing a polyanionic sink essential for high-frequency firing. Post-injury, activated microglia and astrocytes upregulate MMP-9 (itself induced by TNF-α, IL-1β, and HIF1α), which cleaves aggrecan and simultaneously converts proBDNF to mature BDNF. Enzymatic PNN removal by chondroitinase ABC injection in naïve animals is sufficient to induce mechanical allodynia (Dityatev et al., 2010).

**Mechanism 2 — BDNF-TrkB → KCC2 Downregulation.** Microglial BDNF (released via ATP/P2X4 signaling) and primary afferent-derived BDNF bind TrkB.FL, triggering PKC-mediated KCC2 phosphorylation, calpain-mediated proteolytic cleavage, and transcriptional repression (Coull et al., 2003, 2005; Rivera et al., 2004). KCC2 normally maintains low intracellular Cl⁻ (5–10 mM), enabling GABA/glycine hyperpolarization. KCC2 downregulation collapses the Cl⁻ gradient, converting inhibition into excitation — a phenomenon termed "ionic plasticity" (Kahle et al., 2008; Doyon et al., 2016).

**Mechanism 3 — CP-AMPAR-Mediated Ca²⁺ Excitotoxicity.** PV+ interneurons are enriched in GluA2-lacking calcium-permeable AMPA receptors. Sustained post-injury glutamatergic drive produces massive Ca²⁺ influx, overwhelming mitochondrial buffering, opening the mPTP, collapsing ATP production, and generating ROS. This triggers caspase-dependent apoptosis and calpain-mediated necroptosis (Inquimbert et al., 2018). PV buffering capacity is itself depleted, and autophagy is impaired through calpain-mediated cleavage of ATG5/Beclin-1 and mTORC1 hyperactivation.

**Mechanism 4 — Microglial TNF-α/IL-1β Release.** Activated microglia release TNF-α, a master pro-inflammatory cytokine that induces MMP-9, stimulates microglial BDNF release, upregulates NgR1 → RhoA/ROCK pathway, and stabilizes HIF1α via NF-κB-mediated PHD inhibition, driving VEGFA, ANGPT2, and glycolytic enzyme expression. Critically, TNF-α signals through two receptors: TNFR1 (pro-inflammatory, pro-nociceptive) and TNFR2 (pro-resolution, neuroprotective, remyelination-promoting) (Fischer et al., 2015).

**Mechanism 5 — HIF1α Transcriptional Programs.** HIF1α undergoes normoxic stabilization post-injury through ROS-mediated and TNF-α/NF-κB-dependent PHD inhibition. Once stabilized, it dimerizes with HIF1β and drives expression of VEGFA, ANGPT2, MMP-9, MMP-2, BDNF, iNOS, COX-2, and glycolytic enzymes (LDHA, PDK1, HK2), promoting microglial M1-like glycolytic polarization, pathological angiogenesis, BSCB disruption via tight junction protein (occludin, claudin-5) downregulation, and PNN degradation — directly coupling metabolic stress, vascular dysfunction, ECM remodeling, and neuronal disinhibition (Cramer et al., 2003; Palazon et al., 2014).

The NgR1/RhoA-ROCK axis (activated by myelin debris: Nogo-A, MAG, OMgp) further amplifies this cascade by stabilizing pronociceptive ion channels (TRPV1, Nav1.7, Nav1.8) through LIMK/cofilin-mediated actin polymerization, driving MMP-9 expression, and mediating LPA-induced demyelination of primary afferents (Tatsumi et al., 2015; Xie et al., 2017).

**Circuit Consequence.** These five mechanisms converge on PV+ interneuron loss, removing feedforward inhibition from spinal SST+ excitatory interneurons in lamina II. Disinhibited and intrinsically hyperexcitable SST+ neurons relay Aβ tactile input to lamina I projection neurons — the final common output pathway for mechanical allodynia (Duan et al., 2014; Peirs et al., 2015).

### 1.3 Rationale for a Multi-Target Approach

The extraordinary mechanistic complexity of spinal disinhibition explains why monotherapies have consistently failed in chronic pain. Four specific barriers necessitate a coordinated multi-target strategy:

**Redundancy.** The five convergent mechanisms operate through independent molecular cascades. Blocking KCC2 downregulation restores chloride homeostasis but does not prevent ongoing PNN degradation, excitotoxic Ca²⁺ influx, or microglial cytokine release. MMP-9 inhibitors preserve PNN structure but do not reverse established KCC2 loss. TrkB blockade with ANA-12 does not prevent CP-AMPAR-mediated excitotoxicity. Each single-target intervention leaves the other four mechanisms intact and fully capable of maintaining spinal disinhibition.

**Compensation.** Chronic neuropathic pain is a dynamically regulated state, not a static lesion. When one pain-maintenance pathway is blocked, surviving pathways undergo compensatory upregulation. ANA-12 produces only transient analgesia as alternative signaling mechanisms compensate (Deng et al., 2020). Genetic deletion of MMP-9 reduces but does not eliminate allodynia, because MMP-2 and ADAMTS family proteases partially substitute. The nociceptive system is evolutionarily designed to preserve protective function, and therapeutic strategies must account for this biological resilience.

**Biphasic Roles.** Nearly every target has dual functions complicating therapeutic modulation. BDNF drives spinal pain through TrkB.FL-mediated KCC2 downregulation but also supports neurogenesis, synaptic plasticity, and cognition — global TrkB blockade risks memory impairment and mood disturbance. MMP-9 degrades PNNs but is required for synaptic remodeling and wound healing — chronic MMP inhibition causes musculoskeletal toxicity in clinical trials. HIF1α drives the pro-inflammatory transcriptional program but is essential for erythropoiesis, wound healing, and ischemic adaptation — systemic inhibition risks anemia. RhoA/ROCK mediates pain through microglial activation and ion channel trafficking but is required for axonal regeneration — complete blockade impairs recovery. VEGFA promotes neuroma formation and vascular permeability but is necessary for nerve regeneration and Schwann cell survival. These biphasic roles demand cell-type-specific, temporally restricted, or downstream-targeted interventions.

**Cell-Type Specificity.** The disinhibition pathways operate in distinct but interacting cellular compartments: PV+ interneurons (excitotoxicity, KCC2-dependent Cl⁻ homeostasis), SST+ excitatory interneurons (disinhibition-driven hyperexcitability), microglia (TNF-α/IL-1β/BDNF release, p38 MAPK), astrocytes (reactive gliosis, MMP-9 secretion), oligodendrocytes (Nogo-A expression, myelin debris), endothelial cells (BSCB tight junction regulation), and DRG neurons (BDNF/MMP-9/IGF2 release, ion channel trafficking). Systemic interventions modulating these pathways globally produce off-target effects across multiple organ systems. The solution requires cell-type-restricted delivery (AAV with S5E2/mDlx enhancers), localized administration (intrathecal), or targeting of downstream nodes selectively engaged in the pathological state.

A coordinated four-pronged strategy targeting four distinct mechanistic levels — ionic (KCC2), glial/cytoskeletal (ROCK), circuit (SST), and immune (TNFR1) — can overcome these barriers by providing convergent pressure on the spinal disinhibition network while minimizing single-agent doses, reducing off-target toxicity, and exploiting synergistic interactions between mechanistically complementary interventions.

### 1.4 The Four Therapeutic Pillars

**Pillar 1 — KCC2 Enhancement:** Restoring the ionic basis of inhibition. CLP257 derivatives, AAV-S5E2-KCC2 gene therapy, and novel KCC2 stabilizers targeting calpain cleavage and membrane trafficking.

**Pillar 2 — ROCK Inhibition:** Suppressing microglial activation and ion channel remodeling. Clinically approved fasudil and peripherally restricted AS1892802 targeting the RhoA/ROCK convergence hub downstream of NgR1, P2Y12/P2Y13, and LPA receptors.

**Pillar 3 — SST+ Interneuron Silencing:** Direct circuit-level blockade of the mechanical allodynia output pathway. Chemogenetic (hM4Di), optogenetic (eNpHR3.0), pharmacological (SSTR2a antagonists), and ablation (taCasp3) approaches.

**Pillar 4 — TNFR1-Selective TNF-α Blockade:** Reducing neuroinflammation while preserving TNFR2-mediated resolution. XPro1595, a dominant-negative soluble TNF variant selectively neutralizing solTNF/TNFR1.

## 2. RESEARCH METHODOLOGY

### 2.1 Aim 1: KCC2 Enhancement to Restore GABAergic/Glycinergic Inhibition (Years 1–3)

**Rationale.** KCC2 (SLC12A5) is the neuron-specific K⁺-Cl⁻ cotransporter that maintains low intracellular Cl⁻ (~5–10 mM), enabling GABA and glycine to produce Cl⁻ influx and membrane hyperpolarization upon GABA_A or glycine receptor activation. After peripheral nerve injury, BDNF-TrkB.FL signaling triggers KCC2 downregulation through three coordinated mechanisms: (i) PKC-mediated phosphorylation at Ser940, promoting KCC2 internalization from the plasma membrane; (ii) calpain-mediated proteolytic cleavage of the KCC2 C-terminal domain; and (iii) transcriptional repression via CREB-dependent pathways. Because this downstream convergence integrates BDNF, TNF-α, PNN loss, and HIF1α inputs, restoring KCC2 function can reverse disinhibition regardless of which upstream trigger initiated its loss — making KCC2 the single most convergent molecular target in the spinal disinhibition network.

**Approach 1A — CLP257 Derivative Screening.** CLP257 is a carbamazepine-derived positive allosteric modulator that enhances KCC2 Cl⁻ extrusion capacity without altering total protein levels (Gagnon et al., 2013). We will synthesize and screen a focused library of ~200 structural analogs using FLIPR membrane potential assays with the Cl⁻-sensitive dye MQAE in cultured spinal cord neurons from E14 mouse embryos. Compounds showing ≥2-fold enhancement over WT CLP257 will advance to secondary screening in acute spinal cord slices. For in vivo testing, lead compounds will be administered i.p. at 10–100 mg/kg b.i.d. in male and female C57BL/6J mice at chronic SNI timepoints (≥21 days post-injury), with behavioral testing at 1, 2, 4, 8, and 24 h post-dose. Compounds achieving ≥50% allodynia reversal without motor impairment (rotarod, open field) will be characterized for pharmacokinetics (plasma/CSF concentrations, brain penetration, t½) and tested in CCI and CIPN models for generalizability.

**Approach 1B — AAV-Mediated KCC2 Overexpression.** AAV9/PHP.eB vectors expressing KCC2-P2A-EGFP under the mDlx enhancer (broad GABAergic) or S5E2 enhancer (>90% PV+ specificity; Vormstein-Schneider et al., 2020) will be injected stereotaxically into L4–L6 spinal cord (2 × 10^10 vg total). Transduction specificity is validated by dual-label IHC (GFP/PV/SST/NeuN/GFAP/Iba-1); ≥80% specificity required. Behavioral testing at 7, 14, 21, 28, 42 days post-SNI.

**Approach 1C — HTS for KCC2 Stabilizers.** HEK293T cells expressing extracellular FLAG-KCC2 treated with BDNF (100 ng/mL) or ionomycin are screened in 384-well format (Z' > 0.5) against a ~100,000 compound library. Hits preserving surface KCC2 are counter-screened by Rb⁺ flux assay and advanced to in vivo testing.

**Outcome Measures:** von Frey, Hargreaves, acetone drop, CPP; gramicidin-perforated patch E_GABA; Clomeleon FRET imaging of [Cl⁻]ᵢ; KCC2 protein/mRNA/surface biotinylation; KCC2 IHC co-localization. Statistical design: n = 8–12/group, equal sexes; two-way repeated measures ANOVA with Bonferroni correction.

### 2.2 Aim 2: ROCK Inhibition to Suppress Neuroinflammation and Ion Channel Remodeling (Years 1–3)

**Rationale.** RhoA/ROCK is a convergence hub activated by Nogo-A/MAG/OMgp via NgR1/p75NTR/LINGO-1, microglial P2Y12/P2Y13 receptors, EphA1/CXCR4, and LPA/LPAR1. ROCK phosphorylates LIMK/cofilin (actin stabilization, ion channel trafficking), p38 MAPK (M1 polarization), and MARCKS (glial transmitter release). Fasudil is clinically approved in Asia for cerebral vasospasm.

**Approach 2A — Fasudil Dose-Response.** Fasudil (HA-1077) is an ATP-competitive ROCK inhibitor (IC50: ROCK1 = 0.33 µM, ROCK2 = 0.16 µM) clinically approved in Japan and China for cerebral vasospasm, with a well-characterized human safety profile and ongoing Phase II trials for ALS (ROCK-ALS) and Parkinson's disease (ROCK-PD). We will test fasudil (1, 3, 10, 30 mg/kg i.p., b.i.d.) in male and female C57BL/6J mice with SNI at chronic timepoints (≥21 days post-injury). Behavioral pain testing at 0, 1, 2, 4, 8, 24, and 48 h post-first-dose and after 7 and 14 days of continued b.i.d. dosing. Blood pressure monitored by tail-cuff plethysmography to quantify the hypotensive liability at each dose.

**Approach 2B — Peripherally Restricted AS1892802.** Oral AS1892802 (1–10 mg/kg q.d.) compared to fasudil (10 mg/kg b.i.d.) for analgesia, blood pressure, locomotion, and motor coordination. ROCK activity confirmed by phospho-MYPT1 Western blot in brain vs. spinal cord.

**Approach 2C — Cell-Type-Specific ROCK Deletion.** ROCK1^flox/flox;ROCK2^flox/flox mice crossed with Cx3cr1-CreER (microglia), GFAP-Cre (astrocytes), and Advillin-Cre (sensory neurons). Tamoxifen-induced deletion at 14 days post-SNI (chronic phase), with pain assessment for 28 days.

**Approach 2D — Combination with KCC2 Enhancers.** Fasudil + lead KCC2 enhancer at maximally effective individual doses; synergy assessed by two-way ANOVA.

**Outcome Measures:** von Frey, Hargreaves, acetone drop, CPP, rotarod; Iba-1 IHC + Sholl analysis; multiplex ELISA (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, CCL2, IL-10, TGF-β); Western blot (p-p38, p-MYPT1, p-cofilin); Cavα2δ-1 surface biotinylation; gelatin zymography (MMP-9); toluidine blue/EM (demyelination). n = 8–12/group, equal sexes.

### 2.3 Aim 3: SST+ Interneuron Silencing to Block Mechanical Allodynia Output (Years 2–4)

**Rationale.** Spinal SST+ excitatory interneurons in lamina II are the final common output pathway for mechanical allodynia. All upstream disinhibition mechanisms converge on their hyperexcitability. Chemogenetic/optogenetic silencing eliminates allodynia without affecting acute nociception, making this the most direct circuit-level intervention.

**Approach 3A — Chemogenetic Silencing.** SST-IRES-Cre mice receive bilateral AAV8-hSyn-DIO-hM4Di-mCherry (L4–L6, 2 × 10^9 vg/side). CNO (1 mg/kg i.p.) or vehicle administered at chronic SNI timepoints in randomized crossover design (72-h washout). Patch clamp confirmation of hM4Di-mediated hyperpolarization in acute slices (CNO 10 µM).

**Approach 3B — Optogenetic Validation.** AAV8-hSyn-DIO-eNpHR3.0-EYFP injected bilaterally; chronic fiber optic cannulae implanted over L4–L6 dorsal horn. 590 nm illumination (10 mW) during von Frey testing at chronic SNI timepoints. Prediction: allodynia reversed within seconds of light onset, returned within seconds of offset — causally establishing SST+ necessity at sensory-processing timescale.

**Approach 3C — SSTR2a Antagonists.** Screen CYN-154806, PRL-2903, BIM-23627, and novel compounds by intrathecal delivery (5 µL, 0.1–100 nmol) in SNI mice. Lead compounds optimized for selectivity vs. SSTR1/3/4/5, metabolic stability, and oral bioavailability.

**Approach 3D — Targeted Ablation.** AAV8-hSyn-DIO-taCasp3-TEVp in SST-IRES-Cre mice; extent of SST+ loss quantified by IHC. Naïve sensory testing, then SNI + behavioral follow-up (7–28 days). Motor coordination assessed.

**Outcome Measures:** von Frey, Hargreaves, tail flick, acetone drop, CPP, rotarod, open field; patch clamp from hM4Di+/eNpHR+ neurons; in vivo GCaMP6s Ca²⁺ imaging during von Frey; c-Fos IHC in lamina I; dose-response curves for SSTR2a antagonists. n = 8–14/group depending on approach; within-subject designs for optogenetic experiments.

### 2.4 Aim 4: TNFR1-Selective TNF-α Blockade to Suppress Neuroinflammation While Preserving Resolution (Years 2–4)

**Rationale.** TNF-α drives the neuroinflammatory cascade but signals through two receptors with opposing functions: TNFR1 (pro-inflammatory, pro-nociceptive) vs. TNFR2 (pro-resolution, neuroprotective, remyelination). Broad anti-TNF agents (infliximab, etanercept) block both, explaining inconsistent clinical efficacy and rare paradoxical neuropathies. XPro1595 (solTNF/TNFR1-selective dominant-negative variant, A145R/I97T substitutions) preserves TNFR2 signaling.

**Approach 4A — Head-to-Head Comparison.** Male and female C57BL/6J mice with established SNI (≥21 days) will be randomized to five groups (n = 12/group): (i) XPro1595 (10 mg/kg s.c., every 3 days), (ii) infliximab (chimeric anti-TNF mAb, 10 mg/kg i.p., every 3 days), (iii) etanercept (TNFR2-Fc, 10 mg/kg i.p., every 3 days), (iv) vehicle (saline s.c.), or (v) sham surgery + vehicle. Treatments continue for 14 days with behavioral testing on days 0, 3, 7, 10, 14. On day 14, half the mice (n = 6) are perfused for IHC/biochemistry; the remainder (n = 6) are monitored for 14 additional drug-free days to assess durability. The key comparison is XPro1595 vs. broad-spectrum agents for efficacy, pro-resolution markers (IL-10, TGF-β), and immunosuppression indices.

**Approach 4B — Intrathecal vs. Systemic Delivery.** Systemic XPro1595 (10 mg/kg s.c.) compared to intrathecal XPro1595 (1–10 µg in 5 µL via indwelling catheter, every 3 days). Pharmacokinetics: drug levels in plasma/CSF/spinal cord at 6/24/72 h. Systemic immune function: CBC, serum Ig, splenic lymphocyte flow cytometry (CD3/CD4/CD8/CD19/CD11b).

**Approach 4C — TNFR1⁻/⁻ and TNFR2⁻/⁻ Genetic Dissection.** TNFR1⁻/⁻ and TNFR2⁻/⁻ mice (Jackson #003242, #005957) in SNI model vs. WT littermates. Pain behavior, microglial markers, and cytokines at 7/14/21/28 days (n = 10/genotype, equal sexes). Predictions: TNFR1⁻/⁻ = reduced pain; TNFR2⁻/⁻ = exacerbated pain, impaired resolution.

**Approach 4D — Pairwise Combination Studies.** XPro1595 combined with lead KCC2 enhancer, ROCK inhibitor, and SSTR2a antagonist. Submaximal individual doses; Bliss Independence and isobolographic analysis for synergy classification.

**Outcome Measures:** von Frey, Hargreaves, acetone drop, CPP; multiplex ELISA (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, IL-10, TGF-β, CCL2); Iba-1/CD68/GFAP IHC; gelatin zymography (MMP-9); Evans Blue/IgG IHC (BSCB permeability); claudin-5/occludin/ZO-1 Western blot; spinal flow cytometry (CD45hi/CD11b+, Ly6G+, CD3+). n = 10–12/group.

### 2.5 Aim 5: Rational Synergy Screening and Quadruple Combination Optimization (Years 3–5)

**Phase 1 — Pairwise Matrix (Year 3).** All 6 pairwise combinations (KCC2 enhancer [K], ROCK inhibitor [R], SST intervention [S], TNFR1 blocker [T]) tested at 3 dose ratios each in SNI mice. Synergy classified by Bliss Independence and Loewe isobolographic models.

**Phase 2 — Triple Combinations (Year 4).** Best pairwise combinations (≥20% synergy) advance to all 4 triple combinations with factorial dose-response surface designs (3 doses × 3 agents = 27 groups per triple).

**Phase 3 — Quadruple Combination (Year 5).** K+R+S+T at fractional ED50 doses (1/2, 1/4, 1/8). Minimal effective combinatorial dose (MECD ≥50% allodynia reversal) and therapeutic index (TI = TD50_motor/ED50_analgesia) determined. Durability tested with chronic intermittent dosing (8 weeks), validated in CCI/CIPN/diabetic neuropathy models and in rat SNI.

**Outcome Measures:** Bliss combination index; Loewe isobolograms; dose-reduction index; TI; durability (time to 50% allodynia return); plasma/CSF pharmacokinetics; CBC, serum chemistry, organ histopathology (chronic safety). Factorial ANOVA, response surface modeling, Kaplan-Meier for durability.

## 3. EXPECTED RESULTS

### 3.1 Aim 1 — KCC2 Enhancement

CLP257 derivatives will dose-dependently reverse established mechanical allodynia with ED50 ≤30 mg/kg i.p. (≥3-fold potency improvement over parent CLP257 at ~100 mg/kg). AAV-S5E2-KCC2 will provide sustained, cell-type-restricted (>80% PV+ specificity) KCC2 expression with ≥4 weeks of analgesia, demonstrating the feasibility of durable gene therapy for neuropathic pain. At the mechanistic level, Clomeleon ratiometric FRET imaging in PV-Cre × Clomeleon mice will demonstrate restored low [Cl⁻]ᵢ (≤10 mM) in spinal PV+ interneurons; gramicidin-perforated patch clamp from PV+ neurons (identified by tdTomato in PV-Cre × Ai14 reporters) will confirm E_GABA shifts from depolarizing values (~-55 mV post-injury) toward hyperpolarizing values (~-70 mV, the normal resting potential). Male mice will show greater and more sustained reversal than females, consistent with BDNF-TrkB pathway sexual dimorphism (Mapplebeck et al., 2018; Dedek et al., 2019). If confirmed, this will inform sex-specific dosing in subsequent aims.

### 3.2 Aim 2 — ROCK Inhibition

Fasudil (10–30 mg/kg b.i.d.) will partially reverse established allodynia (ED50 ~10–15 mg/kg). AS1892802 will provide comparable analgesia with >50% reduced hypotensive liability. ROCK inhibition will suppress Iba-1 immunoreactivity (amoeboid→ramified shift), decrease p-p38 levels, and reduce Cavα2δ-1 surface expression. Microglia-specific ROCK1/2 KO (Cx3cr1-CreER) will be the primary driver — not neuronal or astrocytic ROCK — confirming the microglial locus of ROCK-mediated pain maintenance.

### 3.3 Aim 3 — SST+ Silencing

hM4Di-CNO will raise paw withdrawal thresholds from <0.1 g to >1.0 g within 30 min (4–6 h duration), without affecting acute thermal nociception. Optogenetic eNpHR will produce immediate (<1 s latency), light-locked allodynia reversal, causally establishing SST+ necessity. Intrathecal SSTR2a antagonists will achieve ED50 of 1–10 nmol. SST+ ablation (taCasp3) will eliminate allodynia while preserving acute nociception and motor function. GCaMP6s imaging will confirm hyperexcitability normalized by silencing.

### 3.4 Aim 4 — TNFR1 Blockade

XPro1595 will produce sustained reversal of mechanical and cold allodynia, with effects detectable by day 3 and plateauing by days 7–10. Critically, XPro1595 will demonstrate superior or equivalent analgesia to infliximab and etanercept at matched doses, but with preserved or enhanced IL-10 and TGF-β levels (indicating intact TNFR2-mediated pro-resolution signaling) and reduced systemic immunosuppression (preserved splenic lymphocyte counts and serum immunoglobulin levels). TNFR1⁻/⁻ mice will show ≥40% reduction in allodynia vs. WT littermates at all post-SNI timepoints; TNFR2⁻/⁻ mice will show exacerbated pain and impaired resolution, directly confirming the TNFR1/TNFR2 dichotomy. At the tissue level, XPro1595 will decrease spinal TNF-α, IL-1β, MMP-9 activity (gelatin zymography), Iba-1 immunoreactivity (M1→M2 microglial shift), and BSCB permeability (reduced Evans Blue extravasation, normalized claudin-5 and occludin expression). Intrathecal XPro1595 will achieve comparable spinal exposure and efficacy at ~10-fold lower doses than systemic administration, defining a translational path toward CNS-restricted anti-TNF therapy with minimal systemic immunosuppression.

### 3.5 Aim 5 — Synergy

**KCC2+ROCK:** moderate synergy (~20–30% above additivity). **KCC2+SST:** strong synergy (>40% above additivity; targeting ionic + circuit levels). **KCC2+TNFR1:** additive-to-moderate synergy. **ROCK+SST:** moderate-to-strong synergy. **ROCK+TNFR1:** additive. **SST+TNFR1:** additive-to-moderate. The quadruple combination at 1/4–1/2 ED50 will produce near-complete allodynia reversal (threshold >1.5 g vs. naïve ~2.0 g) with TI >10. KCC2+SST+TNFR1 will emerge as the most clinically tractable triple combination.

## 4. IMPACT AND TRANSLATIONAL POTENTIAL

### 4.1 Scientific Impact

This proposal will generate transformative insights across multiple domains of pain neuroscience. **First**, it will provide the first systematic demonstration that targeting four convergent disinhibition nodes — chloride homeostasis (KCC2), microglial/cytoskeletal signaling (ROCK), circuit output (SST), and neuroinflammation (TNFR1) — can achieve near-complete reversal of established neuropathic pain. This outcome, unattainable through decades of monotherapy research, would fundamentally shift the conceptual framework for chronic pain treatment from single-target pharmacology to network-level intervention. **Second**, the optogenetic SST experiments (Aim 3B) will provide the first millisecond-timescale causal proof that SST+ interneurons constitute the final common output pathway for mechanical allodynia. While prior chemogenetic and ablation studies have implicated SST+ neurons in allodynia, their slow temporal resolution (hours to days) leaves open the possibility of compensatory circuit adaptations. Optogenetic silencing at the timescale of sensory processing (seconds) will definitively resolve this question. **Third**, the TNFR1/TNFR2 dissection (Aim 4) will resolve a critical dichotomy in cytokine biology — establishing that receptor-selective anti-TNF therapy achieves superior outcomes by preserving TNFR2-mediated resolution — and will redirect clinical anti-cytokine strategies for pain. **Fourth**, the systematic synergy analysis (Aim 5) will establish rigorous quantitative methods (Bliss independence modeling, isobolographic analysis, response surface methodology) for evaluating multi-target pain therapies, providing a template that extends beyond the specific targets tested here. **Fifth**, by testing all interventions at chronic timepoints (≥21 days post-injury), this project will demonstrate that spinal disinhibition can be therapeutically *reversed*, not just prevented — directly addressing the clinically relevant scenario of established, treatment-resistant pain.

### 4.2 Clinical Translation Roadmap

The proposed strategy has been explicitly designed to leverage existing clinical data. **Near-term (<5 years):** Fasudil — a ROCK inhibitor with decades of clinical use in Asia for cerebral vasospasm and ongoing Phase II trials for ALS (ROCK-ALS) and Parkinson's disease (ROCK-PD) — could enter Phase II neuropathic pain trials immediately upon positive Aim 2 results. XPro1595, having completed Phase I safety testing for other indications, could follow a similarly accelerated timeline. The intrathecal delivery route is clinically established through implanted pump systems (baclofen, opioids, ziconotide), providing a clear regulatory pathway. Projected timeline: 3–5 years from preclinical completion to Phase II initiation for fasudil and XPro1595.

**Medium-term (5–10 years):** Optimized KCC2 enhancers (CLP257 derivatives from Aim 1) and SSTR2a antagonists (Aim 3C) could complete IND-enabling studies — formal toxicology in two species, genotoxicity, safety pharmacology, and manufacturing scale-up — and enter Phase I trials. Intrathecal SSTR2a antagonists leverage the regulatory precedent set by ziconotide (Prialt®, an intrathecal N-type calcium channel blocker approved for chronic pain).

**Long-term (>10 years):** AAV-KCC2 gene therapy (Aim 1B) and chemogenetic SST silencing (Aim 3A) represent transformative but longer-term strategies. AAV-mediated gene therapy has achieved clinical validation through approved therapies (Zolgensma for SMA; eladocagene exuparvovec for AADC deficiency). However, pain indications face additional hurdles: demonstration of durable efficacy in long-term follow-up, safety monitoring for AAV genomic integration, and the ethical considerations of irreversible gene therapy for a non-lethal condition. These approaches would be reserved for patients with severe, treatment-refractory neuropathic pain who have exhausted all conventional therapeutic options.

### 4.3 Broader Implications Beyond Nerve Injury

The therapeutic strategies developed here extend well beyond traumatic peripheral nerve injury. **CIPN** affects 30–70% of patients receiving frontline chemotherapeutics (paclitaxel, oxaliplatin, vincristine, bortezomib) and frequently persists long after cancer remission. CIPN shares all five disinhibition mechanisms: KCC2 downregulation, microglial activation, PNN degradation, TNF-α release, and HIF1α stabilization. Our explicit inclusion of CIPN models ensures direct relevance to this large and growing patient population. **Diabetic neuropathy**, affecting ~50% of diabetic patients, involves metabolic, vascular, and inflammatory drivers converging on the same spinal disinhibition cascade — ROCK inhibitors interrupt the mevalonate pathway implicated in diabetic nerve damage, and KCC2 enhancers may reverse glucose-induced Cl⁻ dysregulation. **Post-herpetic neuralgia, spinal cord injury pain, and CRPS** all feature spinal disinhibition and neuroinflammation, and the proposed multi-target approach may be adapted with indication-specific weighting of each pillar.

Beyond pain, KCC2 dysfunction is implicated in **epilepsy, schizophrenia, Rett syndrome, and autism spectrum disorders** — enhancers developed through Aim 1 could have direct therapeutic applications. **ROCK inhibitors** are in clinical development for glaucoma (reducing intraocular pressure), pulmonary hypertension (reducing vascular remodeling), and cancer metastasis — pain indications expand their therapeutic portfolio. The **circuit dissection approach** developed in Aim 3 provides a general template for circuit-based target identification applicable to itch, visceral pain, headache, and fibromyalgia, as well as circuit disorders including OCD, addiction, and epilepsy.

### 4.4 Addressing the Opioid Crisis

The ongoing opioid epidemic — responsible for over 80,000 deaths annually in the United States — is driven in significant part by prescription opioids for chronic non-cancer pain, for which long-term efficacy is minimal and risks of addiction and fatal respiratory depression are substantial. This proposal offers a non-opioid, mechanism-based alternative: (i) none of the four pillars targets μ-opioid receptors, eliminating addiction liability; (ii) KCC2 enhancers may restore endogenous opioid peptide efficacy (enkephalins, endorphins, dynorphins), which requires intact GABAergic inhibition for full analgesic effect (Lau et al., 2020); (iii) ROCK inhibition and TNFR1 blockade, by reducing neuroinflammatory pain drivers, may reduce the motivational salience of pain and consequent opioid-seeking behavior; (iv) effective non-opioid analgesia could reduce new opioid prescriptions and facilitate tapering in currently dependent patients.

### 4.5 Limitations and Mitigation Strategies

**KCC2 specificity:** Use S5E2 PV+-specific AAV enhancer + intrathecal delivery; monitor cognition/mood with behavioral batteries. **ROCK hypotension:** AS1892802 (peripherally restricted); co-administer midodrine if needed; explore intrathecal fasudil. **SSTR2a off-target effects:** Intrathecal delivery; develop biased ligands; explore peripherally restricted antagonists. **Anti-TNF immunosuppression:** TNFR1 selectivity preserves TNFR2 immune surveillance; intermittent dosing; intrathecal delivery at 10-fold lower doses. **Sex differences:** All experiments powered for sex as biological variable; sex-specific combination ratios if needed. **Model translatability:** Test across 3 models (SNI, CCI, CIPN); validate in rats; include CPP for spontaneous pain; consider porcine model for pre-IND studies.
