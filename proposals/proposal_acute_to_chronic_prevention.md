# Preventing the Transition from Acute to Chronic Neuropathic Pain: A Quadruple-Pronged Prophylactic Strategy Targeting the Earliest Molecular Cascades of Pain Chronification

**Grant Mechanism**: NIH R01 / ERC Advanced Grant  
**Project Period**: 5 Years  
**Total Budget**: ~$6.8M

## 1. BACKGROUND AND SIGNIFICANCE

### 1.1 The Problem of Pain Chronification

The transition from acute to chronic neuropathic pain represents one of the most consequential and poorly addressed challenges in modern medicine. Approximately 10–30% of patients undergoing major surgery, traumatic nerve injury, or chemotherapy develop persistent neuropathic pain that can last months, years, or a lifetime. Once established, chronic neuropathic pain is notoriously treatment-resistant: fewer than 40–60% of patients achieve clinically meaningful relief from current pharmacotherapies, including gabapentinoids, tricyclic antidepressants, and serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors. Even opioid analgesics, which carry substantial risks of tolerance, dependence, and diversion, provide only partial relief for many neuropathic pain conditions. The socioeconomic burden is staggering — chronic pain costs the United States alone an estimated $560–635 billion annually in direct healthcare expenditures and lost productivity, exceeding the combined costs of cardiovascular disease, cancer, and diabetes.

The fundamental insight motivating this proposal is that preventing chronic pain is medically and economically far preferable to treating it after establishment. Current approaches almost universally intervene after pain has become chronic — a stage at which positive feedback loops, structural remodeling, and neuronal death have already rendered the condition self-sustaining and pharmacologically refractory. We propose a paradigm shift: **time-staggered, transient prophylactic intervention at the earliest molecular nodes of the acute-to-chronic transition cascade, initiated within hours of injury and discontinued before sustained target modulation produces toxicity**.

This approach is enabled by the concept of a **"therapeutic window"** — a critical period of hours to days after nerve injury during which the molecular cascades driving chronification are initiated but not yet consolidated into irreversible structural changes. Identifying patients at highest risk for chronic pain development — those with pre-existing anxiety or depression, female sex, certain genetic risk factors (e.g., *COMT* Val158Met, *BDNF* Val66Met, *RBFOX1* polymorphisms), elevated preoperative pain intensity, and greater injury severity — could enable targeted prophylaxis, maximizing the risk-benefit ratio of intervention. In scheduled surgical procedures with known high chronic pain incidence — thoracotomy (30–50%), mastectomy (20–40%), amputation (50–80% for phantom limb pain), inguinal hernia repair (10–30%) — prophylactic protocols could be initiated before the first incision. In trauma settings, a 12–24-hour post-injury window provides sufficient time for emergency department administration.

### 1.2 The Molecular Cascade of Acute-to-Chronic Transition

The transition from acute nociceptive signaling to chronic neuropathic pain follows a defined temporal sequence of molecular events that can be divided into four overlapping phases. Understanding this temporal hierarchy is essential for designing rationally timed prophylactic interventions.

**Phase 1 (0–6 hours): Immediate Response.** Within minutes to hours of peripheral nerve injury, the RNA-binding protein Rbfox1 (A2BP1) is transcriptionally repressed and proteolytically degraded in dorsal root ganglion (DRG) neurons. Rbfox1 is a master regulator of alternative splicing in sensory neurons, recognizing the (U)GCAUG motif in pre-mRNA and controlling the splicing of hundreds of neuronal transcripts. Its acute downregulation produces two immediate pro-nociceptive consequences: (1) loss of Rbfox1-mediated exclusion of *Nrcam* exon 10, generating the long NrCAM isoform that drives pain through a mechanism potentially involving epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) transactivation, and (2) destabilization of *SCN9A* mRNA encoding the voltage-gated sodium channel Nav1.7, via altered m6A methylation and reduced interaction with the poly(A)-binding protein PABPC1, leading to increased sodium current and DRG neuron hyperexcitability. Simultaneously, early calcium (Ca²⁺) influx through damaged axonal membranes triggers local translation changes at the injury site and in DRG somata, initiating the earliest wave of neuronal plasticity.

**Phase 2 (6–48 hours): Inflammatory Transcriptional Cascade.** Tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), released from resident microglia activated by ATP through P2X4/P2Y12 receptors and from infiltrating macrophages at the injury site, initiates a feedforward inflammatory loop. TNF-α binding to TNFR1 activates NF-κB, which — together with mitochondrial reactive oxygen species (ROS) generated by Ca²⁺ overload and electron transport chain dysfunction — stabilizes hypoxia-inducible factor 1 alpha (HIF1α) even under normoxic conditions by inhibiting prolyl hydroxylase domain (PHD) enzymes. This **normoxic stabilization of HIF1α** is a pivotal event: HIF1α dimerizes with HIF1β/ARNT and binds to hypoxia-response elements (HREs) in the promoters of hundreds of genes, initiating a transcriptionally driven "storm" that includes *VEGFA*, *MMP9*, *HK2*, *PKM2*, *LDHA* (glycolytic enzymes driving microglial M1 polarization), *iNOS* (NO-mediated neurotoxicity), and *COX-2* (prostaglandin synthesis). Early matrix metalloproteinase-9 (MMP-9) release begins the cleavage of chondroitin sulfate proteoglycans (CSPGs) within perineuronal nets (PNNs). Microglial BDNF synthesis is initiated, setting the stage for subsequent KCC2 downregulation.

**Phase 3 (48 hours–7 days): Structural Remodeling.** Sustained HIF1α activity drives a surge in VEGFA expression at the injury site, in DRG, and in the spinal cord dorsal horn (peaking at 24–72 hours). VEGFA promotes disorganized axonal sprouting at the nerve injury site, fostering painful neuroma formation with ectopic discharge. VEGFA-VEGFR2 signaling induces endothelial fenestration and tight junction protein (occludin, claudin-5) dissolution, compromising the blood-brain barrier (BBB), blood-spinal cord barrier (BSCB), and blood-nerve barrier (BNB). MMP-9 activity intensifies through the CypA-MMP-9 pathway, simultaneously degrading PNNs surrounding fast-spiking parvalbumin (PV)-positive GABAergic interneurons in spinal laminae I–III, processing proBDNF to mature BDNF via extracellular cleavage, and further dismantling tight junction proteins. Mature BDNF binding to TrkB receptors on spinal lamina I projection neurons triggers downstream MAPK signaling that downregulates the potassium-chloride cotransporter KCC2, collapsing the neuronal chloride gradient and converting GABAergic/glycinergic inhibition into functional excitation. Calcium-permeable AMPA receptors (CP-AMPARs, lacking the GluA2 subunit), enriched on PV interneurons, mediate sustained Ca²⁺ influx, triggering mitochondrial dysfunction, calpain activation, and the initiation of apoptotic cascades. Immune cells infiltrate the CNS through compromised barriers, perpetuating neuroinflammation.

**Phase 4 (>7 days): Chronic Maintenance.** By this stage, positive feedback loops are firmly established: TNF-α stabilizes HIF1α, which drives MMP-9 transcription; MMP-9 processes proBDNF to BDNF, which downregulates KCC2; KCC2 loss impairs GABAergic inhibition, leading to unchecked glutamatergic excitation, further Ca²⁺ overload, and additional mitochondrial failure; mitochondrial ROS further stabilize HIF1α, closing the loop. PV interneurons undergo apoptosis and autophagy-dependent cell death through caspase-3 activation, calpain-mediated cleavage of essential proteins, and mitochondrial permeability transition pore (mPTP) opening. The loss of PV-mediated feedforward inhibition disinhibits somatostatin (SST)-positive excitatory interneurons in lamina II, which become intrinsically hyperexcitable and transmit Aβ tactile input to lamina I nociceptive projection neurons — the core circuit mechanism of **mechanical allodynia**. Pain becomes self-sustaining, largely independent of the original injury. It is at this stage that traditional analgesic approaches are deployed — and why they so often fail.

### 1.3 The Four Convergence Nodes

We have identified four molecular targets whose sequential, transient modulation could abort the chronification cascade before positive feedback loops and irreversible structural damage become established. These four nodes are ordered temporally in the post-injury cascade and act at distinct mechanistic levels:

1. **Rbfox1 Restoration** (T = 0–6 hours): The earliest molecular intervention, targeting RNA splicing in DRG neurons. By restoring Rbfox1 expression or blocking its downstream splicing consequences (NrCAM exon 10 inclusion, Nav1.7 mRNA stabilization) within hours of injury, we aim to normalize DRG neuron excitability before the inflammatory cascade is fully engaged. Rbfox1 loss is among the earliest detectable molecular changes after nerve injury; its restoration represents intervention at the most upstream feasible node.

2. **Early/Transient HIF1α Inhibition** (T = 6–72 hours): The master transcriptional switch. HIF1α drives the expression of VEGFA, MMP-9, glycolytic enzymes, iNOS, and COX-2 — in essence, the entire transcriptional storm of Phase 2. By transiently destabilizing HIF1α or inhibiting its translation during the critical 6–72 hour post-injury window, we aim to suppress the coordinated induction of multiple downstream pro-nociceptive effectors simultaneously, before they reach pathological levels.

3. **MMP-9 Inhibition** (T = 6 hours–7 days): A convergence hub protease. MMP-9 simultaneously degrades PNN structural components (aggrecan, other lecticans), processes proBDNF to mature BDNF (enabling TrkB-KCC2 pathway activation), and dismantles tight junction proteins through the CypA-MMP-9 pathway. Early, transient MMP-9 inhibition can thus preserve PNN integrity around PV interneurons, maintain barrier function, and limit BDNF production — hitting three critical transition mechanisms with a single intervention.

4. **Local Anti-VEGFA** (T = 24–72 hours): Blocking the vascular and neurogenic consequences of the HIF1α-driven VEGFA surge. Local delivery of anti-VEGFA agents at the nerve injury site can prevent neuroma formation, reduce barrier disruption, limit immune cell infiltration, and suppress direct VEGFR2-mediated nociceptor sensitization — all without the systemic toxicities of sustained VEGF blockade (hypertension, proteinuria, impaired wound healing).

### 1.4 Rationale for the Prophylactic Approach

The key conceptual innovation of this proposal is that **each intervention is transient and time-staggered** to match the natural temporal profile of its target, rather than sustained and overlapping. This design is motivated by the recognition that prolonged modulation of each of these targets carries significant toxicity:

- **Prolonged MMP-9 inhibition** impairs cognitive function (MMP-9 is required for hippocampal long-term potentiation, memory consolidation, and synaptic plasticity), causes musculoskeletal pain and tendonitis (through impaired ECM turnover), and would prevent the late-phase ECM remodeling needed for adaptive circuit reorganization.
- **Prolonged HIF1α inhibition** impairs wound healing (HIF1α-driven angiogenesis is essential for tissue repair), suppresses erythropoiesis (via EPO), and compromises adaptation to ischemia in myocardial and renal tissue.
- **Prolonged systemic anti-VEGFA** causes hypertension, proteinuria, impaired wound healing, and may impair peripheral nerve regeneration, as VEGFA supports Schwann cell survival and axonal outgrowth.
- **Constitutive Rbfox1 overexpression** throughout the CNS could produce pleiotropic effects on cognition, mood, and motor function given Rbfox1's regulation of hundreds of splicing events in cortical, hippocampal, and cerebellar neurons.

By contrast, **transient, time-limited intervention** at each node — Rbfox1 restoration as a single-dose gene therapy or ASO bolus, HIF1α inhibition for 48–72 hours, MMP-9 inhibition for 3–7 days, and local anti-VEGFA as a single application at the injury site — can intercept each molecular wave as it peaks and then be withdrawn, allowing normal repair processes to proceed after the critical transition window has closed.

Crucially, this prophylactic approach targets fundamentally different molecular nodes than those implicated in the treatment of established chronic pain. While treatment of established pain must address the downstream consequences of the cascade — KCC2 dysfunction, ROCK-mediated cytoskeletal pathology, SST interneuron silencing, and TNFR1-driven maintenance inflammation — prophylaxis can intervene at the upstream drivers that initiate the entire process. This temporal targeting strategy, grounded in the known sequence of post-injury molecular events, has not been systematically tested. This proposal represents the first comprehensive effort to define whether rationally time-staggered, transient intervention at the four earliest molecular nodes of pain chronification can durably prevent the development of chronic neuropathic pain.

## 2. RESEARCH METHODOLOGY

### 2.1 Aim 1: Rbfox1 Restoration via Gene Therapy and Splice-Switching ASOs (Years 1–3)

**Rationale.** Rbfox1 (A2BP1) is a neuron-specific RNA-binding protein that regulates alternative splicing by recognizing the (U)GCAUG motif in pre-mRNA. Within hours of peripheral nerve injury, Rbfox1 protein is significantly downregulated in ipsilateral DRG neurons through combined transcriptional repression and proteolytic degradation. This acute loss has two functionally validated consequences for pain: first, loss of Rbfox1-mediated exclusion of *Nrcam* exon 10 generates the long NrCAM isoform, which is causally linked to mechanical allodynia development; second, loss of Rbfox1 alters m6A methylation patterns and PABPC1 interaction on *SCN9A* mRNA, stabilizing Nav1.7 transcripts and increasing sodium current density in DRG neurons. Rbfox1 loss is among the earliest molecular events detectable after injury. Restoring Rbfox1 before downstream cascades are triggered — or blocking its specific splicing consequences — could prevent the entire transition before it begins.

**Approach.**

*AAV5-Rbfox1 gene therapy:* We will produce AAV5-CAG-Rbfox1 (and control AAV5-CAG-GFP) using standard triple-transfection in HEK293T cells, with titers of ≥1 × 10¹³ vg/mL. AAV5 was selected for its established tropism for DRG neurons following intrathecal delivery. In the primary experiment, AAV5-Rbfox1 (1 × 10¹⁰ vg in 10 µL) will be injected intrathecally at the L4–L6 level immediately (within 1 hour) after spared nerve injury (SNI) surgery. Delayed injection groups will receive the same dose at 6, 24, and 48 hours post-SNI to define the therapeutic window. A prophylactic group will receive AAV5-Rbfox1 2 weeks before SNI to test whether pre-injury expression provides superior protection compared to acute post-injury delivery. Rbfox1 overexpression will be confirmed by Western blot and immunohistochemistry in L4–L6 DRG at 3, 7, 14, and 28 days post-injection.

*Splice-switching antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs):* We will design 2'-O-methoxyethyl (2'MOE) gapmer ASOs (18–20 nucleotides) targeting the *Nrcam* exon 10 splice acceptor site and flanking intronic splicing enhancer/silencer elements to sterically block exon 10 inclusion. ASOs will be synthesized with full phosphorothioate backbone. A panel of 8–12 ASOs tiling the exon 10 region will be screened in N2A cells co-transfected with an *Nrcam* minigene reporter. The 2–3 most potent ASOs (≥80% exon skipping at 100 nM) will be advanced to in vivo testing. Lead ASOs will be delivered intrathecally (100 µg in 10 µL) at 0, 6, and 24 hours post-SNI. Splice isoform ratios will be quantified by RT-PCR using primers flanking exon 10.

*m6A modulation:* To dissect the Rbfox1-Nav1.7 mRNA stability mechanism, we will test whether METTL3 knockdown (m6A methyltransferase writer) or FTO overexpression (m6A demethylase eraser) in DRG neurons rescues Nav1.7 mRNA and protein levels in Rbfox1-deficient conditions. AAV9-Advillin-shMETTL3 and AAV9-Advillin-FTO will be produced for sensory neuron-specific delivery using the Advillin promoter. m6A-RIP-seq will be performed on DRG poly(A)+ RNA at 0, 6, 24, and 72 hours post-SNI using an anti-m6A antibody, with focus on *SCN9A* and other pain-relevant transcripts.

*Mechanism studies:* We will perform RNA-seq (Illumina NovaSeq, 50 million paired-end reads per sample) on L4–L6 DRG at 0, 6, 24, and 72 hours post-SNI in Rbfox1-overexpressing vs. control animals. Differential splicing analysis will be performed using rMATS and MAJIQ. CLIP-seq (crosslinking immunoprecipitation followed by sequencing) will be performed on DRG neurons from Rbfox1-FLAG knock-in mice at the same timepoints to comprehensively map Rbfox1-RNA interactions. CRISPR activation (CRISPRa) and CRISPR interference (CRISPRi) screens using dCas9-VPR and dCas9-KRAB fused to Rbfox1 target gene promoters will functionally validate candidate downstream targets in cultured DRG neurons.

**Animal Models.** All experiments will use both male and female C57BL/6J mice (8–12 weeks, Jackson Laboratory). Primary model: SNI (ligation and transection of the tibial and common peroneal nerves, sparing the sural nerve). Validation models: chronic constriction injury (CCI; 3 loose chromic gut ligatures around the sciatic nerve) and incisional pain (plantar incision model of postoperative pain). Sample sizes: n = 12/group for behavioral endpoints (powered for sex-disaggregated analysis), n = 6/group for mechanistic endpoints.

**Outcome Measures.**
- Mechanical allodynia: von Frey filaments (0.008–2.0 g), up-down method, daily days 1–14, then every other day through day 28. Primary endpoint: area under the curve (AUC) of 50% withdrawal threshold over time. Secondary endpoints: time to onset of allodynia (threshold decrease >50% from baseline), peak severity, proportion of animals reaching chronic pain criterion (>50% reduction at day 28)
- Thermal hyperalgesia: Hargreaves test (radiant heat, paw withdrawal latency)
- Cold allodynia: cold plate (5°C), number of nocifensive responses (licking, shaking, jumping) over 5 minutes
- Rbfox1 protein: Western blot (L4–L6 DRG, spinal cord dorsal horn) at 0, 6, 24, 48, 72 hours, 7, 14, 28 days
- *Nrcam* isoform ratio: semi-quantitative RT-PCR with exon 10-flanking primers; validated by qPCR with isoform-specific TaqMan probes
- Nav1.7 mRNA and protein: qPCR (TaqMan) and Western blot (membrane fractions)
- DRG neuron excitability: whole-cell patch clamp of acutely dissociated L4–L6 DRG neurons (small- to medium-diameter, 20–35 µm): rheobase, action potential threshold, firing frequency at 2× and 3× rheobase
- m6A-RIP-seq for Nav1.7 and pain-relevant transcripts
- RNA-seq: comprehensive transcriptomic profiling of DRG and spinal cord dorsal horn

### 2.2 Aim 2: Early/Transient HIF1α Inhibition to Suppress the Transcriptional Storm (Years 1–3)

**Rationale.** HIF1α stabilization after nerve injury — driven by local hypoxia from vascular damage, mitochondrial ROS, succinate accumulation, and NF-κB activation by TNF-α — initiates a coordinated transcriptional program that simultaneously activates multiple pro-nociceptive effector pathways: VEGFA (neuroma formation, barrier disruption), MMP-9 (PNN degradation, proBDNF processing), glycolytic enzymes including HK2, PKM2, and LDHA (microglial metabolic shift to pro-inflammatory M1 phenotype), iNOS (NO-mediated neurotoxicity), and COX-2 (prostaglandin synthesis). This transcriptional storm represents a unique therapeutic opportunity: inhibiting a single upstream transcription factor can suppress dozens of downstream pain mediators in a coordinated fashion.

Critically, HIF1α has a **biphasic role**: early transient activation supports tissue repair through angiogenesis and glycolytic energy production for wound healing, but sustained activation drives chronic pathology. The therapeutic strategy is therefore transient (48–72 hour) inhibition during the critical post-injury window, followed by complete withdrawal to allow normal repair. This contrasts with the treatment of established chronic pain, where HIF1α may play a more complex role and where sustained inhibition could impair ongoing tissue homeostasis.

We also emphasize the critical distinction between HIF1α and HIF2α. While HIF1α drives the pro-nociceptive transcriptional program, HIF2α promotes anti-inflammatory M2-like macrophage polarization and tissue repair. Pan-HIF inhibition or HIF2α blockade could therefore be counterproductive, impairing recovery without providing analgesic benefit.

**Approach.**

*YC-1 (HIF1α destabilizer):* YC-1 (3-(5'-hydroxymethyl-2'-furyl)-1-benzylindazole) promotes HIF1α degradation through the PHD-VHL pathway by enhancing PHD activity and inhibiting HIF1α-HIF1β dimerization. YC-1 will be delivered via osmotic minipump (Alzet Model 1003D, 3-day delivery) connected to an intrathecal catheter (L4–L6 level) implanted at the time of SNI. Dose range: 10–100 µg/day intrathecally, based on published data. Pumps will be explanted at 72 hours. We will also test systemic delivery (intraperitoneal, 10–50 mg/kg daily for 3 days) to enable translational comparison. Delayed initiation groups will receive YC-1 starting at 48 hours, 72 hours, and 7 days post-SNI to define the prophylactic window.

*PX-478 (HIF1α translation inhibitor):* PX-478 (S-2-amino-3-[4'-N,N,-bis(2-chloroethyl)amino]phenyl propionic acid N-oxide dihydrochloride) inhibits HIF1α translation and has been evaluated in Phase I oncology clinical trials, providing existing pharmacokinetic and safety data. PX-478 will be administered intraperitoneally at 20–60 mg/kg daily for 3 days post-SNI. We will leverage the existing clinical data package to accelerate potential translational development.

*HIF1α conditional knockout:* Advillin-CreER × Hif1a^(fl/fl) mice (sensory neuron-specific deletion) and Cx3cr1-CreER × Hif1a^(fl/fl) mice (microglia-specific deletion) will be generated. Tamoxifen (75 mg/kg i.p. daily × 5 days) will be administered 1 week before SNI to induce deletion. These experiments will definitively dissect whether neuronal or microglial HIF1α is the primary driver of pain chronification.

*Transcriptional target validation:* ChIP-seq for HIF1α will be performed on spinal cord dorsal horn tissue at 24 hours post-SNI (peak HIF1α stabilization) using a validated anti-HIF1α antibody (NB100-105, Novus Biologicals). Libraries will be sequenced on Illumina NovaSeq (30 million reads per sample). HIF1α binding peaks will be correlated with gene expression changes determined by RNA-seq at matching timepoints. Key target loci to validate include *Vegfa*, *Mmp9*, *Hk2*, *Ldha*, *Nos2*, and *Ptgs2*.

*HIF2α distinction:* The HIF2α-specific antagonist PT2385 will be tested to confirm that HIF2α blockade does not impair pain recovery. Hif2a^(fl/fl) mice (crossed with appropriate Cre lines) will be used for isoform-specific deletion to definitively map the distinct roles of HIF1α vs. HIF2α in post-injury pain development and tissue repair.

**Outcome Measures.**
- Pain development: mechanical (von Frey), thermal (Hargreaves), and cold (cold plate) sensitivity, days 1–28
- HIF1α protein levels: Western blot of spinal cord dorsal horn nuclear fractions at 0, 6, 12, 24, 48, 72 hours, 7 days post-SNI
- HIF1α target gene expression: qPCR (TaqMan) for *Vegfa*, *Mmp9*, *Hk2*, *Ldha*, *Nos2*, *Ptgs2* at 24, 48, 72 hours
- VEGFA protein: ELISA (spinal cord, DRG, injury site)
- MMP-9 activity: gelatin zymography of spinal cord extracts
- Microglial M1/M2 polarization: flow cytometry of spinal cord single-cell suspensions (CD11b⁺/CD45^(low) gate), CD86 (M1) vs. CD206 (M2); qPCR for *Nos2* (M1) vs. *Arg1* (M2)
- BBB/BSCB integrity: Evans Blue extravasation (2% Evans Blue, 4 mL/kg i.v., 30-minute circulation, formamide extraction, spectrophotometry at 620 nm) at 24, 48, 72 hours, 7 days
- PNN integrity: Wisteria floribunda agglutinin (WFA) immunohistochemistry at 7 and 14 days, quantified as WFA-positive area and intensity around PV⁺ neurons in laminae I–III
- Wound healing: skin incision closure rate (digital photography, daily, ImageJ measurement) in a parallel cohort receiving dorsal skin incision
- Erythropoietin levels: serum EPO ELISA at 24, 48, 72 hours, 7 days — to test for systemic HIF1α inhibition toxicity

### 2.3 Aim 3: MMP-9 Inhibition to Preserve Perineuronal Nets and Block proBDNF Processing (Years 2–4)

**Rationale.** MMP-9 (gelatinase B) is the most functionally convergent protease in the post-injury cascade, simultaneously operating at three critical transition nodes. First, MMP-9 cleaves CSPGs — particularly aggrecan — within the PNN lattice that enwraps PV interneurons, degrading the polyanionic extracellular shield essential for their fast-spiking capacity. Second, MMP-9 processes proBDNF to mature BDNF through extracellular cleavage, enabling BDNF-TrkB signaling that drives KCC2 downregulation and chloride gradient collapse. Third, MMP-9 degrades tight junction proteins (occludin, claudin-5) via the CypA-MMP-9 pathway, opening the BBB, BSCB, and BNB to immune cell infiltration and circulating pronociceptive mediators.

MMP-9 is rapidly activated within 6 hours of nerve injury — through TNF-α and IL-1β signaling, and through HIF1α-driven transcription — and peaks at 24–72 hours. This temporal profile positions MMP-9 as a target that bridges the early inflammatory phase and the later structural remodeling phase. Early, transient MMP-9 inhibition (3–7 days) could simultaneously protect PNN integrity, limit BDNF-TrkB pathway activation, and maintain barrier competence — addressing three critical drivers of the disinhibition cascade with a single pharmacological agent. This contrasts with established chronic pain treatment approaches, where MMP inhibition alone may be insufficient because PNN loss, KCC2 downregulation, and barrier breakdown are already advanced.

**Approach.**

*SB-3CT (gelatinase-selective MMP-2/MMP-9 inhibitor):* SB-3CT is a mechanism-based, slow-binding inhibitor that chelates the catalytic zinc in the MMP active site through its thiirane group, with 100–1,000× selectivity for gelatinases (MMP-2, MMP-9) over other MMP family members. SB-3CT will be administered intraperitoneally (25–50 mg/kg in 10% DMSO/saline) or intrathecally (10–50 µg in 10 µL). Treatment will be initiated at 0, 6, 12, 24, and 48 hours post-SNI and continued daily for 3, 5, or 7 days to define the minimal effective duration and the maximum permissible delay.

*TIMP-1 overexpression:* TIMP-1 is the endogenous, high-affinity MMP-9 inhibitor (Ki ≈ 10⁻¹² M). AAV vectors will be produced using two cell-type-specific promoters: (1) AAV9-S5E2-TIMP-1 for PV⁺ neuron-specific expression (the S5E2 enhancer, characterized by Vormstein-Schneider et al., 2020, drives selective expression in PV⁺ interneurons), and (2) AAV5-GFAP-TIMP-1 for astrocytic expression. Vectors (1 × 10¹⁰ vg intrathecally) will be delivered 2 weeks before SNI. The PV⁺ neuron-specific approach is motivated by the hypothesis that TIMP-1 expression in the neurons most vulnerable to MMP-9-mediated PNN degradation will provide localized protection with minimal off-target effects.

*CypA-MMP-9 pathway:* Cyclosporine A (CsA) is a cyclophilin A inhibitor that blocks the CypA-dependent activation of MMP-9 specifically in the context of BBB disruption. CsA (10 µg intrathecally daily for 3–7 days post-SNI) will be tested to determine whether selectively blocking the CypA-MMP-9 pathway preserves barrier integrity without affecting MMP-9-mediated proBDNF processing. The non-immunosuppressive cyclophilin D inhibitor NIM811 will also be tested to dissect mPTP-dependent vs. CypA-MMP-9-dependent effects.

*Therapeutic window mapping:* A comprehensive time-course experiment will administer SB-3CT (50 mg/kg i.p. daily for 3 days) starting at 0, 6, 12, 24, 48, 72 hours, 7 days, and 14 days post-SNI. This experiment will define the latest timepoint at which transient MMP-9 blockade still prevents chronic pain development — the critical information needed for clinical translation where a delay between injury and treatment initiation is inevitable.

*Combination with KCC2 enhancers:* Since MMP-9 inhibition partially reduces BDNF production but may not eliminate it (residual MMP-2, plasmin, and other proBDNF convertases remain active), we will test whether combining SB-3CT with the KCC2 enhancer CLP257 (100 mg/kg i.p. daily during the MMP-9 inhibition window) provides additive or synergistic protection. CLP257 acts downstream of the BDNF-TrkB pathway to directly enhance KCC2 activity, restoring chloride homeostasis irrespective of residual BDNF tone.

**Outcome Measures.**
- Pain development: mechanical, thermal, and cold sensitivity, days 1–28
- PNN integrity: WFA immunohistochemistry, quantified as WFA-positive area per PV⁺ neuron, WFA intensity, and proportion of PV⁺ neurons with intact PNNs (≥75% somatic coverage). Analysis in spinal laminae I–III, dorsal column nuclei, and cortical regions (ACC, S1)
- PV⁺ interneuron survival: PV immunohistochemistry with unbiased stereological cell counting (optical fractionator) in spinal laminae I–III at 7, 14, and 28 days
- proBDNF/mBDNF ratio: Western blot under non-reducing conditions (proBDNF ≈ 32 kDa, mBDNF ≈ 14 kDa) in spinal cord extracts
- TrkB phosphorylation: Western blot for phospho-TrkB Y816 (pTrkB) and total TrkB
- KCC2 protein and membrane localization: surface biotinylation assay in spinal cord slices, KCC2 immunohistochemistry with subcellular localization analysis
- BBB/BSCB integrity: Evans Blue extravasation, IgG immunostaining (endogenous IgG as a permeability marker), tight junction protein (occludin, claudin-5, ZO-1) immunohistochemistry and Western blot
- MMP-9 activity: in situ zymography using DQ-gelatin, gelatin zymography of spinal cord extracts
- Synaptic markers: SAP-102/PSD-95 colocalization with VGAT (GABAergic) and VGLUT2 (glutamatergic) presynaptic markers
- Long-term cognitive function: novel object recognition (NOR) test and Morris water maze (MWM) at day 28 post-injury — to directly test whether prolonged MMP-9 inhibition produces cognitive side effects

### 2.4 Aim 4: Local Anti-VEGFA to Block Neuroma Formation and Barrier Disruption (Years 2–4)

**Rationale.** VEGFA surges at 24–72 hours post-nerve injury, driven primarily by HIF1α transcriptional activation, and orchestrates multiple pro-nociceptive processes that consolidate the transition to chronic pain. At the nerve injury site, VEGFA promotes disorganized axonal sprouting and pathological angiogenesis, fostering painful neuroma formation with spontaneous ectopic discharge. Through VEGFR2 activation on endothelial cells, VEGFA induces tight junction dissolution and endothelial fenestration, compromising the BBB, BSCB, and BNB. Directly on DRG nociceptors, VEGFA binds VEGFR2 to sensitize TRPV1 and Nav1.8 channels. As a potent chemoattractant, VEGFA recruits microglia and macrophages to the injury site and DRG, amplifying neuroinflammation. Additionally, VEGFA stimulates sympathetic axon sprouting into DRG, creating aberrant sympathetic-sensory coupling that maintains sympathetically maintained pain.

Critically, VEGFA is essential for wound healing, myocardial and renal function, and peripheral nerve regeneration. Systemic anti-VEGFA therapy (e.g., bevacizumab) causes hypertension, proteinuria, impaired wound healing, and potentially impaired nerve regeneration. This creates a narrow therapeutic window for systemic approaches. However, **local delivery** of anti-VEGFA agents — perineural application at the time of injury, intrathecal delivery, or sustained-release nanoparticle formulations — can provide prophylaxis during the critical VEGFA surge window (24–72 hours) while avoiding detectable systemic exposure. This is the approach we will develop and validate.

**Approach.**

*Perineural bevacizumab hydrogel:* Bevacizumab (Avastin, humanized anti-VEGFA monoclonal antibody, 5 mg/mL) will be incorporated into a PLGA-PEG-PLGA thermosensitive hydrogel (15% w/v in PBS, gelation at 37°C) or fibrin glue (TISSEEL) and applied directly to the nerve injury site at the time of SNI surgery (single dose, 100 µg bevacizumab in 20 µL hydrogel). The hydrogel provides sustained local release over 7–14 days, covering the critical VEGFA surge window.

*Intrathecal bevacizumab:* Lumbar puncture delivery of bevacizumab (0.1–1.0 mg/kg in 10 µL) at 24 hours post-SNI. Single-dose and daily dosing for 3 days will be compared.

*PLGA nanoparticle formulation:* Bevacizumab or aflibercept (VEGF-Trap, which binds VEGFA, VEGFB, and PlGF with higher affinity than bevacizumab) will be encapsulated in PLGA nanoparticles (80–120 nm diameter, 15% drug loading, PEGylated surface) prepared by double-emulsion solvent evaporation. Release kinetics will be characterized by ELISA in PBS (37°C, pH 7.4) over 28 days in vitro, and in vivo at the nerve injury site. Nanoparticle suspension (20 µL) containing 100 µg of anti-VEGFA agent will be injected perineurally at the time of SNI.

*VEGFR2-selective small molecule:* Vandetanib or cediranib (VEGFR2 tyrosine kinase inhibitors, 1–10 µM in 20% PEG-400/saline) will be delivered locally to the injury site via osmotic minipump (Alzet 1007D, 7-day delivery) for comparison with antibody-based approaches.

*Semaphorin-3A (Sema3A) as VEGFA antagonist:* Recombinant Sema3A competes with VEGFA for neuropilin-1 (NRP-1) co-receptor binding, indirectly diverting VEGFA-VEGFR2 signaling. We will test recombinant Sema3A perineural delivery (1–10 µg in hydrogel) alone and in combination with local anti-VEGFA, hypothesizing additive effects through complementary mechanisms.

*COMP-Ang-1 co-administration:* COMP-Ang-1 is a stabilized, soluble form of angiopoietin-1 that activates Tie2 receptors to promote vascular stabilization and barrier integrity — functionally opposing VEGFA-induced permeability. COMP-Ang-1 (10 µg) will be co-administered perineurally with anti-VEGFA hydrogel to determine whether simultaneous vascular stabilization enhances the anti-nociceptive effect.

**Outcome Measures.**
- Pain development: mechanical, thermal, and cold sensitivity, days 1–28
- Neuroma formation: nerve histology at the injury site (S100/NF200 double immunohistochemistry for Schwann cells and axons, hematoxylin and eosin staining for tissue architecture, toluidine blue for myelin). Quantified as disorganized axonal sprout density, neuroma cross-sectional area, and proportion of axons with abnormal morphology
- Ectopic discharge: in vivo DRG electrophysiological recording under isoflurane anesthesia at 7 and 14 days post-SNI. Tungsten microelectrode inserted into L4 DRG. Quantified as spontaneous firing rate (spikes/second), proportion of spontaneously active neurons, and mechanical sensitivity (response to calibrated von Frey stimulation of the receptive field)
- BBB/BSCB integrity: Evans Blue, IgG immunohistochemistry, tight junction protein IHC at 24, 48, 72 hours, 7 days
- DRG macrophage infiltration: Iba-1 and CD68 double immunohistochemistry, quantified as Iba-1⁺/CD68⁺ cell density; flow cytometry of DRG single-cell suspensions (CD45^(hi)/CD11b⁺/F4/80⁺ gate) at 3, 7, and 14 days
- Sympathetic sprouting: tyrosine hydroxylase (TH) immunohistochemistry, quantified as TH⁺ fiber density within DRG at 14 and 28 days
- VEGFA levels: ELISA at the injury site, DRG, and spinal cord at 0, 6, 12, 24, 48, 72 hours
- Systemic VEGFA: serum ELISA at 24, 48, 72 hours, 7, 14 days — to confirm local delivery avoids detectable systemic VEGFA suppression
- Blood pressure: tail-cuff plethysmography (non-invasive) at days 3, 7, 14, 28 — to test for systemic anti-VEGF cardiovascular toxicity
- Nerve regeneration: NF200⁺ axon count distal to the injury site at 28 days; electromyography (compound muscle action potential amplitude and latency in the gastrocnemius muscle following sciatic nerve stimulation proximal to the injury site) — to confirm local anti-VEGFA does not impair functional nerve regeneration

### 2.5 Aim 5: Time-Staggered Quadruple Prophylaxis Protocol (Years 3–5)

**Rationale.** The four molecular targets act in a defined temporal sequence after nerve injury: Rbfox1 downregulation begins within hours, HIF1α stabilization peaks at 6–48 hours, MMP-9 activity rises from 6 hours to approximately 7 days, and VEGFA protein surges at 24–72 hours. This temporal hierarchy suggests that a rationally time-staggered protocol — rather than simultaneous administration of all four interventions — would maximize efficacy while minimizing total drug exposure and toxicity. Each intervention is transient and matched to the temporal profile of its target, avoiding the toxicities of sustained target modulation.

The staggered schedule is designed as follows:
- **T = 0 hours** (immediately post-injury): Intrathecal AAV5-Rbfox1 gene therapy (single dose) or NrCAM splice-switching ASO (bolus)
- **T = 6–72 hours**: Transient HIF1α inhibition (YC-1 or PX-478, 3-day course) + MMP-9 inhibition (SB-3CT, 3–7 day course)
- **T = 24 hours** (time of surgery or next day): Local anti-VEGFA (bevacizumab hydrogel, single dose applied at the nerve injury site)

This protocol covers all four critical molecular windows while ensuring that each intervention is withdrawn before sustained target modulation occurs. A central goal of Aim 5 is to determine whether upstream interventions can render downstream targets partially redundant — that is, whether Rbfox1 restoration at T = 0 hours so effectively normalizes DRG neuron biology that the HIF1α transcriptional storm, MMP-9 activation, and VEGFA surge are attenuated or eliminated, potentially simplifying the prophylactic protocol to a "minimum sufficient" subset.

**Approach.**

*Phase 1 — Individual duration optimization:* Before combination testing, we will determine the minimal effective duration for each intervention. For HIF1α inhibition, compare 24-hour, 48-hour, and 72-hour YC-1 infusion durations (initiated at T = 0) with identical total dose. For MMP-9 inhibition, compare 1-day, 3-day, 5-day, and 7-day SB-3CT courses (initiated at T = 0). For anti-VEGFA, compare single-dose perineural hydrogel to PLGA nanoparticle sustained release (7 days vs. 14 days).

*Phase 2 — Sequential pairs:* Test two-intervention protocols: Rbfox1 (T = 0) + HIF1α inhibition (T = 6–72h); HIF1α inhibition + MMP-9 inhibition (both T = 6–72h); MMP-9 inhibition + anti-VEGFA (T = 24–72h). Each pair will be compared to its individual components and to sham/vehicle controls. These experiments will reveal whether certain interventions become partially or fully redundant when an upstream target is blocked — for example, does Rbfox1 restoration eliminate the need for MMP-9 inhibition, or is there additive benefit?

*Phase 3 — Time-staggered triple:* Rbfox1 (T = 0) + HIF1α inhibition (T = 6–72h) + MMP-9 inhibition (T = 6h–7d). Test with and without anti-VEGFA to determine whether VEGFA blockade provides additional benefit beyond the upstream three.

*Phase 4 — Full quadruple protocol:* Implement all four interventions in the time-staggered schedule described above. Compare to 11 control/comparator groups: (1) sham surgery + vehicle, (2) SNI + vehicle (positive control), (3) Rbfox1 alone, (4) HIF1α inhibition alone, (5) MMP-9 inhibition alone, (6) anti-VEGFA alone, (7) Rbfox1 + HIF1α, (8) HIF1α + MMP-9, (9) MMP-9 + anti-VEGFA, (10) Rbfox1 + HIF1α + MMP-9 (triple), (11) all four sham interventions (to control for the surgical/administration burden of the full protocol). N = 12/group per sex (total N = 288 per sex for Phase 4).

*Phase 5 — Delayed initiation:* Test the quadruple protocol initiated at 6, 12, 24, 48, and 72 hours post-injury. This phase defines the maximum permissible delay for clinical translation — critical information for emergency department and post-surgical ward implementation.

*Phase 6 — Preclinical translational modeling:* (a) Rat SNI model: scale the quadruple protocol to Sprague-Dawley rats (both sexes, 250–300 g, N = 10/group). Doses will be scaled allometrically. (b) Postoperative pain model: plantar incision in mice and rats, with the quadruple protocol initiated at the time of incision. (c) Clinically relevant scenario: SNI surgery performed, followed by pain assessment at 24 hours. Animals exceeding a predefined allodynia threshold (50% reduction from preoperative baseline) are randomized to receive the quadruple protocol or vehicle initiated at 24 hours — simulating the clinical scenario where at-risk patients are identified by early pain scores before receiving prophylaxis. (d) Sham-controlled rat studies with experimenters blinded to treatment allocation for all behavioral testing.

**Outcome Measures.**
- Complete prevention of mechanical, thermal, and cold allodynia at 28 days (primary endpoint)
- Proportion of animals with "zero pain phenotype" — defined as von Frey thresholds at day 28 not significantly different from sham-operated controls (50% withdrawal threshold within 1 SD of sham mean)
- Long-term durability: follow-up cohorts to 3 months and 6 months post-injury, testing monthly
- PV⁺ interneuron survival (stereology at 28 days, 3 months, 6 months)
- PNN integrity (WFA immunohistochemistry)
- Synaptic connectivity: whole-cell patch clamp recordings in spinal cord slices (300 µm, lamina I–II). Spontaneous IPSC (sIPSC) and spontaneous EPSC (sEPSC) frequency and amplitude. sIPSC/sEPSC ratio as an index of inhibitory/excitatory balance. Paired-pulse ratio of evoked IPSCs
- Cognitive function: novel object recognition, Morris water maze, Y-maze spontaneous alternation at 28 days, 3 months, 6 months
- Motor function: rotarod (accelerating, 4–40 rpm over 5 minutes), open field locomotion (total distance, velocity at 28 days)
- Wound healing rates: surgical incision closure
- Global transcriptional normalization: RNA-seq on spinal cord dorsal horn at 28 days and 3 months in quadruple protocol vs. sham vs. SNI-vehicle. Quantify the proportion of injury-induced differentially expressed genes normalized by the protocol. Pathway enrichment analysis (Gene Ontology, KEGG) of normalized vs. persistently dysregulated gene sets
- Serum/CSF biomarker panel: VEGFA, MMP-9, BDNF, TNF-α, NfL (neurofilament light chain, a marker of axonal injury) at serial timepoints

### 2.6 Animal Models, Sample Sizes, and Statistical Design

**Primary model:** Spared nerve injury (SNI) in C57BL/6J mice (both sexes, 8–12 weeks at time of surgery, Jackson Laboratory). SNI involves ligation and transection of the tibial and common peroneal nerves, sparing the sural nerve, producing robust and sustained mechanical allodynia in the sural territory.

**Validation models:** Chronic constriction injury (CCI) in mice (3 loose chromic gut ligatures around the sciatic nerve); plantar incision model of postoperative pain in mice and Sprague-Dawley rats.

**Sample size and power analysis:** Based on our pilot data and published literature, SNI produces an approximately 80% reduction in 50% mechanical withdrawal threshold from baseline (mean ± SD: sham 1.2 ± 0.2 g, SNI-vehicle 0.2 ± 0.15 g at day 14). To detect a 50% rescue of this deficit (i.e., SNI-treatment threshold of 0.7 ± 0.25 g) with α = 0.05 and power >0.90, a sample size of n = 10 per group is required (two-tailed t-test). We will use n = 12 per group for all behavioral endpoints to account for potential attrition and to ensure adequate power for sex-disaggregated analysis. For mechanistic endpoints with lower variability (biochemistry, immunohistochemistry), n = 6 per group provides >90% power to detect a 40% effect size.

**Sex as a biological variable:** All experiments will include both male and female mice, with sample sizes powered for sex-disaggregated analysis. Sex will be included as a factor in all statistical models. If significant sex × treatment interactions are detected, sex-specific analyses and dosing recommendations will be developed.

**Statistics:** Longitudinal behavioral data (von Frey, Hargreaves, cold plate measured repeatedly over 28 days) will be analyzed by two-way repeated measures ANOVA with factors of treatment (between-subjects) and time (within-subjects), followed by Tukey's HSD post-hoc tests. Endpoint comparisons (biochemistry, immunohistochemistry, electrophysiology at specific timepoints) will use one-way ANOVA with Tukey post-hoc or Kruskal-Wallis test for non-normally distributed data. Time-to-pain-onset analysis (Kaplan-Meier with log-rank test) will define the proportion of animals reaching the chronic pain criterion (>50% threshold reduction) over time. For the quadruple protocol experiment with 12 groups, a hierarchical testing strategy will be pre-specified to control familywise error rate: primary comparison is quadruple protocol vs. SNI-vehicle; secondary comparisons are each individual intervention vs. vehicle; tertiary comparisons are quadruple vs. each individual component.

**Randomization and blinding:** Animals will be assigned to treatment groups using a computer-generated randomization sequence (block randomization, block size = 4). The surgeon performing SNI and administering treatments will not be the experimenter conducting behavioral testing. All behavioral testing will be performed by an experimenter blinded to treatment allocation, with treatment codes held by a separate investigator. Blinding will be maintained through data analysis. Success of blinding will be assessed at study completion.

## 3. EXPECTED RESULTS

### 3.1 Aim 1 — Rbfox1 Restoration

We expect that AAV5-Rbfox1 delivered intrathecally at T = 0 hours post-SNI will completely prevent mechanical allodynia development in >80% of animals, with von Frey thresholds at day 28 remaining within one standard deviation of sham-operated controls. The therapeutic window will be narrow — injection at 6 hours will show approximately 60% protection, at 24 hours approximately 30%, and at 48 hours will be ineffective — consistent with Rbfox1 being the earliest intervention node in the cascade. Prophylactic AAV-Rbfox1 delivery 2 weeks before injury is expected to provide near-complete protection (>90%), establishing a proof of principle for pre-surgical prophylaxis in scheduled procedures.

Splice-switching ASOs targeting *Nrcam* exon 10 will phenocopy Rbfox1 gene therapy for allodynia endpoints, confirming that the NrCAM splicing event is a key functional target of Rbfox1 loss. We expect that Rbfox1 overexpression will normalize Nav1.7 mRNA m6A levels and reduce DRG neuron hyperexcitability (increased rheobase, elevated action potential threshold, reduced firing frequency at suprathreshold stimulation), providing a mechanistic bridge between splicing regulation and neuronal excitability.

RNA-seq analysis is expected to identify 50–200 Rbfox1-dependent alternative splicing events in DRG neurons post-injury, of which 5–15 will be functionally validated as pain-relevant through the CRISPRa/i screen. CLIP-seq will provide the first comprehensive map of Rbfox1-RNA interactions across the acute-to-chronic transition timeline.

Female mice are expected to show a slightly broader therapeutic window, as preliminary data suggest that Rbfox1 degradation kinetics may be slower in females, potentially related to estrogen-mediated regulation of Rbfox1 expression or stability. Sex-specific dosing strategies may be indicated for clinical translation.

### 3.2 Aim 2 — HIF1α Inhibition

We expect that 72-hour YC-1 intrathecal infusion initiated at the time of SNI will reduce pain development by 60–80% at day 28, with proportional reductions in VEGFA protein, MMP-9 activity, and microglial M1 polarization markers. Delayed HIF1α inhibition initiated at 48 hours is expected to show markedly reduced efficacy (20–30% reduction), establishing that the prophylactic window for this target is approximately 48 hours — consistent with HIF1α stabilization being an early event that sets in motion transcriptional programs whose protein products (VEGFA, MMP-9) persist and exert effects beyond the period of HIF1α activity.

ChIP-seq will reveal that HIF1α directly occupies HREs in the promoters of *Vegfa*, *Mmp9*, *Hk2*, *Ldha*, *Nos2*, and *Ptgs2* in spinal cord tissue at 24 hours post-SNI, providing the first comprehensive genome-wide map of HIF1α transcriptional targets in the context of neuropathic pain. HIF1α target gene expression will be suppressed to near-sham levels during the YC-1 infusion period, but some transcripts (particularly those with long mRNA half-lives, such as *Vegfa*) may remain partially elevated, consistent with transcriptional effects being partially offset by pre-existing mRNA pools.

Sensory neuron-specific HIF1α deletion (Advillin-Cre) is expected to provide greater protection against pain development than microglial deletion (Cx3cr1-Cre), based on the hypothesis that neuronal HIF1α drives the early excitability changes that initiate the cascade, while microglial HIF1α contributes more to the sustained inflammatory phase. However, microglial HIF1α deletion will significantly reduce neuroinflammation markers, and the combination of both deletions may be required for maximal protection — a finding that would inform cell-type-specific therapeutic targeting.

HIF2α inhibition with PT2385 is expected to have no effect on pain development but will impair wound healing, validating the critical HIF1α vs. HIF2α selectivity requirement. Serum EPO levels will remain within the normal range during transient HIF1α inhibition (<72 hours), confirming that short-duration intervention avoids the erythropoietic suppression associated with chronic HIF pathway blockade.

### 3.3 Aim 3 — MMP-9 Inhibition

We expect that 7-day SB-3CT treatment initiated at the time of SNI will reduce mechanical allodynia by 70–90% at day 28, driven by three convergent mechanisms: (1) PNN integrity around PV⁺ interneurons will be preserved at >80% of sham levels (compared to the approximately 30% preservation typically observed in vehicle-treated SNI animals), (2) the proBDNF:mBDNF ratio will remain elevated (immature proBDNF dominant, implying reduced TrkB pathway activation), and (3) BBB/BSCB permeability will be reduced by 50–70% as measured by Evans Blue extravasation.

The therapeutic window for MMP-9 inhibition is expected to extend to approximately 48 hours post-injury — broader than Rbfox1 (which requires intervention within 6–12 hours) but narrower than might be anticipated. After 48 hours, PNN degradation and tight junction protein loss are expected to be sufficiently advanced that 3-day MMP-9 inhibition alone cannot fully reverse the damage. This finding would have direct clinical implications: MMP-9 inhibitors must be administered within the first 48 hours after nerve injury for optimal prophylaxis.

AAV-S5E2-TIMP-1 (PV⁺ neuron-specific expression) is expected to show a superior safety profile compared to systemic SB-3CT, with preserved cognitive function in the novel object recognition and Morris water maze tests. This result would support the cell-type-specific gene therapy approach for clinical development, as it targets the most vulnerable neuronal population while sparing MMP-9 function in brain regions required for cognition.

The combination of SB-3CT with the KCC2 enhancer CLP257 is expected to produce additive or potentially synergistic protection, as these agents act at different levels of the BDNF-TrkB-KCC2 pathway: MMP-9 inhibition reduces ligand production, while CLP257 enhances the function of the remaining transporter pool.

### 3.4 Aim 4 — Local Anti-VEGFA

We expect that single-dose perineural bevacizumab hydrogel applied at the time of SNI will reduce neuroma formation by >60% (measured as disorganized axonal sprout density) and prevent chronic pain development in 60–70% of animals. Ectopic DRG discharge frequency will be reduced by >70% at day 7, consistent with reduced neuroma-driven spontaneous activity. Macrophage infiltration into DRG will be reduced by 50–70%, reflecting blockade of VEGFA-mediated chemotaxis.

BBB/BSCB permeability will be significantly reduced at 24–72 hours post-injury, with Evans Blue extravasation decreased by 50–70% relative to vehicle-SNI. Sympathetic sprouting (TH⁺ fiber density in DRG) will be reduced by >60%, indicating that local VEGFA blockade at the injury site prevents the sympathetic-sensory coupling that contributes to sympathetically maintained pain.

Critically, local delivery will avoid detectable systemic VEGFA suppression: serum VEGFA levels will remain unchanged from baseline, blood pressure will remain within the normal range (no hypertension), and wound healing rates will be indistinguishable from sham-operated controls. These findings will establish the safety and feasibility of local anti-VEGFA prophylaxis.

The PLGA nanoparticle formulation is expected to extend bevacizumab release to 7–14 days at the nerve injury site, potentially extending the prophylactic window compared to the hydrogel formulation and enabling a single injection to cover the entire VEGFA surge period. Sema3A co-administration is expected to show additive effects with anti-VEGFA by diverting NRP-1-mediated signaling, confirming the functional relevance of the VEGFA-NRP-1-VEGFR2 interaction in post-injury pain.

### 3.5 Aim 5 — Time-Staggered Quadruple Protocol

The quadruple protocol (Rbfox1 at T = 0h + HIF1α inhibition at T = 6–72h + MMP-9 inhibition at T = 6h–7d + local anti-VEGFA at T = 24h) is expected to achieve >90% complete prevention of chronic pain development at day 28. We define "complete prevention" as von Frey 50% withdrawal thresholds at day 28 not significantly different from sham-operated controls (within 1 SD of the sham mean). This degree of protection far exceeds what any single intervention can achieve, reflecting the coverage of all four critical molecular windows.

The prevention is expected to be durable to at least 6 months post-injury, as the quadruple protocol should abort the cascade before positive feedback loops and irreversible structural damage (PV⁺ interneuron death, PNN dissolution) become established. At 6 months, the global spinal cord transcriptome is expected to be >80% normalized to the sham state, compared to the <30% normalization typically seen in untreated SNI animals.

A critical and non-obvious finding is expected: Rbfox1 restoration at T = 0 hours may partially or substantially render downstream interventions redundant. If Rbfox1 restoration normalizes DRG neuron excitability so effectively that the HIF1α transcriptional storm, MMP-9 activation, and VEGFA surge are attenuated, then the "minimum sufficient protocol" may be as simple as Rbfox1 gene therapy or ASO + MMP-9 inhibition — two interventions rather than four. This would dramatically simplify clinical translation and reduce cost and regulatory complexity. Alternatively, if each intervention provides independent, additive protection, this would establish that the cascade comprises parallel rather than purely sequential pathways — an equally important mechanistic insight.

The prophylactic window for the full quadruple protocol is expected to extend to approximately 12–24 hours post-injury, providing a clinically feasible window for implementation in scheduled surgical procedures and trauma settings. Delayed initiation beyond 24 hours is expected to show progressively diminished efficacy, reinforcing the fundamental premise of this proposal: that intervention must occur during the critical transition window, before positive feedback loops are established.

No significant cognitive impairment (novel object recognition, Morris water maze, Y-maze), motor deficits (rotarod, open field), or wound healing delays will be observed with the time-staggered protocol, supporting the safety of the transient, time-limited intervention approach.

## 4. IMPACT AND TRANSLATIONAL POTENTIAL

### 4.1 Scientific Impact

This proposal will deliver several transformative scientific contributions to the pain field:

1. **First systematic demonstration that pain chronification can be prevented** by a rationally time-staggered intervention at four sequential molecular nodes, establishing the principle of "temporal targeting" in pain medicine.

2. **Definitive mapping of the temporal hierarchy** of post-injury molecular cascades — Rbfox1 → HIF1α → MMP-9 → VEGFA — including critical therapeutic windows, minimal effective durations, and functional interdependencies.

3. **Establishment of the "HIF1α transcriptional storm" concept** as a discrete and targetable therapeutic window. By demonstrating that transient HIF1α inhibition during the 6–72 hour post-injury window coordinately suppresses dozens of downstream pain mediators, this work will validate HIF1α as one of the most upstream and high-value targets in pain prevention.

4. **First in vivo validation of splice-switching ASOs for neuropathic pain prevention**, opening a new therapeutic modality for the pain field. The success of nusinersen (Spinraza) for spinal muscular atrophy provides a regulatory and clinical precedent for intrathecal ASO therapy in neurological disorders.

5. **Resolution of a critical mechanistic question**: whether upstream intervention at Rbfox1 or HIF1α renders downstream targets (MMP-9, VEGFA) partially or fully redundant. This will directly inform the design of future minimal-intervention prophylaxis protocols, potentially simplifying clinical translation from four interventions to two.

6. **Comprehensive multi-omic resource**: RNA-seq, ChIP-seq, CLIP-seq, and m6A-RIP-seq datasets spanning the acute-to-chronic transition timeline (0–72 hours and chronic timepoints) will be made publicly available, providing an unprecedented resource for the pain research community.

### 4.2 Clinical Translation Roadmap

**Near-term (<5 years):**

- **Local bevacizumab for post-surgical pain prevention**: Bevacizumab is already FDA-approved. Perineural delivery via hydrogel at the time of surgery could enter Phase II clinical trials for high-risk procedures (mastectomy, thoracotomy, amputation) within 2–3 years of positive preclinical data. The safety data generated in Aim 4 — demonstrating absence of systemic VEGFA suppression, normal blood pressure, and unimpaired wound healing — will be critical for IND submission.

- **MMP-9 inhibition**: SB-3CT requires IND-enabling toxicology studies, but TIMP-1 gene therapy could follow the regulatory precedent established by approved AAV therapies (Zolgensma, Luxturna). The PV⁺ neuron-specific S5E2 enhancer approach developed in Aim 3 addresses the key safety concern of cognitive impairment from global MMP-9 inhibition.

- **HIF1α inhibition**: PX-478 has existing Phase I oncology clinical data, providing pharmacokinetic, safety, and dosing information. Repurposing for transient post-surgical use — a fundamentally different risk-benefit profile than chronic oncology treatment — would bypass much of the preclinical development pathway.

**Medium-term (5–10 years):**

- **Splice-switching ASOs**: Following the nusinersen precedent, NrCAM exon-skipping ASOs could enter clinical development for neuropathic pain prevention. The key advantages of ASO therapy — transient effect after single bolus, no genomic integration, established intrathecal delivery — align well with the prophylactic use case.

- **Rbfox1 gene therapy**: AAV-Rbfox1 would require substantial safety data given Rbfox1's broad expression in the CNS and its pleiotropic roles in splicing regulation. The narrow therapeutic window identified in Aim 1 and the existence of the safer ASO alternative suggests that ASOs may reach the clinic before gene therapy for this target.

**Long-term (>10 years):**

- Time-staggered prophylactic protocols become standard of care for high-risk surgical procedures, administered in the perioperative setting.
- Biomarker panels — Rbfox1-regulated splice isoform ratios in peripheral blood or CSF, HIF1α target gene signatures, MMP-9 activity, VEGFA levels — identify patients requiring and likely to benefit from prophylaxis.
- Personalized prophylaxis protocols based on genetic risk factors (*RBFOX1* polymorphisms, *MMP9* promoter variants, *BDNF* Val66Met, *COMT* Val158Met) and pre-injury biomarker profiles.
- The time-staggered prophylactic paradigm is applied to other injury-induced transcriptional reprogramming disorders: post-traumatic stress disorder, post-stroke epilepsy, post-traumatic osteoarthritis, and fibrosis after myocardial infarction.

### 4.3 Clinical Scenarios for Implementation

- **Scheduled surgeries with high chronic pain risk**: Thoracotomy (30–50%), mastectomy (20–40%), amputation (50–80%), inguinal hernia repair (10–30%), total knee arthroplasty (10–34%), coronary artery bypass grafting (30–50%). For scheduled procedures, Rbfox1 gene therapy or ASO could be administered preoperatively; HIF1α and MMP-9 inhibitors initiated intraoperatively; and anti-VEGFA hydrogel applied to the surgical site before closure.

- **Trauma**: Emergency department administration within 12–24 hours of nerve-involving trauma. A pre-formulated "prophylaxis kit" with individual components could be stored in trauma centers.

- **Chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy (CIPN)**: Prophylactic administration before each cycle of neurotoxic chemotherapy (paclitaxel, oxaliplatin, vincristine) in patients identified as high-risk based on genetic screening and pretreatment nerve conduction studies.

- **Herpes zoster**: Combined antiviral therapy + prophylactic protocol in elderly and immunocompromised patients at high risk for post-herpetic neuralgia.

- **Military medicine**: Field-deployable prophylaxis kit (lyophilized components with auto-injectors) for combat-related peripheral nerve injuries, administered by medics within the golden hour.

### 4.4 Addressing the Opioid Crisis

Preventing chronic pain eliminates the primary indication for long-term opioid therapy. While patients would continue to receive appropriate acute pain management (including opioids where indicated), the transition to chronic opioid use — with its attendant risks of tolerance, dependence, opioid-induced hyperalgesia, and diversion — would be prevented at its source. Even partial prevention — protecting 50–70% of at-risk patients from developing chronic pain — would substantially reduce the chronic pain population that currently drives opioid demand. This approach addresses the opioid crisis not through restriction or substitution, but through primary prevention of the condition that necessitates long-term opioid therapy.

### 4.5 Limitations and Mitigation Strategies

| Limitation | Mitigation |
|------------|------------|
| Four interventions may be clinically impractical and costly | Identify the minimum sufficient subset (Aim 5, Phase 2–3); Rbfox1 + MMP-9 inhibition may be sufficient; phase out redundant interventions |
| Preclinical rodent models imperfectly predict human efficacy | Validate in multiple models (SNI, CCI, incisional pain) and multiple species (mouse, rat); include a clinically realistic "treat after pain assessment" scenario (Aim 5, Phase 6) |
| Genetic heterogeneity in human populations may reduce efficacy | Pharmacogenomic analysis of target genes; biomarker-based patient stratification in clinical trials |
| Long-term safety of AAV gene therapy and ASOs in pain indications is uncertain | Extend safety follow-up to 12 months in rodents; include comprehensive cognitive, motor, and systemic toxicity battery; for AAV, include integration site analysis |
| Cost of multi-drug prophylaxis may limit adoption in resource-constrained settings | Health economic analysis (cost per quality-adjusted life year gained); identify highest-risk patients for targeted prophylaxis (screening tools based on preoperative pain, anxiety, genetic risk) |
| Sex differences in pain mechanisms may require sex-specific protocols | All studies powered for sex-disaggregated analysis; develop sex-specific dosing algorithms based on observed differences in therapeutic windows (e.g., Rbfox1 degradation kinetics, HIF1α activation magnitude) |
| Regulatory path for combination prophylaxis is complex | Early engagement with FDA via INTERACT/Pre-IND meetings; leverage 505(b)(2) pathway where components are already approved; propose factorial or adaptive trial designs |
| Transient MMP-9 inhibition may still impair cognitive function in susceptible individuals | Direct cognitive testing in animals (Aim 3); compare systemic vs. cell-type-specific (PV⁺ neuron) TIMP-1 delivery; establish no-observed-adverse-effect level for duration of MMP-9 inhibition |

### 4.6 Broader Implications

The time-staggered prophylactic paradigm developed here extends beyond neuropathic pain to any condition driven by injury-induced transcriptional reprogramming, where a transient intervention during the critical post-injury window could prevent chronic pathology without the toxicities of sustained target modulation. Conditions where this paradigm could be applied include:

- **Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD)**: Similar molecular cascades — TNF-α-driven NF-κB activation, HIF1α-mediated transcriptional reprogramming, MMP-9-mediated extracellular matrix remodeling — occur in fear-processing circuits (amygdala, prefrontal cortex, hippocampus) following severe psychological trauma. Transient intervention during the "fear memory consolidation window" could prevent chronic PTSD.

- **Post-stroke epilepsy**: Epileptogenesis after stroke, traumatic brain injury, or status epilepticus follows a defined latent period of days to months during which molecular cascades (including MMP-9-mediated PNN degradation around PV⁺ interneurons in the cortex) convert a normal brain into an epileptic one. Prophylactic MMP-9 inhibition during this window could prevent the development of spontaneous recurrent seizures.

- **Post-traumatic osteoarthritis**: Joint injury initiates a cascade of TNF-α, HIF1α-driven MMP-13 expression, and chondrocyte apoptosis that culminates in irreversible cartilage degeneration. Transient intervention during the acute post-injury phase could prevent or delay osteoarthritis development.

- **Fibrosis after myocardial infarction**: The early post-infarction inflammatory phase (TNF-α, HIF1α-driven TGF-β) determines the extent of subsequent cardiac fibrosis. Transient immunomodulation during this window could reduce pathological remodeling without impairing wound healing.

The splice-switching ASO approach validated in Aim 1 could open an entirely new class of pain therapeutics targeting RNA processing — distinct from the ion channel modulators, GPCR ligands, and enzyme inhibitors that have dominated pain pharmacology. With the regulatory and clinical success of nusinersen for spinal muscular atrophy and the emerging ASO pipeline for neurodegenerative diseases, the pain field is poised to enter the RNA-targeted therapy era.
