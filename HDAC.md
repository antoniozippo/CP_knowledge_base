# Involvement of Histone Deacetylases (HDACs) in Neuropathic Pain

Histone deacetylases (HDACs) are a family of enzymes that remove acetyl groups from lysine residues on histone tails, promoting chromatin compaction and transcriptional repression. Alongside histone acetyltransferases (HATs), they govern the epigenetic landscape that controls gene expression in neurons, glial cells, and immune cells. In neuropathic pain, specific HDAC isoforms are dysregulated in the dorsal root ganglia (DRG), spinal dorsal horn, and pain‑related brain regions, where they suppress anti‑nociceptive genes—most notably those involved in GABAergic inhibition—or, conversely, restrain pro‑nociceptive genes whose de‑repression leads to pain. Pharmacological and genetic inhibition of HDAC1, HDAC2, HDAC3, HDAC4, and HDAC6 has been shown to alleviate mechanical allodynia, thermal hyperalgesia, and neuroinflammation in multiple preclinical models, positioning HDACs as promising epigenetic targets for chronic pain.

### 🧬 The HDAC Family: Classification, Function, and Expression

The 18 human HDACs are divided into four classes based on their homology to yeast enzymes and their catalytic mechanisms. Those most relevant to neuropathic pain belong to Class I and Class II.

- **Class I HDACs (HDAC1, HDAC2, HDAC3, HDAC8)** : Nuclear enzymes that function primarily as transcriptional corepressors within multiprotein complexes (Sin3, NuRD, CoREST). They deacetylate histones H3 and H4, leading to gene silencing.
    - **HDAC1 & HDAC2**: Often function redundantly; highly expressed in the CNS.
    - **HDAC3**: Distinct from HDAC1/2; implicated in spinal sensitisation and GABAergic gene suppression.
- **Class II HDACs**:
    - **Class IIa (HDAC4, HDAC5, HDAC7, HDAC9)** : Shuttle between the nucleus and cytoplasm; HDAC4 is strongly linked to pain.
    - **Class IIb (HDAC6, HDAC10)** : Primarily cytoplasmic; HDAC6 deacetylates non‑histone substrates (α‑tubulin, HSP90, cortactin) and is a major regulator of axonal transport, mitochondrial dynamics, and immune responses.
- **Class III (Sirtuins, SIRT1–7)** : NAD⁺‑dependent deacetylases; generally neuroprotective and anti‑inflammatory. SIRT1 activation alleviates pain.
- **Class IV (HDAC11)** : The sole member; functions in immune cells.

### 🔬 Mechanisms of HDAC Dysregulation in Neuropathic Pain

Multiple HDACs exhibit altered expression or activity in the nociceptive pathway after injury, and their target genes provide a direct mechanistic link to core pain processes.

#### 1. HDAC‑Mediated Suppression of GABAergic Inhibition

This is one of the most prominent and consistent mechanisms by which HDACs promote pain.

- **HDAC3, HDAC4, HDAC6 in the spinal dorsal horn**: After chronic constriction injury (CCI), the mRNA and protein levels of HDAC3, HDAC4, and HDAC6 are significantly elevated in the ipsilateral spinal dorsal horn. These HDACs bind to the promoters of *Gad1* and *Gad2* (encoding the GABA‑synthesising enzymes GAD67 and GAD65) and the *Gabra2* and *Gabra3* (GABA<sub>A</sub> receptor subunits), deacetylating histones H3 and H4 and thereby silencing their expression. The resulting loss of GABAergic tone directly contributes to the disinhibition that underlies mechanical allodynia.
- **HDAC1 and HDAC2 in inhibitory neurons**: HDAC1 and HDAC2 suppress the expression of genes critical for inhibitory neurotransmission, including *Slc32a1* (VIAAT/VGAT, the vesicular GABA/glycine transporter) and various GABA<sub>A</sub> receptor subunits. Their inhibition restores inhibitory synapse function.
- **Functional validation**: Intrathecal injection of the pan‑HDAC inhibitor **suberoylanilide hydroxamic acid (SAHA, vorinostat)** or the HDAC3‑selective inhibitor **RGFP966** reverses the CCI‑induced downregulation of GAD65 and GABA, increases spinal GABA content, and alleviates mechanical allodynia and thermal hyperalgesia. Conversely, blocking GABA<sub>A</sub> receptors partially reverses the analgesic effect of HDAC inhibitors, confirming that restoration of GABAergic transmission is a key mechanism.

#### 2. HDAC2‑Mediated Restraint of the Calcium Channel Subunit α<sub>2</sub>δ‑1

While some HDACs are pronociceptive, HDAC2 in primary sensory neurons exerts a tonic anti‑nociceptive effect that is lost after nerve injury.

- **Constitutive restraint**: In healthy DRG neurons, HDAC2 binds to the promoter of *Cacna2d1* (encoding the α<sub>2</sub>δ‑1 subunit of voltage‑gated calcium channels) and maintains a repressive chromatin state, keeping α<sub>2</sub>δ‑1 expression low.
- **Injury‑induced de‑repression**: After peripheral nerve injury, **casein kinase 2 (CK2)** phosphorylates HDAC2 at Ser<sup>394</sup>. This phosphorylation promotes HDAC2 nuclear export, relieving the transcriptional repression of *Cacna2d1*. The resulting upregulation of α<sub>2</sub>δ‑1 enhances calcium currents at primary afferent synapses, facilitating neurotransmitter release and central sensitisation. This mechanism is a major driver of gabapentin‑sensitive pain.
- **Therapeutic implication**: Preventing HDAC2 phosphorylation or nuclear export would maintain α<sub>2</sub>δ‑1 repression and is a distinct strategy from using HDAC inhibitors, which could paradoxically worsen pain by inhibiting the protective HDAC2 pool.

#### 3. HDAC4 and Spinal Synaptic Plasticity

HDAC4 is specifically upregulated in the spinal dorsal horn after nerve injury and contributes to central sensitisation.

- **GABAergic suppression**: As noted, HDAC4 represses GAD65 and GABA expression.
- **Enhancement of glutamatergic transmission**: HDAC4 can also promote the transcription of genes involved in excitatory synaptic plasticity, such as *Grin2b* (GluN2B NMDA receptor subunit), through indirect mechanisms, enhancing NMDA receptor‑mediated currents.
- **Regulation by microRNAs**: Downregulation of miR‑206, which targets HDAC4, contributes to HDAC4 upregulation after CCI. Restoring miR‑206 reduces HDAC4, restores GABA synthesis, and alleviates pain.

#### 4. HDAC6: Cytoskeletal Dynamics, Axonal Transport, and Neuroinflammation

Unlike nuclear‑focused HDACs, HDAC6 operates primarily in the cytoplasm and is a unique therapeutic target.

- **Substrates and pain relevance**:
    - **α‑tubulin**: HDAC6 deacetylates α‑tubulin, destabilising microtubules. In the damaged axon, increased HDAC6 activity impairs axonal transport of mitochondria, synaptic vesicles, and signalling endosomes, contributing to axonal dysfunction and neuropathic pain.
    - **HSP90**: Deacetylation of HSP90 by HDAC6 modulates its chaperone activity, which controls the stability of client proteins including RIPK1 and RIPK3, key mediators of necroptosis and inflammation.
    - **Cortactin**: Regulates actin dynamics and immune cell migration.
- **Peripheral and central roles**:
    - **Peripheral nerve**: HDAC6 inhibition with **ACY‑1083** or **tubastatin A** ameliorates sensory hypersensitivity in models of postoperative, inflammatory, and chemotherapy‑induced neuropathic pain. These inhibitors restore microtubule acetylation, improve mitochondrial transport, and reduce macrophage infiltration and pro‑inflammatory cytokine levels in the injured nerve.
    - **Spinal cord**: Spinal HDAC6 upregulation contributes to microglial activation and pro‑inflammatory cytokine production. Intrathecal HDAC6 inhibitors reduce microgliosis, suppress TNF‑α and IL‑1β, and attenuate mechanical allodynia.
- **Immunomodulatory effects**: HDAC6 inhibitors shift microglia from a pro‑inflammatory M1 to an anti‑inflammatory M2 phenotype and enhance regulatory T‑cell (Treg) function, promoting resolution of neuroinflammation.

#### 5. Class III HDACs (Sirtuins) and Pain Resolution

In contrast to the generally pronociceptive roles of Class I and IIb HDACs, the sirtuin SIRT1 is predominantly neuroprotective and analgesic.

- **SIRT1 activation**: SIRT1 deacetylates NF‑κB, FOXO, and p53, reducing oxidative stress, inflammation, and apoptosis. Pharmacological SIRT1 activators (e.g., resveratrol, SRT1720) or genetic overexpression of SIRT1 in the spinal cord or DRG attenuates neuropathic pain in CCI, SNI, and diabetic neuropathy models by suppressing neuroinflammation and restoring mitochondrial function.

#### 6. Epigenetic Crosstalk and Histone Modification Landscape

Nerve injury induces a global shift in the histone modification landscape in pain pathways.

- **Reduced H3 and H4 acetylation**: In the spinal dorsal horn after CCI, global histone H3 and H4 acetylation levels are decreased, consistent with HDAC activation. HDAC inhibitors restore acetylation and open chromatin at anti‑nociceptive gene loci.
- **Increased H3K9 methylation**: The repressive mark H3K9me2, deposited by G9a methyltransferase, is also increased at GABAergic gene promoters. Combined HDAC and G9a inhibition may provide greater reversal of gene silencing.
- **BET bromodomain proteins**: Acetylated histones are read by BET (bromodomain and extra‑terminal) proteins such as BRD4, which can recruit transcriptional activators. BET inhibitors (e.g., JQ1) have shown analgesic effects by suppressing pro‑nociceptive gene transcription, particularly of *Scn9a* (Nav1.7) and *Scn10a* (Nav1.8) in DRG neurons.

### 🔬 Preclinical Evidence of Modulation

A wide range of HDAC inhibitors, from pan‑inhibitors to isoform‑selective compounds, have been tested in neuropathic pain models.

#### Pan‑HDAC Inhibitors
- **Suberoylanilide hydroxamic acid (SAHA, vorinostat)** : FDA‑approved for cutaneous T‑cell lymphoma. Intrathecal or systemic SAHA administration significantly attenuates mechanical allodynia and thermal hyperalgesia in CCI and SNL models. It restores spinal histone acetylation, reverses GAD65 and GABA downregulation, reduces microglial and astrocytic activation, and suppresses NF‑κB‑driven inflammation.
- **Trichostatin A (TSA)** : A classic, potent pan‑HDAC inhibitor. Intrathecal TSA reduces pain behaviours in neuropathic and inflammatory pain models, restores GABAergic gene expression, and enhances acetylation at the promoters of *Gad1* and *Gabra2*.
- **Valproic acid**: An anticonvulsant and mood stabiliser with broad HDAC inhibitory activity. Chronic valproate treatment increases histone acetylation in the spinal cord and alleviates neuropathic pain in animal models.

#### Class I‑Selective Inhibitors
- **RGFP966 (HDAC3‑selective)** : Intrathecal RGFP966 reverses CCI‑induced GAD65/GABA loss, reduces glial activation, and alleviates mechanical and thermal hypersensitivity. Its selectivity avoids the toxicity of pan‑inhibition.
- **Entinostat (MS‑275, HDAC1/3‑selective)** : An orally bioavailable benzamide HDAC inhibitor that has shown efficacy in preclinical neuropathic pain models by restoring GABAergic tone and reducing inflammation.

#### HDAC6‑Selective Inhibitors
- **ACY‑1083**: A peripherally restricted HDAC6 inhibitor. Local treatment at the nerve injury site or systemic administration ameliorates mechanical and cold hypersensitivity in post‑surgical, inflammatory, and CIPN models. It restores axonal transport, reduces macrophage infiltration, and promotes nerve repair.
- **Tubastatin A**: A widely used HDAC6 inhibitor with CNS penetration. Intrathecal injection alleviates allodynia, suppresses spinal microgliosis, and reduces TNF‑α and IL‑1β levels.
- **Ricolinostat (ACY‑1215)** : An oral HDAC6 inhibitor in clinical trials for cancer; has shown analgesic effects in preclinical models.

#### Genetic Approaches
- **HDAC4 conditional knockout**: Deletion of *Hdac4* in spinal neurons reduces mechanical allodynia after CCI and prevents the downregulation of GABAergic markers.
- **HDAC2 overexpression or nuclear trapping**: Genetic strategies that prevent HDAC2 nuclear export maintain *Cacna2d1* repression and protect against neuropathic pain development.
- **HDAC6 knockout**: *Hdac6*‑/‑ mice show reduced mechanical hypersensitivity in inflammatory and neuropathic pain models, associated with preserved microtubule integrity and reduced peripheral inflammation.

#### BET Bromodomain Inhibitors
- **JQ1**: A selective BET bromodomain inhibitor that displaces BRD4 from acetylated chromatin. JQ1 reduces the expression of Nav1.7 and Nav1.8 in DRG neurons, inhibits microglial activation, and alleviates neuropathic pain in SNI and CCI models.

### 🩺 Clinical Implications and Therapeutic Opportunities

- **Drug repurposing with pan‑HDAC inhibitors**: Vorinostat, romidepsin, and panobinostat are FDA‑approved for haematological malignancies. They have well‑characterised pharmacokinetics and toxicity profiles. Small clinical studies or case reports have noted pain relief in some cancer patients treated with these drugs, and prospective trials for chronic pain could be justified, though their side‑effect profile limits chronic use.
- **Class I‑ and isoform‑selective inhibitors for chronic pain**: Selective HDAC3 inhibitors (RGFP966‑class) or HDAC1/3 inhibitors (entinostat) could avoid the bone marrow suppression, gastrointestinal toxicity, and neurotoxicity associated with pan‑HDAC inhibition, making them more suitable for chronic, non‑malignant pain.
- **Peripherally restricted HDAC6 inhibitors**: HDAC6 inhibitors like ACY‑1083, with limited CNS exposure, could be developed for peripheral neuropathies (e.g., diabetic neuropathy, CIPN) without central side effects.
- **Epigenetic biomarker‑guided therapy**: Histone acetylation patterns in circulating immune cells or cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) could serve as biomarkers of central HDAC activity and guide patient selection for HDAC inhibitor therapy.
- **Combining HDAC inhibitors with other analgesics**: Preclinical data suggest synergy between HDAC inhibitors and GABAergic agents (e.g., gabapentinoids), anti‑inflammatory drugs, or even opioid therapy to enhance analgesia and reduce tolerance.

### ⚠️ A Note of Caution and Complexity

- **Context‑dependent roles of HDACs**: As exemplified by HDAC2 in DRG neurons, not all HDACs are pronociceptive. Some function to restrain pain genes, and broad‑spectrum HDAC inhibition could inadvertently de‑repress these genes, potentially exacerbating pain. Isoform‑selective targeting is highly desirable.
- **Physiological requirements**: HDACs are essential for haematopoiesis, cardiac function, and neurodevelopment. Pan‑HDAC inhibitors carry significant risks of myelosuppression, cardiotoxicity, gastrointestinal toxicity, and neurotoxicity, limiting their chronic use for pain. Isoform‑selective or peripherally restricted drugs may mitigate these risks.
- **Dual role of HDAC6 in regeneration**: While acute HDAC6 inhibition can relieve pain, HDAC6 is also required for axonal regeneration and remyelination. The timing of HDAC6 inhibitor administration relative to injury is critical: early inhibition may block inflammation and pain, but prolonged, complete blockade could impair long‑term recovery.
- **Epigenetic memory and reversibility**: A potential advantage of HDAC inhibitor therapy is the ability to reverse maladaptive epigenetic marks, potentially producing disease‑modifying effects that outlast the drug’s presence. However, this epigenetic reprogramming could also lead to persistent off‑target effects if critical genes are inadvertently altered.
- **Sex differences**: Preclinical pain studies using HDAC inhibitors have largely been performed in male animals; sex‑specific effects on histone acetylation and HDAC expression are understudied but may influence therapeutic responses.
- **Interactions with other pain pathways**:
    - **BDNF/TrkB**: HDAC inhibitors restore GABAergic inhibition, counteracting the disinhibition driven by BDNF‑TrkB‑KCC2 signalling.
    - **TNF‑α, NF‑κB**: SIRT1 deacetylates NF‑κB, inhibiting its activity; pan‑HDAC inhibitors also reduce NF‑κB‑driven inflammation. Conversely, HDAC3 can enhance NF‑κB activity.
    - **NLRP3 inflammasome**: HDAC6 inhibition reduces NLRP3 activation by stabilising microtubules and facilitating autophagic clearance of damaged mitochondria, linking epigenetic regulation to inflammasome‑mediated neuroinflammation.
    - **α<sub>2</sub>δ‑1 and gabapentinoids**: The HDAC2‑Cacna2d1 axis directly intersects with the gabapentinoid target, suggesting that patients with elevated α<sub>2</sub>δ‑1 due to HDAC2 dysfunction may be especially responsive to gabapentinoid therapy.
    - **Perineuronal nets (PNNs)** : HDAC inhibitors can increase the expression of aggrecan and other PNN components by promoting histone acetylation, potentially restoring PNN integrity around PV interneurons and reinforcing inhibition.
    - **JAK/STAT3**: HDACs can deacetylate STAT3, modulating its transcriptional activity. SIRT1 deacetylates and inhibits STAT3, while HDAC3 can promote STAT3 activation, linking epigenetic regulation to cytokine signalling.
