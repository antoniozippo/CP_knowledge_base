# Involvement of CCL2/CCR2 in Neuropathic Pain

The C-C motif chemokine ligand 2 (CCL2), also known as monocyte chemoattractant protein‑1 (MCP‑1), and its primary receptor, C-C chemokine receptor type 2 (CCR2), form a signalling axis that is now recognised as a master orchestrator of neuroimmune crosstalk in chronic pain. Accumulating evidence implicates the CCL2/CCR2 axis in the pathogenesis of neuropathic pain at three hierarchical levels: peripheral sensitisation, spinal central sensitisation, and supraspinal network modulation, where it also drives the comorbidity of pain with anxiety and cognitive impairment[reference:0]. Beyond its classical role as a monocyte chemoattractant, CCL2 acts directly on nociceptors and spinal neurons to enhance excitability, promotes glial activation, and facilitates excitatory synaptic transmission, making the CCL2/CCR2 pathway a high‑priority target for the development of non‑opioid analgesics.

## 🧬 The CCL2/CCR2 System: Ligand, Receptor, and Physiological Roles

- **Ligand (CCL2/MCP‑1)** : CCL2 is a small (8–14 kDa) chemokine of the CC subfamily. Under physiological conditions, it is expressed at low levels in the nervous system, but is massively upregulated after nerve injury in multiple cell types, including injured primary sensory neurons, spinal astrocytes, and stromal cells within the damaged nerve.
- **Receptor (CCR2)** : CCR2 is a G‑protein‑coupled receptor (Gi/o‑coupled) that exists in two splice variants: CCR2A and CCR2B. CCR2B is the predominant isoform and is expressed on:
    - Peripheral and central nociceptive neurons (DRG, spinal cord)
    - Microglia and astrocytes in the spinal cord and brain
    - Monocytes/macrophages infiltrating the injured nerve and DRG
    - Neurons in supraspinal pain‑processing regions (ACC, CeA, NAc, hippocampus)
- **Physiological Functions**: In health, CCL2/CCR2 signalling contributes to immune surveillance, leukocyte trafficking, and neuronal–glial communication. It also participates in synaptic plasticity and is emerging as a key mediator of neuron–microglia crosstalk in the CNS.

## 🔬 Mechanisms of CCL2/CCR2 Dysregulation in Neuropathic Pain

Following peripheral nerve injury, the CCL2/CCR2 axis is profoundly dysregulated across multiple anatomical compartments, contributing to pain through a combination of direct neuronal sensitisation, glial activation, and immune cell recruitment.

### 1. Peripheral Sensitisation

At the level of the primary nociceptor and the injured nerve, CCL2 drives peripheral sensitisation through several mechanisms:

- **Modulation of ion channels**: CCL2, via CCR2 on nociceptor terminals, enhances the activity of TRPV1, Nav1.8, and HCN2 channels, increasing neuronal excitability and lowering the action potential threshold[reference:1].
- **Enhanced CGRP release and calcium‑dependent exocytosis**: CCL2/CCR2 signalling promotes the release of calcitonin gene‑related peptide (CGRP) and facilitates calcium‑dependent vesicle exocytosis, efficiently transmitting pain signals from the periphery to the CNS[reference:2].
- **Direct excitation of nociceptors**: CCL2 directly depolarises DRG neurons and evokes calcium transients through CCR2, demonstrating that it can act as an independent pain mediator rather than merely as an immune chemoattractant.
- **Monocyte/macrophage recruitment into the injured nerve**: CCL2 expression is markedly increased in the damaged sciatic nerve after chronic constriction injury (CCI), predominantly from stromal cells rather than immune cells. This chemokine gradient drives the infiltration of CCR2‑expressing monocytes/macrophages into the lesion, which correlates with pain behaviour. Intraneural microinjection of CCL2 alone is sufficient to trigger long‑lasting pain and local monocyte/macrophage recruitment[reference:3].

### 2. Spinal Cord Central Sensitisation

In the spinal dorsal horn, CCL2/CCR2 signalling contributes to the amplification and persistence of nociceptive transmission through multiple parallel pathways:

- **NMDAR‑dependent plasticity**: CCL2, acting on CCR2‑expressing excitatory neurons in spinal lamina IIo, enhances glutamate release presynaptically and, postsynaptically, increases NMDA receptor (GluN2B) currents via the ERK‑GluN2B pathway, thereby facilitating long‑term potentiation and central sensitisation[reference:4][reference:5].
- **Microglial activation**: CCL2 is released from the central terminals of injured primary afferent neurons and from astrocytes, binding to CCR2 on spinal microglia. This triggers microglial activation, leading to the release of pronociceptive mediators including brain‑derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF), tumour necrosis factor‑α (TNF‑α), and interleukin‑1β (IL‑1β)[reference:6][reference:7].
- **GABAergic disinhibition**: Microglial‑derived BDNF, released downstream of CCL2/CCR2 signalling, acts on neuronal TrkB receptors to downregulate the potassium‑chloride cotransporter KCC2, collapsing the chloride gradient and converting GABAergic inhibition into excitation—a core mechanism of mechanical allodynia[reference:8][reference:9].
- **Opioid receptor desensitisation**: CCL2/CCR2 signalling contributes to the desensitisation of opioid receptors in the spinal cord, providing a mechanistic link between neuroinflammation and reduced opioid responsiveness in chronic pain states[reference:10].
- **Neuronal–astrocytic positive‑feedback loop**: SNL increases glutamate release, activating neuronal NMDARs and leading to reduced expression of miR‑186‑5p. This disinhibition results in CXCL13 upregulation in spinal neurons, which activates astrocytes to release CCL2 and CCL7. These astrocytic mediators further maintain microglial activation, creating a self‑reinforcing loop that enhances and prolongs neuropathic pain[reference:11].

### 3. Supraspinal Central Networks

The role of CCL2/CCR2 extends beyond the spinal cord to brain regions involved in pain processing and its affective/motivational comorbidities:

- **Anterior cingulate cortex (ACC)** : CCL2 acts on CCR2‑expressing excitatory neurons in the ACC, upregulating NR2B‑containing NMDA receptors, which participates in the regulation of chronic pain and pain‑related anxiety‑like behaviours[reference:12].
- **Central amygdala (CeA) and nucleus accumbens (NAc)** : CCL2/CCR2 signalling in these limbic structures mediates the emotional and motivational dimensions of chronic pain.
- **Hippocampus**: CCL2/CCR2 signalling contributes to pain‑associated cognitive impairment, linking chronic neuropathic pain to deficits in learning and memory[reference:13][reference:14].
- **Descending facilitation**: The CCL2/CCR2 axis increases pain sensitisation through the descending facilitation system, including the periaqueductal gray (PAG) and rostral ventromedial medulla (RVM), key components of the brainstem pain‑modulatory network[reference:15][reference:16].

## 🔬 Preclinical Evidence of Modulation

Pharmacological, genetic, and natural product‑based interventions targeting the CCL2/CCR2 pathway consistently reduce neuropathic pain in multiple preclinical models.

### Pharmacological CCR2 Antagonists

- **CCR2 antagonists**: Blockade of CCR2 with selective antagonists effectively reduces hypersensitivity to thermal and mechanical stimuli in chronic pain states, including diabetic neuropathy. Simultaneous blockade of both CCR2 and CCR5 by dual antagonists produces enhanced analgesia[reference:17].
- **CCR2‑selective pepducin PP101**: A cell‑penetrating allosteric modulator targeting intracellular CCR2. Chronic intrathecal administration of PP101 was effective in alleviating neuropathic and bone cancer pain, attenuating the altered phenotype of DRG neurons and reducing neuroinflammation. Importantly, PP101 did not cause deleterious tumour progression or adverse behavioural effects[reference:18].
- **Spinal CCL2 pronociceptive action**: The pronociceptive effect of CCL2 in the spinal cord is no longer effective in CCR2 receptor antagonist‑treated rats, confirming CCR2 as the primary receptor mediating spinal CCL2‑induced pain facilitation[reference:19].

### Genetic Approaches

- **CCL2 knockout mice**: Deletion of CCL2 reduces macrophage expansion in the DRG and attenuates neuropathic pain in both male and female mice, demonstrating a non‑sexually‑dimorphic role for CCL2 in macrophage‑mediated pain initiation[reference:20].
- **Stromal cell‑derived CCL2 deletion**: Using irradiated bone‑marrow chimeric mice, specific deletion of CCL2 in stromal cells, but not immune cells, abolished monocyte/macrophage recruitment to the injured nerve and prevented pain hypersensitivity, demonstrating that stromal cells are the critical source of pathogenic CCL2[reference:21].

### Electroacupuncture and Natural Products

- **Electroacupuncture (EA)** : EA at ST36 and GB34 significantly attenuated mechanical allodynia and thermal hyperalgesia in L5 spinal nerve ligation (SNL) rats, accompanied by reductions in CCL2, CCR2, microglial marker Iba‑1, and pro‑inflammatory cytokines. Crucially, intrathecal administration of recombinant CCL2 completely abolished EA's analgesic effects, establishing the causal necessity of CCL2/CCR2 signalling in EA‑mediated analgesia[reference:22].
- **Polyphenolic extracts**: Daily administration of polyphenolic extracts from grape stalks and coffee prevented both reflexive (mechanical allodynia, thermal hyperalgesia) and non‑reflexive chronic neuropathic pain in spinal cord‑injured female mice. This was associated with reduced supraspinal gliosis and regulation of CCL2/CCR2 and CX3CL1/CX3CR1 signalling in key pain‑processing regions including the PAG, RVM, ACC, and amygdala[reference:23].
- **Other CCR2‑modulating natural compounds**: Various natural products have been shown to inhibit the CCL2/CCR2 axis and provide analgesia, including polyphenols, flavonoids, and plant sterols.

### Spinal Cord Injury‑Induced Central Neuropathic Pain

Spinal cord hemisection injury leads to the development of mechanical and cold allodynia, accompanied by upregulation of CCL2/CCR2 mRNA both above and below the lesion level. This association suggests that CCL2/CCR2 plays an important role in the development of central neuropathic pain after SCI, extending its relevance beyond peripheral neuropathic pain[reference:24].

## 🩺 Clinical Implications and Therapeutic Opportunities

- **CCR2 antagonists as non‑opioid analgesics**: Targeting the CCL2/CCR2 chemokine axis is effective at relieving pain in rodent models of inflammatory and neuropathic pain, representing a promising avenue for the development of non‑opioid analgesics[reference:25].
- **Pepducins and allosteric modulators**: Intracellular allosteric modulators like PP101 offer a novel pharmacological strategy to overcome the poor selectivity of traditional orthosteric CCR2 antagonists. PP101 targets neuroimmune crosstalk through allosteric inhibition of CCR2 and could represent an effective and safe avenue for chronic pain management[reference:26].
- **Clinical translation challenges**: Despite robust preclinical data, clinical trials targeting CCR2 for inflammatory conditions and painful neuropathies have failed to meet expectations and have all been discontinued due to lack of efficacy. This highlights a significant translational gap between preclinical models and human pain conditions[reference:27].
- **Dual CCR2/CCR5 antagonism**: Simultaneous blockade of both CCR2 and CCR5 has shown enhanced efficacy in preclinical models, suggesting that targeting multiple chemokine receptors may overcome the limitations of selective CCR2 monotherapy[reference:28].
- **Biomarker potential**: Elevated CCL2 levels in cerebrospinal fluid, serum, or wound exudate may serve as a biomarker for pain severity, neuroinflammation, and patient stratification in future clinical trials.
- **Electroacupuncture and non‑pharmacological interventions**: The finding that electroacupuncture produces analgesia through CCL2/CCR2 downregulation opens avenues for non‑pharmacological modulation of chemokine signalling in chronic pain management.

## ⚠️ A Note of Caution and Complexity

- **Translational failures**: The disconnect between robust preclinical efficacy and negative clinical trials for CCR2 antagonists is a major concern. Potential reasons include differences in human versus rodent chemokine biology, redundancy among chemokine systems, and inadequate target engagement in human patients.
- **Chemokine redundancy**: CCL2 is not the only ligand for CCR2. CCL7, CCL8, CCL11, CCL12, and CCL13 also bind to CCR2, and these ligands may compensate when CCL2 is selectively blocked. This redundancy necessitates targeting the receptor (CCR2) rather than individual ligands, or employing dual receptor blockade strategies.
- **Physiological roles of CCR2**: CCR2 is essential for monocyte trafficking, immune surveillance, and wound healing. Chronic systemic CCR2 blockade could impair host defence, increase infection risk, and delay tissue repair. Localised delivery (intrathecal) or peripherally restricted agents may mitigate these concerns.
- **Sex differences**: While the contribution of CCL2‑driven macrophage infiltration to neuropathic pain appears non‑sexually‑dimorphic in some studies, other reports indicate sex‑dependent regulation of chemokine signalling. For example, SCI‑induced neuropathic pain in female mice was prevented by polyphenolic extracts that modulated CCL2/CCR2 and CX3CL1/CX3CR1 signalling, and sex‑specific therapeutic strategies may be required[reference:29][reference:30].
- **Context‑dependent roles**: CCL2/CCR2 signalling can have protective functions in certain contexts. For example, regulatory T cells require peripheral CCL2‑CCR2 signalling to facilitate the resolution of medication overuse headache‑related behavioural sensitisation, indicating a dual role that depends on the cellular context and pain aetiology.
- **Interactions with Other Pain Pathways**: The CCL2/CCR2 axis is deeply integrated with the broader neuroimmune pain network:
    - **BDNF/TrkB**: CCL2/CCR2‑activated microglia release BDNF, which downregulates KCC2 and produces GABAergic disinhibition, directly linking chemokine signalling to the canonical disinhibition pathway.
    - **P2X4 receptor**: CCL2/CCR2 and P2X4 receptors converge on microglial BDNF release, representing parallel but potentially synergistic pathways of microglial‑mediated central sensitisation[reference:31].
    - **CX3CL1/CX3CR1**: CCL2/CCR2 and CX3CL1/CX3CR1 are co‑regulated in the spinal cord and supraspinal structures after nerve injury, and polyphenols that modulate both pathways prevent chronic pain[reference:32].
    - **TNF‑α and IL‑1β**: CCL2/CCR2‑activated microglia produce TNF‑α and IL‑18, which activate astrocytes via TNFR and IL‑18R, forming a glial‑mediated inflammatory cascade that amplifies neuroinflammation[reference:33].
    - **NMDA receptor**: CCL2/CCR2 signalling enhances NMDAR‑mediated synaptic plasticity through the ERK‑GluN2B pathway, providing a direct mechanistic link between chemokine signalling and glutamatergic central sensitisation[reference:34].
    - **TRPV1, Nav1.8, HCN2**: CCL2 modulates these ion channels in peripheral nociceptors, directly increasing neuronal excitability and contributing to peripheral sensitisation[reference:35].
    - **Opioid receptors**: CCL2/CCR2 signalling desensitises opioid receptors in the spinal cord, potentially explaining the reduced efficacy of opioids in chronic neuropathic pain states[reference:36].
