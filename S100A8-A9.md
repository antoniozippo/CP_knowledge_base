# Involvement of S100A8 in Neuropathic Pain

S100A8 (also known as MRP8 or calgranulin A) is a low‑molecular‑weight calcium‑binding protein that commonly forms a heterodimer with S100A9 (calgranulin B). The S100A8/A9 complex is a prototypical damage‑associated molecular pattern (DAMP) released from activated myeloid cells, keratinocytes, and stressed neurons. Once liberated, it activates Toll‑like receptor 4 (TLR4) and the receptor for advanced glycation end‑products (RAGE) to drive neuroinflammation, peripheral immune activation, and direct neuronal sensitization—all central processes in neuropathic pain.

### 🧬 S100A8 and Its Receptors in the Pain Pathway

- **DAMP nature**: S100A8 is passively released from necrotic cells and actively secreted by neutrophils, monocytes, and microglia via non‑classical pathways. It functions as an endogenous danger signal (alarmin).
- **Receptors**: The major pain‑relevant receptors for S100A8/A9 are **TLR4** and **RAGE**. TLR4 is expressed on dorsal root ganglion (DRG) neurons, spinal microglia, and astrocytes. RAGE is upregulated in inflamed tissues and spinal cord after nerve injury.
- **Calcium binding**: S100A8 contains two EF‑hand calcium‑binding motifs. Calcium binding induces a conformational change that exposes a hydrophobic surface necessary for receptor engagement, directly linking Ca²⁺ signals to DAMP release.

### 🔬 Mechanisms of S100A8 in Neuropathic Pain

S100A8 contributes to neuropathic pain through multiple interconnected mechanisms, spanning the peripheral nerve, DRG, spinal cord, and brain.

- **Direct Excitation of Nociceptors**: S100A8 directly excites DRG nociceptors. Application of S100A8 to cultured DRG neurons evokes a rapid increase in intracellular Ca²⁺ primarily through TLR4. This direct neuronal response stimulates release of the pro‑algesic chemokine MCP‑1/CCL2 and sensitizes primary afferent terminals. Nociceptor‑specific responses have been confirmed using Pirt‑GCaMP3 mice, where S100A8‑induced Ca²⁺ signals occur predominantly in nociceptor sub‑populations.[reference:0][reference:1][reference:2]

- **TLR4‑Mediated Signaling**: S100A8 binding to TLR4 on DRG neurons, microglia, and astrocytes triggers MyD88‑dependent and TRIF‑dependent pathways, leading to activation of NF‑κB and MAP kinases (JNK, ERK, p38). Downstream consequences include:
    - Upregulation of pro‑inflammatory cytokines (TNF‑α, IL‑1β, IL‑6), which further sensitize nociceptors.
    - Microglial M1 polarization and sustained neuroinflammation in the spinal dorsal horn.
    - Increased expression of pronociceptive chemokines (CCL2, CXCL1).[reference:3][reference:4][reference:5]

- **RAGE‑Mediated Signaling**: S100A8 also signals through RAGE, particularly in chronic inflammatory states. RAGE activation similarly converges on NF‑κB and MAPK pathways, sustaining a feed‑forward loop of inflammation and pain. The dual receptor engagement (TLR4 and RAGE) makes S100A8 a potent and versatile driver of pain chronicity.[reference:6][reference:7]

- **Neutrophil‑Derived S100A8/A9 and Peripheral‑Central Crosstalk**: Following peripheral nerve injury, neutrophils infiltrate the injured nerve and DRG and release massive amounts of S100A8/A9. These alarmins can also enter the spinal cord parenchyma when the blood‑spinal cord barrier (BSCB) is compromised. In the spinal cord, S100A8/A9 activates microglia and astrocytes, creating a neuroinflammatory milieu that amplifies central sensitization.[reference:8][reference:9]

- **Disruption of Ionic Homeostasis and Neuronal Hyperexcitability**: S100A8/A9‑induced signaling, particularly through TLR4/TNF pathways, leads to **downregulation of the Kir4.1 potassium channel** in spinal astrocytes and neurons. Kir4.1 is essential for spatial K⁺ buffering and maintaining the resting membrane potential. Its loss results in impaired K⁺ clearance, extracellular K⁺ accumulation, neuronal depolarization, hyperexcitability, and persistent pain.[reference:10][reference:11]

- **Mitochondrial Dysfunction and Apoptosis**: In spinal cord ischemia‑reperfusion injury models, S100A8 is transcriptionally upregulated by the AP‑1 family member Fra‑1. S100A8 then activates TLR4 pathways that promote neuronal apoptosis and neuroinflammation. Furthermore, sustained S100A8/A9 signaling can directly induce mitochondrial pathology, including mitochondrial Ca²⁺ overload and reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, which contribute to neuronal hyperexcitability and the apoptosis/autophagy dysfunction discussed in a previous section.[reference:12][reference:13]

- **Kv Channel Modulation and Hyperexcitability**: Beyond Kir4.1, S100A8/A9‑TLR4 signaling can alter the function of voltage‑gated Kv channels (Kv1.3, Kv1.5), reducing the delayed rectifier current that normally limits action potential firing. This reduction in Kv current contributes to the hyperexcitable state of nociceptors after injury.

### 🔬 Preclinical Evidence of Modulation

- **Pharmacological Inhibition**:
    - **TLR4 antagonists**: TAK‑242 (resatorvid), a selective TLR4 inhibitor, and LPS‑RS (a TLR4 antagonist) block S100A8‑induced Ca²⁺ responses in DRG neurons and reduce mechanical allodynia in neuropathic pain models.
    - **S100A8/A9 neutralizing antibodies**: Anti‑S100A8/A9 monoclonal antibodies reduce synovitis‑associated pain, decrease inflammatory cytokine levels, and attenuate pain behavior in models of inflammatory and neuropathic pain.[reference:14]
    - **RAGE blockade**: Soluble RAGE or RAGE antagonists partially reduce S100A8‑induced pain and inflammation, especially in chronic pain states with high RAGE expression.
    - **CD47‑blocking antibody**: In spinal cord injury, CD47‑blocking antibodies reduce neutrophil extracellular trap (NET) formation, lower S100A8/A9 production, protect BSCB integrity, and alleviate neuronal death and pain.[reference:15]

- **Genetic Approaches**:
    - **S100A8 shRNA knockdown**: Interference with S100A8 expression using shRNA in the trigeminal ganglion or spinal cord significantly increases pain thresholds, reduces inflammatory factor release (TNF‑α, IL‑1β, IL‑6, CGRP), and inhibits microglial M1 polarization in migraine and neuropathic pain models.[reference:16][reference:17]
    - **TLR4 knockout**: *Tlr4*‑/‑ mice show attenuated or absent responses to S100A8 in DRG neurons and reduced pain behavior in some, though not all, neuropathic pain models.[reference:18]
    - **Fra‑1 knockdown**: Knockdown of Fra‑1, an upstream transcription factor for S100A8, alleviates S100A8‑mediated neuronal apoptosis and inflammatory factor release after spinal cord ischemia‑reperfusion injury.[reference:19]

- **Sex‑Specific Effects**: RNA‑seq analyses have revealed that S100A8 and S100A9 expression may play a sex‑specific role in pain relief, with a more prominent contribution in males. Comparable behavioral outcomes in males and females do not necessarily rely on the same gene expression profiles, highlighting the need for sex‑stratified analysis.[reference:20][reference:21]

### 🩺 Clinical Implications and Therapeutic Opportunities

- **Biomarker Potential**: S100A8/A9 levels in wound exudate, serum, plasma, and cerebrospinal fluid have been correlated with pain intensity in various conditions. For example, S100A8/A9 concentrations in wound exudate from venous leg ulcers show significant associations with neuropathic pain and continuous pain scores. In fibromyalgia, S100A8/A9 is upregulated and may serve as a diagnostic or prognostic biomarker.[reference:22][reference:23][reference:24]

- **Postherpetic Neuralgia (PHN)**: The herpes zoster neuralgia model demonstrates the clinical relevance of S100A8/A9. The protein complex mediates peripheral immune activation, neuroinflammation, and pain sensitization through TLR4/TNF pathways. Targeting S100A8/A9 represents a potential disease‑modifying strategy for PHN, a notoriously difficult‑to‑treat condition.[reference:25][reference:26]

- **Therapeutic Targeting Strategies**:
    - **Monoclonal antibodies against S100A8/A9**: Humanized neutralizing antibodies are in preclinical development. By sequestering extracellular S100A8/A9, they aim to block both TLR4 and RAGE activation.
    - **Small‑molecule TLR4 antagonists**: Drugs like TAK‑242 are well‑characterized and could be repurposed for neuropathic pain, especially via intrathecal delivery to limit systemic immunosuppression.
    - **Small‑molecule RAGE inhibitors**: Azeliragon (TTP488), a RAGE inhibitor that reached Phase III trials for Alzheimer’s disease, could be investigated for pain indications where RAGE signaling predominates.
    - **Inhibition of S100A8/A9 release**: Blocking the non‑classical secretory pathways of S100A8/A9 from activated neutrophils and microglia could reduce the local DAMP load without completely abolishing basal levels.

- **Wound Pain Management**: The strong association between S100A8/A9 levels and pain in chronic wounds suggests that local modulation of S100A8/A9 could offer an objective method to evaluate wound pain status and guide analgesic therapy.[reference:27]

### ⚠️ A Note of Caution and Complexity

- **Host Defence Functions**: S100A8/A9 is not merely a pathological molecule; it is an essential host defence mediator. It exhibits antimicrobial activity by chelating divalent cations (Zn²⁺, Mn²⁺) essential for microbial growth. In neuropathic foot ulcers of diabetic patients, S100A8/A9 levels correlate with wound healing capacity. Systemic, prolonged neutralization of S100A8/A9 could impair innate immunity and wound repair.[reference:28][reference:29]

- **Dual Role in Acute vs. Chronic Pain**: In the acute phase of inflammation, S100A8/A9 can have pro‑resolving roles. Transient activation of neutrophils and release of S100A8 or S100A9 are pro‑resolving in models of CCI, NGF‑induced pain, and CFA‑induced inflammatory pain. Chronic, sustained elevation, however, drives persistent pain. The therapeutic window for S100A8/A9 blockade is likely narrow and condition‑specific.[reference:30]

- **Receptor Redundancy**: S100A8 signals through both TLR4 and RAGE, and the relative contribution of each receptor may vary with pain etiology, tissue compartment, and chronicity. Blocking one receptor may simply shift signaling to the other.

- **Sex Differences**: As noted, S100A8/A9 expression patterns and functional contributions may differ between males and females, which must be considered in the design of clinical trials and therapeutic strategies.

- **Off‑Target Effects**: TLR4 and RAGE are widely expressed and participate in normal immune surveillance, bone metabolism, and synaptic plasticity. Systemic inhibition could lead to immunosuppression, impaired cognition, and altered bone homeostasis. Localized delivery (intrathecal, topical) is highly desirable.

- **Interactions with the Wider Pain Network**:
    - **TNF‑α**: S100A8/A9 drives TNF‑α production via TLR4/NF‑κB, and TNF‑α in turn upregulates S100A8/A9 expression, creating a positive‑feedback loop that amplifies neuroinflammation.
    - **BDNF/TrkB**: S100A8/A9‑activated microglia release BDNF, which downregulates KCC2 and contributes to GABAergic disinhibition. The TLR4‑p38 pathway activated by S100A8 is the same pathway that drives BDNF secretion.
    - **RhoA/ROCK**: TLR4 signaling activated by S100A8/A9 can engage RhoA/ROCK to alter cytoskeletal dynamics, contributing to neuronal plasticity and growth cone collapse in injured nerves.
    - **BBB/BSCB Integrity**: S100A8/A9 promotes neutrophil NETosis and MMP release, which degrade tight junction proteins and compromise barrier integrity, facilitating further immune cell infiltration and DAMP entry into the CNS.
    - **HIF1α/VEGFA**: Hypoxia‑driven HIF1α can upregulate S100A8 expression in microglia, linking metabolic stress to DAMP‑induced pain. VEGFA‑induced vascular permeability facilitates S100A8/A9 extravasation into neural tissues.
    - **Calcium‑Binding Protein and Apoptosis**: As a calcium‑binding protein, S100A8 is both regulated by and regulates intracellular Ca²⁺ dynamics, linking it to the Ca²⁺‑driven apoptosis and autophagy pathways in GABAergic interneurons discussed earlier.
