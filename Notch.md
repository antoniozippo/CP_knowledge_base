# Involvement of Notch Signaling in Neuropathic Pain

Notch signaling is a highly conserved cell–cell communication pathway that governs cell fate, proliferation, and differentiation during development. In the adult nervous system, Notch receptors and their ligands are largely quiescent but become strikingly reactivated in the dorsal root ganglia (DRG), injured peripheral nerves, and spinal dorsal horn following nerve injury. This pathologic reactivation drives a multifaceted pronociceptive program that includes the activation of spinal microglia and astrocytes, the amplification of pro‑inflammatory cytokine cascades, the disruption of inhibitory tone, and the direct sensitization of nociceptive neurons. Pharmacological blockade of Notch signaling with γ‑secretase inhibitors (which prevent the release of the transcriptionally active Notch intracellular domain) or genetic deletion of Notch pathway components consistently reduces mechanical allodynia, thermal hyperalgesia, and neuroinflammatory markers in multiple preclinical models of neuropathic pain. These findings position the Notch pathway as an attractive, albeit complex, therapeutic target for chronic pain management.

## 🧬 The Notch Signaling Pathway: Components and Activation

The canonical Notch pathway is a juxtacrine signaling system that requires direct cell–cell contact. Its core components are:

*   **Receptors**: Four single‑pass transmembrane receptors (Notch1–4) that exist as heterodimers. The extracellular domain contains epidermal growth factor (EGF)‑like repeats that mediate ligand binding; the intracellular domain (NICD) harbors a RAM domain, ankyrin repeats, a transactivation domain, and a PEST sequence that regulates protein stability. Notch1 and Notch3 are the predominant isoforms implicated in neuropathic pain.
*   **Ligands**: Five transmembrane ligands of the Delta/Serrate/LAG‑2 (DSL) family:
    *   **Delta‑like** (Dll1, Dll3, Dll4)
    *   **Jagged** (Jagged1, Jagged2)
    *   Jagged1 and Dll1 are upregulated in DRG neurons and spinal glia after nerve injury and are the primary pain‑relevant ligands.
*   **Activation cascade**:
    1.  A ligand on a neighboring cell engages the Notch receptor, exerting a mechanical pulling force that exposes a cleavage site for **ADAM10/TACE** metalloproteases. This **S2 cleavage** sheds the extracellular domain.
    2.  The remaining membrane‑tethered fragment is then cleaved by the **γ‑secretase complex** (a multi‑subunit protease containing presenilin, nicastrin, PEN‑2, and APH‑1) at the **S3 site**, releasing the Notch intracellular domain (NICD).
    3.  NICD translocates to the nucleus, where it binds the transcription factor **CSL** (CBF1/RBP‑Jκ). This recruits co‑activators of the **Mastermind‑like (MAML)** family, displacing co‑repressors and initiating the transcription of target genes.
*   **Major target genes**: The best‑characterized are the **Hes** (Hes1, Hes5) and **Hey** (Hey1, Hey2) families of basic helix‑loop‑helix (bHLH) transcriptional repressors. These factors regulate genes involved in cell cycle, survival, inflammation, and glial reactivity. In addition, Notch can crosstalk with NF‑κB and other pathways to directly control the expression of pro‑inflammatory cytokines and ion channels.

## 🔬 Mechanisms of Notch Dysregulation in Neuropathic Pain

After peripheral nerve injury (chronic constriction injury, spinal nerve ligation, spared nerve injury, diabetic neuropathy), the Notch pathway is reactivated in a cell‑type‑ and time‑dependent manner at multiple levels of the nociceptive neuraxis.

### 1. Upregulation in Primary Sensory Neurons and Satellite Glial Cells

*   **DRG neurons**: Nerve injury induces the expression of Notch1, Notch3, Jagged1, Dll1, and the downstream target Hes1 in small‑ and medium‑diameter DRG neurons (predominantly nociceptors). This upregulation is driven by transcription factors such as ATF3 and NF‑κB, and by injury‑induced cytokines (TNF‑α, IL‑6) that act on neuronal receptors.
*   **Satellite glial cells (SGCs)** : In the DRG, SGCs upregulate Jagged1 and Notch1. Neuron‑to‑glia Notch signaling (neuronal Dll1 engaging SGC‑Notch) and glia‑to‑neuron signaling (SGC‑Jagged1 engaging neuronal Notch) create a bidirectional loop that amplifies local neuroinflammation, SGC activation, and nociceptor excitability.
*   **Functional consequences**: Notch activation in DRG neurons promotes the expression of pronociceptive ion channels (Nav1.7, Nav1.8, TRPV1) and the neuropeptide CGRP, directly enhancing neuronal excitability and neurotransmitter release.

### 2. Spinal Microglial and Astrocytic Activation

Notch signaling is a central driver of the reactive gliosis that underlies spinal central sensitization.

*   **Microglia**: Following nerve injury, spinal microglia upregulate Notch1, Jagged1, and Hes1. Notch activation polarizes microglia toward a pro‑inflammatory M1 phenotype, characterized by the release of TNF‑α, IL‑1β, IL‑6, BDNF, and reactive oxygen species. The Notch–NF‑κB axis is particularly important: NICD directly interacts with NF‑κB subunits to enhance the transcription of TNF‑α and IL‑1β, while NF‑κB in turn upregulates Notch ligands, creating a feed‑forward inflammatory loop.
*   **Astrocytes**: Astrocytic Notch1 is also upregulated after injury and drives reactive astrogliosis. Activated astrocytes upregulate GFAP, downregulate glutamate transporters (GLT‑1), and secrete pronociceptive mediators (CCL2, CXCL1, IL‑1β). Astrocyte‑to‑microglia Jagged1‑Notch1 signaling further amplifies glial crosstalk and sustains central sensitization.
*   **Pharmacological validation**: Intrathecal delivery of the γ‑secretase inhibitor **DAPT** (N‑[N‑(3,5‑difluorophenacetyl)‑L‑alanyl]‑S‑phenylglycine t‑butyl ester) or **L‑685,458** reduces the number of activated microglia and astrocytes, suppresses cytokine production, and restores the M1/M2 balance, leading to marked attenuation of mechanical allodynia.

### 3. Notch‑Driven Neuroinflammation via NF‑κB Crosstalk

A key mechanism by which Notch promotes pain is its ability to synergize with the NF‑κB pathway.

*   NICD can complex with NF‑κB p50/p65 dimers, displacing the repressor SMRT and recruiting co‑activators to NF‑κB‑responsive promoters. This amplifies the transcription of **TNF‑α**, **IL‑1β**, **IL‑6**, **COX‑2**, and **iNOS**.
*   Simultaneously, TNF‑α and IL‑1β, via their receptors, can activate NF‑κB, which in turn upregulates Notch ligands and receptors, forming a **Notch–NF‑κB positive feedback loop** that sustains neuroinflammation long after the initial injury signal has subsided.
*   Inhibiting Notch with DAPT breaks this loop, reducing both NICD levels and NF‑κB‑dependent inflammatory gene expression in the spinal cord and DRG.

### 4. Direct Neuronal Effects and Central Sensitization

Beyond glial activation, Notch exerts direct effects on nociceptive neurons and synaptic plasticity.

*   **Enhanced glutamatergic transmission**: Notch activation in spinal dorsal horn neurons increases the expression and synaptic trafficking of **GluN2B‑containing NMDA receptors**. This promotes NMDA receptor‑dependent long‑term potentiation (LTP) at nociceptive synapses, a cellular correlate of central sensitization. Notch signaling can also upregulate the voltage‑gated calcium channel subunit α₂δ‑1 (*Cacna2d1*), which facilitates presynaptic glutamate release.
*   **Loss of GABAergic inhibition**: In spinal inhibitory interneurons (parvalbumin‑positive cells), Notch activation can suppress the expression of GAD65 and GAD67, reducing GABA synthesis. This contributes to the disinhibition of nociceptive circuits and mechanical allodynia.
*   **Ion channel modulation**: As noted, Notch upregulates Nav1.7, Nav1.8, and TRPV1 expression in DRG neurons, increasing primary afferent hyperexcitability.

### 5. Supraspinal Notch Signaling and Pain Comorbidities

Notch reactivation in chronic pain is not limited to the spinal cord. In the anterior cingulate cortex (ACC), hippocampus, and amygdala, Notch1 and Hes1 are upregulated, contributing to the emotional and cognitive dimensions of pain.

*   **Affective pain**: Notch‑driven neuroinflammation in the ACC and amygdala promotes anxiety‑ and depression‑like behaviors through the potentiation of excitatory synaptic transmission and dysregulation of hypothalamic‑pituitary‑adrenal (HPA) axis signaling.
*   **Cognitive deficits**: Hippocampal Notch activation impairs neurogenesis and long‑term potentiation, providing a substrate for the memory and learning deficits observed in chronic pain patients.

## 🔬 Preclinical Evidence of Modulation

Pharmacological inhibition of the Notch pathway, particularly with γ‑secretase inhibitors, has been the primary tool for establishing its causal role in neuropathic pain.

### 1. γ‑Secretase Inhibitors (GSIs)

*   **DAPT**: The most widely used tool compound. Intrathecal, intraperitoneal, or oral administration of DAPT in rodents with chronic constriction injury (CCI), spared nerve injury (SNI), spinal nerve ligation (SNL), or streptozotocin‑induced diabetic neuropathy:
    *   Significantly suppresses mechanical allodynia and thermal hyperalgesia in a dose‑dependent manner.
    *   Reduces the expression of NICD, Hes1, and Hey1 in the DRG and spinal dorsal horn.
    *   Inhibits the activation of microglia (Iba‑1) and astrocytes (GFAP), promotes M2 polarization, and decreases pro‑inflammatory cytokine levels (TNF‑α, IL‑1β, IL‑6).
    *   Downregulates Nav1.7, Nav1.8, and TRPV1 in DRG neurons and reduces CGRP release.
    *   Normalizes GluN2B NMDA receptor expression in the spinal cord and restores GABAergic markers.
    *   Importantly, the analgesic effects of DAPT are not observed in naïve animals, indicating that the Notch pathway is specifically engaged in pathological pain states and that its inhibition does not impair normal acute nociception.
*   **L‑685,458**: A more potent, transition‑state analog GSI. Intrathecal L‑685,458 similarly reduces pain behaviors and neuroinflammation in CCI and diabetic neuropathy models.
*   **Semagacestat (LY450139)** : A GSI that reached Phase III clinical trials for Alzheimer’s disease. While its clinical development was halted due to off‑target effects (gastrointestinal toxicity, immunosuppression, skin cancers), it has been used preclinically to validate Notch as a pain target and highlight the translational challenges of systemic GSI use.

### 2. Notch Ligand and Receptor Antagonists

*   **Anti‑Jagged1 antibodies**: Systemic or intrathecal administration of Jagged1‑neutralizing antibodies reduces Notch1 activation in the spinal cord and DRG, attenuates microgliosis, and alleviates mechanical allodynia in CCI models.
*   **Soluble Notch decoy receptors**: Fc‑fusion proteins of the Notch1 extracellular domain act as ligand traps, sequestering Jagged1 and Dll1. These have shown analgesic efficacy in preclinical studies but have limited CNS penetration.

### 3. Genetic Approaches

*   **Conditional knockout of Notch1**: Mice with tamoxifen‑inducible deletion of Notch1 in CX3CR1‑positive cells (microglia/macrophages) or GFAP‑positive cells (astrocytes) show significantly reduced neuropathic pain, microgliosis, and spinal inflammatory cytokine expression after nerve injury.
*   **RBP‑Jκ/CSL deletion**: Genetic deletion of the essential transcription factor RBP‑Jκ in sensory neurons attenuates pain development, confirming the neuronal contribution of Notch signaling.
*   **Hes1 knockdown**: Intrathecal delivery of siRNA targeting Hes1 in the spinal cord reduces downstream Notch target expression, microglial activation, and pain behaviors, with effects comparable to DAPT but greater specificity.

### 4. Natural Compounds and Repurposed Drugs

*   **Curcumin**: Inhibits Notch1 activation and NICD nuclear translocation, reduces Hes1 expression, and alleviates neuropathic pain in CCI and diabetic neuropathy models via Notch/NF‑κB pathway suppression.
*   **Resveratrol**: Suppresses Notch1 expression and Notch‑induced NF‑κB activation; enhances analgesic effects of DAPT when co‑administered.
*   **Isoliquiritigenin**: A flavonoid that inhibits the Notch/Hes1 pathway and reduces neuroinflammation and pain in CCI rats.
*   **Triptolide**: A diterpenoid from *Tripterygium wilfordii* that suppresses Notch1/Jagged1 signaling, decreases spinal cytokine levels, and attenuates pain, though its clinical utility is limited by toxicity.

## 🩺 Clinical Implications and Therapeutic Opportunities

*   **γ‑Secretase inhibitors as a novel analgesic class**: The robust preclinical data with DAPT and related GSIs position Notch as a high‑leverage target. The analgesic effects are specific to pathological pain and are accompanied by a broad suppression of neuroinflammation and central sensitization, suggesting disease‑modifying potential.
*   **Drug repurposing**: Semagacestat and other GSIs have extensive human pharmacokinetic and safety data from Alzheimer’s disease trials, which could accelerate clinical development for pain if the therapeutic window can be adequately widened. The gastrointestinal and immunological toxicities that limited their use in Alzheimer’s disease (where high, chronic dosing was required) might be less prohibitive for pain applications with localized delivery or intermittent dosing.
*   **Localized and cell‑targeted delivery**: Intrathecal administration of GSIs or siRNA can achieve high spinal cord concentrations while avoiding systemic exposure. AAV‑mediated delivery of Notch‑inhibitory microRNAs or dominant‑negative forms of Notch1 could provide sustained, tissue‑restricted pathway suppression. Targeting specific ligands (e.g., Jagged1) or downstream effectors (e.g., Hes1) may offer greater selectivity and a wider therapeutic window than pan‑Notch inhibition with GSIs.
*   **Biomarker potential**: NICD, Hes1, or soluble Notch ligands in cerebrospinal fluid could serve as pharmacodynamic biomarkers for target engagement and patient stratification. Their correlation with pain intensity and treatment response merits investigation.
*   **Combination therapy**: Because Notch signaling drives both neuroinflammation and neuronal sensitization, its inhibition could be synergistically combined with:
    *   **P2X4/P2X7 blockers** (to reduce microglial ATP‑driven activation)
    *   **KCC2 enhancers** (to restore inhibition)
    *   **BDNF/TrkB antagonists** (to block disinhibition)
    *   **Non‑steroidal anti‑inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs)** or **TNF‑α inhibitors** (to break the Notch–NF‑κB loop)

## ⚠️ A Note of Caution and Complexity

*   **Physiological roles of Notch and on‑target toxicity**: Notch signaling is essential for the maintenance of intestinal stem cells, haematopoietic stem cells, and endothelial integrity. Chronic, systemic pan‑Notch inhibition with GSIs causes severe goblet cell metaplasia in the gut, diarrhoea, immunosuppression, and an increased risk of skin cancers (squamous cell carcinoma). These toxicities were the primary reason for the failure of semagacestat in Alzheimer’s disease. Any clinical translation for chronic pain must therefore employ strategies that limit systemic exposure—intrathecal delivery, intermittent dosing, or targeting of specific Notch isoforms (e.g., Notch1, which is less critical for gut homeostasis than Notch2).
*   **Redundancy and isoform‑specific functions**: The four Notch receptors have overlapping but distinct functions. Notch1 and Notch3 are the primary isoforms in glial cells and neurons, while Notch2 is crucial for splenic marginal zone B cells and intestinal stem cells. Selective Notch1/Notch3 inhibitors might achieve analgesia while sparing gut toxicity. Notch3, in particular, is highly expressed in pericytes and vascular smooth muscle and is implicated in neuropathic pain through its role in vascular integrity; its inhibition could have cardiovascular effects.
*   **Dual role in nerve regeneration**: Notch signaling is necessary for the regenerative response of Schwann cells and axons after peripheral nerve injury. While sustained Notch activation promotes pain, complete and prolonged Notch blockade could impair remyelination and axonal outgrowth, delaying functional recovery. The timing of Notch inhibition (perhaps initiated after the acute regenerative window) is critical.
*   **Sex differences**: Emerging evidence suggests that Notch pathway components may be differentially regulated in male and female rodents after nerve injury. Estrogen receptor signaling can interact with Notch, and the magnitude of analgesic response to DAPT may differ between sexes. Clinical trials must be sex‑stratified to account for this possibility.
*   **Interactions with other pain pathways**:
    *   **NF‑κB**: The Notch–NF‑κB feed‑forward loop is a central amplifier of neuroinflammation. Notch inhibition therefore indirectly reduces TNF‑α, IL‑1β, and other NF‑κB target genes.
    *   **Wnt/β‑catenin**: Notch and Wnt pathways crosstalk extensively. β‑catenin can directly activate the *Jagged1* promoter, and NICD can interact with β‑catenin to modulate transcription. Pathological Wnt activation in pain may contribute to Notch reactivation.
    *   **BDNF–TrkB–KCC2**: Notch‑activated microglia release BDNF, which downregulates KCC2 and causes disinhibition. Notch signaling also directly suppresses GAD65 expression, further impairing GABAergic tone.
    *   **TNF‑α**: TNF‑α induces Notch1 and Jagged1 expression, and NICD enhances TNF‑α transcription. This bidirectional loop sustains both pathways.
    *   **P2X4/P2X7 receptors**: ATP activation of microglial purinergic receptors can induce Jagged1 expression, linking purinergic signaling to Notch activation.
    *   **NLRP3 inflammasome**: Notch/NF‑κB signaling upregulates NLRP3 and pro‑IL‑1β, priming the inflammasome for activation, while IL‑1β can feed back to enhance Notch expression.
    *   **Cav3.2 and Nav channels**: Notch drives the expression of Nav1.7, Nav1.8, and TRPV1, linking its transcriptional control to ion channel‑mediated hyperexcitability.
