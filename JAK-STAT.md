# Involvement of JAK/STAT in Neuropathic Pain

The Janus kinase (JAK)/signal transducer and activator of transcription (STAT) pathway is a central intracellular signalling cascade that transduces extracellular cytokine signals into transcriptional programs driving neuroinflammation, glial activation, and neuronal sensitisation in neuropathic pain. Among the four JAK isoforms (JAK1, JAK2, JAK3, TYK2) and seven STAT family members (STAT1–STAT6, including STAT5a and STAT5b), the IL‑6/gp130/JAK2/STAT3 axis is the most extensively validated for its pronociceptive role. After peripheral nerve injury, a surge of pro‑inflammatory cytokines—particularly interleukin‑6 (IL‑6), leukaemia inhibitory factor (LIF), and oncostatin M—activates JAK/STAT signalling in spinal microglia, astrocytes, dorsal root ganglion (DRG) neurons, and supraspinal pain‑processing regions. The resulting gene expression programs promote central sensitisation, suppress inhibitory tone, and sustain chronic pain. Pharmacological inhibition of JAK2 or STAT3, or genetic deletion of STAT3 in glial cells, reliably attenuates mechanical allodynia, thermal hyperalgesia, and neuroinflammatory markers in diverse preclinical models, positioning the JAK/STAT pathway as a high‑value therapeutic target.

### 🧬 The JAK/STAT Pathway: Components and Activation

*   **JAK kinases**: JAK1, JAK2, JAK3, and TYK2 are receptor‑associated tyrosine kinases that are constitutively bound to the intracellular domains of type I and type II cytokine receptors. JAK1 and JAK2 are the isoforms most implicated in neuropathic pain signalling. JAK2 is particularly critical for gp130‑family cytokine signalling.
*   **STAT transcription factors**: STAT1, STAT2, STAT3, STAT4, STAT5a, STAT5b, and STAT6 are latent cytoplasmic transcription factors. Upon JAK‑mediated phosphorylation of a conserved tyrosine residue, STATs dimerise, translocate to the nucleus, and bind to specific DNA response elements to regulate the transcription of hundreds of target genes. **STAT3** is the predominant STAT activated in the spinal cord after nerve injury and is the master regulator of the reactive glial phenotype.
*   **Canonical activation sequence**:
    1.  Cytokine binding induces receptor dimerisation and activation of receptor‑associated JAKs.
    2.  Active JAKs phosphorylate tyrosine residues on the receptor, creating docking sites for STATs via their SH2 domains.
    3.  Receptor‑recruited STATs are phosphorylated by JAKs on a conserved tyrosine (e.g., Tyr705 for STAT3).
    4.  Phosphorylated STATs dissociate, form homo‑ or heterodimers, and translocate to the nucleus.
    5.  Nuclear STAT dimers bind to interferon‑γ‑activated sequence (GAS) elements or interferon‑stimulated response elements (ISREs), driving gene expression.
*   **Negative regulation**:
    *   **SOCS (suppressor of cytokine signalling)** proteins: STAT3 itself induces SOCS3, which binds to the receptor and JAKs to terminate signalling in a negative‑feedback loop.
    *   **PIAS (protein inhibitor of activated STAT)** : Block STAT DNA binding.
    *   **Tyrosine phosphatases**: Dephosphorylate STATs and JAKs.

### 🔬 Mechanisms of JAK/STAT in Neuropathic Pain

Following peripheral nerve injury, JAK/STAT signalling is engaged at multiple sites—peripheral nerve, DRG, spinal cord, and brain—by a variety of injury‑induced cytokines.

*   **Spinal microglial and astrocytic STAT3 activation**: This is the most extensively characterised mechanism. After nerve injury (e.g., spinal nerve ligation, chronic constriction injury, spared nerve injury), IL‑6 and LIF are rapidly upregulated in the spinal cord. They bind to the gp130 receptor complex on microglia and astrocytes, leading to JAK2‑dependent STAT3 phosphorylation. Activated STAT3 drives the transcription of genes that sustain the reactive glial phenotype:
    *   Pro‑inflammatory cytokines: IL‑1β, TNF‑α, IL‑6 itself (autocrine loop).
    *   Chemokines: CCL2, CXCL1, CX3CL1.
    *   Growth factors and neurotrophins: BDNF, NGF, VEGF.
    *   Enzymes: COX‑2, iNOS, MMP‑9.
    *   Glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP) and vimentin (markers of reactive astrogliosis).
    *   The STAT3‑dependent transcriptome in glia is necessary for the full development and maintenance of mechanical allodynia and thermal hyperalgesia.

*   **DRG neuronal JAK/STAT3 and hyperexcitability**: IL‑6 acts directly on DRG neurons to activate JAK/STAT3. This induces the expression of pronociceptive ion channels (Nav1.7, Nav1.8, TRPV1, TRPA1) and suppresses potassium channels (Kv1.4, Kv4.2), leading to increased excitability, ectopic firing, and peripheral sensitisation. STAT3 also drives the expression of CGRP in sensory neurons, enhancing neurogenic inflammation.

*   **Supraspinal JAK/STAT3 and pain comorbidities**: In the anterior cingulate cortex (ACC), hippocampus, and amygdala, IL‑6‑mediated JAK/STAT3 activation contributes to the synaptic dysfunction, reduced neurogenesis, and neuroinflammatory changes that underlie pain‑related anxiety, depression, and cognitive impairment.

*   **JAK2/STAT3 as a convergence point for multiple pronociceptive pathways**: JAK/STAT3 is activated downstream of many of the systems previously discussed in this series:
    *   **TLR4**: TLR4 signalling through MyD88 and NF‑κB induces IL‑6 production, which then activates JAK/STAT3. Thus, JAK/STAT3 transduces the innate immune response into a sustained glial activation programme.
    *   **NLRP3 inflammasome**: IL‑1β released by the NLRP3 inflammasome can, via its receptor (IL‑1R), activate JAK/STAT3 in neurons and glia, linking inflammasome activation to STAT3‑driven gene expression.
    *   **CCL2/CCR2 and CX3CL1/CX3CR1**: These chemokine axes converge on IL‑6 production, which engages JAK/STAT3.
    *   **TNF‑α**: TNF‑α receptor (TNFR1) signalling can activate STAT3 via Src kinases, and TNF‑α also induces IL‑6, amplifying JAK/STAT3 activation.
*   **The IL‑6/JAK2/STAT3 positive‑feedback loop**: STAT3 drives the transcription of IL‑6 itself, as well as IL‑6 receptor (gp80) and the signalling co‑receptor gp130, creating an autocrine/paracrine loop that sustains glial activation long after the initial injury signal has subsided.
*   **JAK/STAT1 and STAT5 in pain modulation**:
    *   **STAT1**: Primarily activated by interferons (IFN‑γ), STAT1 can contribute to microglial M1 polarisation and neuroinflammation. However, its role in neuropathic pain is less prominent and may be context‑dependent.
    *   **STAT5**: Activation by erythropoietin (EPO), growth hormone, and prolactin can have neuroprotective effects. In some contexts, STAT5 may counteract STAT3‑mediated neuroinflammation, indicating that the net effect of JAK/STAT signalling depends on the balance of STAT isoforms activated.
*   **SOCS3 dysregulation**: In chronic neuropathic pain, the negative regulator SOCS3 is often downregulated or its induction is insufficient, leading to sustained STAT3 activity. Restoring SOCS3 expression is a potential therapeutic strategy to terminate aberrant JAK/STAT signalling.

### 🔬 Preclinical Evidence of Modulation

Pharmacological inhibition of JAK or STAT3, as well as genetic deletion, consistently reduces neuropathic pain across a broad range of animal models.

*   **JAK2 inhibitors**:
    *   **AG490**: A classical, relatively selective JAK2 inhibitor. Intrathecal or systemic administration of AG490 significantly reduces mechanical allodynia and thermal hyperalgesia in models of spinal nerve ligation (SNL), chronic constriction injury (CCI), spinal cord injury (SCI), and chemotherapy‑induced peripheral neuropathy (CIPN). AG490 treatment decreases spinal STAT3 phosphorylation, suppresses microglial and astrocytic activation, and lowers pro‑inflammatory cytokine levels.
    *   **Ruxolitinib (INCB018424)** : A dual JAK1/JAK2 inhibitor approved for myelofibrosis. In preclinical pain models, ruxolitinib attenuates mechanical allodynia, reduces glial activation, and normalises STAT3‑dependent gene expression. Its CNS penetration is limited, but intrathecal delivery produces robust analgesia.
    *   **Fedratinib (SAR302503)** : A JAK2‑selective inhibitor that has shown efficacy in reducing neuropathic pain in rodent models.
    *   **AZD1480**: A JAK2 inhibitor that suppresses STAT3 activation and alleviates pain in cancer‑induced bone pain models.
*   **STAT3 inhibitors**:
    *   **Stattic**: A small molecule that inhibits STAT3 dimerisation and nuclear translocation. Subcutaneous or intrathecal Stattic administration alleviates neuropathic pain in models of postherpetic neuralgia, CCI, and diabetic neuropathy. It deactivates JAK1/STAT3 signalling, reduces spinal microgliosis, and normalises pain thresholds.
    *   **S3I‑201**: Another STAT3 dimerisation inhibitor that has shown analgesic efficacy in preclinical neuropathic pain models.
    *   **C188‑9**: A STAT3 inhibitor that targets the phosphotyrosyl peptide binding site; reduces pain and neuroinflammation in animal studies.
    *   **Cell‑permeable STAT3 inhibitory peptides**: Peptide aptamers that block STAT3 dimerisation have been delivered intrathecally and shown to reduce pain behaviours.
*   **Genetic approaches**:
    *   **Conditional STAT3 knockout**: Mice with STAT3 deletion specifically in GFAP‑expressing astrocytes or CX3CR1‑expressing microglia show significantly attenuated mechanical allodynia, reduced gliosis, and lower spinal cytokine levels after nerve injury. Deletion in DRG neurons also reduces pain, confirming a neuronal contribution.
    *   **SOCS3 overexpression**: Viral delivery of SOCS3 to the spinal cord inhibits STAT3 activation and reduces neuropathic pain in CCI and SNL models.
    *   **MicroRNA‑mediated regulation**: Several miRNAs (e.g., miR‑124, miR‑146a) that target IL‑6 or STAT3 are downregulated after nerve injury; restoring their expression mimics JAK/STAT inhibition and alleviates pain.
*   **Electroacupuncture and natural compounds**:
    *   Electroacupuncture at ST36 and GB34 has been shown to suppress IL‑6/JAK2/STAT3 signalling in the spinal cord of neuropathic pain models, contributing to its analgesic effect.
    *   **Resveratrol**, **curcumin**, **paeoniflorin**, and other natural products inhibit JAK2/STAT3 signalling and reduce pain.

### 🩺 Clinical Implications and Therapeutic Opportunities

*   **Drug repurposing with JAK inhibitors**: Several JAK inhibitors are already approved for rheumatoid arthritis, psoriatic arthritis, ulcerative colitis, and other inflammatory conditions, including tofacitinib (JAK1/JAK3), baricitinib (JAK1/JAK2), upadacitinib (JAK1), and ruxolitinib. Their safety profiles are well‑characterised, and some have been tested in chronic pain populations. A clinical trial of baricitinib in patients with chronic low back pain is ongoing. Repurposing these drugs for neuropathic pain is an attractive, fast‑to‑clinic strategy.
*   **STAT3 as a specific target**: STAT3 inhibitors may offer greater selectivity and avoid the immunosuppressive effects of pan‑JAK inhibition, as STAT3 is downstream of only a subset of cytokines. However, no STAT3 inhibitor is yet approved for any indication; several are in early‑phase oncology trials.
*   **Intrathecal or local delivery**: To circumvent systemic immunosuppression and target spinal JAK/STAT3 directly, intrathecal delivery of JAK or STAT3 inhibitors is being explored preclinically. Localised delivery to the DRG or injured nerve is another possibility.
*   **Biomarker potential**: Phosphorylated STAT3 (p‑STAT3) in cerebrospinal fluid immune cells or in skin biopsies may serve as a biomarker of central neuroinflammation. Elevated serum IL‑6 levels have been associated with chronic pain conditions, though they lack specificity.
*   **Combination with other agents**: Because JAK/STAT3 integrates signals from multiple cytokines, combining JAK/STAT3 inhibitors with agents that block upstream triggers (e.g., TNF‑α inhibitors, IL‑6 receptor antibodies) or downstream effectors (e.g., BDNF/TrkB blockade) could achieve synergistic analgesia.

### ⚠️ A Note of Caution and Complexity

*   **Immunosuppression and infection risk**: JAK/STAT signalling is essential for haematopoiesis, antiviral immunity, and immune surveillance. Broad‑spectrum or prolonged JAK inhibition increases the risk of serious infections (e.g., herpes zoster, tuberculosis), as observed with tofacitinib and baricitinib in rheumatoid arthritis. STAT3‑specific inhibitors may have a narrower immunosuppressive profile but can still impair IL‑10‑mediated anti‑inflammatory responses, as STAT3 is also activated by IL‑10.
*   **Physiological roles of STAT3**: STAT3 is critical for liver regeneration, cardiac protection, and neurodevelopment. Systemic inhibition can cause hepatotoxicity, anaemia, and neurotoxicity. The therapeutic window for JAK/STAT inhibition in chronic pain is therefore likely narrow, favouring localised or intermittent dosing.
*   **Paradoxical effects of STAT3 in different cell types**: While STAT3 in spinal glia is pronociceptive, STAT3 activation in certain neuronal populations or in peripheral nerves may promote regeneration and repair. Ablating STAT3 globally could impair recovery from nerve injury.
*   **Redundancy among STAT family members**: STAT1 and STAT5 may compensate for STAT3 loss in some contexts, and their activation by interferons and growth factors could produce conflicting effects on pain. Selective targeting of STAT3 over STAT1 is required.
*   **Temporal dynamics**: JAK/STAT3 signalling is important in both the initiation and maintenance phases of neuropathic pain, but the dominant cellular source shifts over time (from microglia to astrocytes). The optimal timing of intervention may need to account for this transition.
*   **Sex differences**: Some preclinical studies indicate that the IL‑6/JAK2/STAT3 pathway is more prominent in male rodents, while females may depend more on adaptive immune mechanisms. This could translate to sex‑specific efficacy of JAK/STAT inhibitors.
*   **Interactions with other pain pathways**: JAK/STAT3 is downstream of nearly every major neuroimmune signalling cascade discussed in this series:
    *   It transduces signals from **TLR4**, **TNF‑α**, **IL‑1β**, **CCL2**, and **CX3CL1**, integrating diverse upstream inputs into a common transcriptional program.
    *   STAT3 drives the expression of **BDNF** and downregulates **KCC2**, contributing to disinhibition.
    *   STAT3 induces **COX‑2** and **mPGES‑1**, increasing prostaglandin synthesis.
    *   It promotes **VEGF** and **MMP‑9** expression, linking to vascular remodelling and barrier disruption.
    *   STAT3 upregulates the **NLRP3** inflammasome components, creating a feed‑forward loop with innate immunity.
    *   Thus, JAK/STAT3 inhibition could be a high‑leverage intervention that simultaneously addresses multiple parallel pain‑driving pathways.
