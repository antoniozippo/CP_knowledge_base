## Findings vs Existing Claims

### F5.1: NISCI phase 2b — anti-Nogo-A antibody NG101 missed its primary endpoint
- **Evidence:** Multicenter randomized double-blind placebo-controlled phase 2b trial (n=129 randomized; six intrathecal NG101 injections over 4 weeks after acute cervical SCI). Primary endpoint UEMS change at 6 months: no significant difference vs placebo (ΔUEMS 1.37 [95% CI −1.44 to 4.18]). Secondary SCIM self-care improved; post-hoc subgroup with motor-incomplete injury showed UEMS/SCIM gains below MCID thresholds. Well tolerated; rates of spasticity were ~half of placebo and no excess neuropathic pain emerged. [Kucher/curci-led NISCI consortium, Lancet Neurol 2025, PMID 39706632; NCT03935321]
- **Relation to KB:** EC-0094 context and the dossier's claim that NG101 "demonstrated improved neurological and functional recovery" → **CONTRADICTS** (efficacy optimism not supported at population level; mechanism EC-0094 itself remains intact — NG101 does neutralize Nogo-A). Partially SUPPORTS dossier caution on therapeutic windows (EC-0095): benefit signals confined to motor-incomplete subgroup.

### F5.2: Anti-LINGO-1 (opicinumab) — three consecutive phase 2 failures close the remyelination route
- **Evidence:** SYNERGY (n=419, relapsing MS): no dose-linear improvement in confirmed disability improvement (primary missed; non-monotonic dose-response). RENEW (acute optic neuritis): remyelination by VEP latency did not differ from placebo in ITT at week 24 (only per-protocol signal). AFFINITY Part 1 (n=263): ODRS difference 0.15 (−0.05 to 0.35), p=0.148 — negative. Program effectively discontinued. [Cadavid et al., Lancet Neurol 2019, DOI 10.1016/S1474-4422(19)30137-1; Cadavid et al., Lancet Neurol 2017, PMID 28229892; AFFINITY, Mult Scler, PMID 41454463]
- **Relation to KB:** Dossier statement "LINGO-1 antagonists … may have analgesic benefits by promoting remyelination" → **CONTRADICTS** as a clinical-translational premise (no human evidence of remyelination efficacy, and pain outcomes were never measured); the mechanistic co-receptor role of LINGO-1 in the NgR1 complex (dossier §NgR1 system; Yiu & He framework) remains **SUPPORTED** preclinically.

### F5.3: Nogo-A→TRPV1 stabilization axis reconfirmed and extended to a microtubule arm
- **Evidence:** Lineage confirming the KB's core DRG mechanism: Li et al., Sci Signal 2014;7(334):ra61 (LIMK-dependent actin polymerization in primary sensory neurons drives inflammatory heat hyperalgesia via TRPV1); Hu et al., FASEB J 2019;33(1):668-682 (Nogo-A maintains TRPV1 function in rat DRG neurons); Chen et al., IJMS 2021;22(19):10360, PMID 34638704 (Nogo-A promotes microtubule polymerization via inhibiting CRMP2 phosphorylation; paclitaxel rescues TRPV1 down-regulation in Nogo-A-KO rats; nocodazole attenuates CFA heat hyperalgesia and SNI mechanical pain).
- **Relation to KB:** EC-0069 (LIMK phosphorylates/inhibits cofilin), EC-0030 (cofilin depolymerizes actin), dossier section "Direct Stabilization of TRPV1" → **SUPPORTS**; Chen 2021 adds a parallel CRMP2/microtubule branch acting on TRPV1 protein stability → **EXTENDS**.

### F5.4: RhoA/ROCK–p38 MAPK microglial cascade reconfirmed as current standard model
- **Evidence:** Tatsumi E, Yamanaka H, Kobayashi K, et al. Glia 2015;63(2):216-228, DOI 10.1002/glia.22745 — RhoA/ROCK mediates p38 MAPK activation and morphological changes downstream of P2Y12/13 receptors in spinal microglia in neuropathic pain. Independently reconfirmed by P2Y12-GTP-RhoA/ROCK2-p38 work (Zhang et al., PubMed 30778044) and consolidated in a 2026 narrative review covering RhoA/ROCK across pain models [Exploring the Role of RhoA/ROCK Signaling in Pain, Aging Dis 2026;17(2):981-1001, DOI 10.14336/AD.2024.1539].
- **Relation to KB:** EC-0107 (RhoA activates ROCK), RhoA-ROCK.md §2 (microglial p38 switch), EC-0074 (MMP-9 upregulates ROCK), EC-0146 context (microglial BDNF/ROCK interplay) → **SUPPORTS**. Note: the sweep brief labeled "Tatsumi 2015" as an *NgR1* landmark; the actual paper is microglial RhoA/ROCK (fits Theme 5's RhoA half; see Limitations).

### F5.5: Fasudil/riprasudil preclinical base widened; dual regenerative role reinforced
- **Evidence:** (a) Fasudil augments axon regeneration, remyelination and functional recovery after sciatic nerve transection via ROCK/PI3K/AKT [Li et al., J Orthop Translat 2024, DOI 10.1016/j.jot.2024.06.006]; (b) fasudil prevents neuropathic pain in Minamata-disease model rats [Fujimura et al., Toxicol Lett 2022]; (c) intrathecal ripasudil suppresses microglial p-p38/Iba1 activation and raises hindpaw withdrawal threshold at days 14-28 after thoracic SCI [Kishima et al., Spine 2021, DOI 10.1016/j.spinee.2020.08.011]; (d) Y-27632 attenuates rhizotomy cold hyperalgesia while promoting monoaminergic sprouting [Ramer et al., J Neurosci 2004;24(48):10796]; CXCR4→RhoA/ROCK2 blockade by fasudil abrogates bone-cancer pain [Xu et al., Mol Pain 2020, DOI 10.1177/1744806920919568].
- **Relation to KB:** EC-0100 (ROCK inhibited by fasudil), QC-0050 (ripasudil SCI allodynia up to 28 days), QC-0051 (RhoA siRNA > BA-210 durability), EC-0110 (context-dependent regeneration-vs-pain roles) → **SUPPORTS** all four; item (a) strengthens the KB caution that ROCK inhibition is pro-regenerative in peripheral nerve repair → **EXTENDS** EC-0110.

### F5.6: LPA–LPAR1–RhoA–ROCK demyelination cascade extended to peripheral glia and new antagonists
- **Evidence:** (a) Cobra-venom selective A-fiber demyelination model: injury-elevated LPA acts on LPA1R of satellite glial cells around large DRG neurons → Cx43 gap-junction coupling spreads activation to SGCs of nociceptive neurons; Ki16425 reverses sensitization [Demyelination-related pain: role of lysophosphatidic acid in satellite glial cell–neuron crosstalk, J Transl Med 2025, DOI 10.1186/s12967-025-07568-y]; (b) novel LPA1 antagonist UD-039 blocks LPA-induced Schwann-cell dedifferentiation (MBP/PMP22↓, p75↑, CCL2↑) and intraneural demyelination; oral prodrug UD-059Na restores allodynia, nerve conduction velocity and intraepidermal fiber density in diabetic rats [IASP World Congress 2024 posters UD-039 / UD-059Na]; (c) oxypeucedanin relieves SNI/CCI pain by blocking spinal LPAR1/RhoA/ROCK (p-MYPT1↓), reversed by LPAR1 over-expression [Oxypeucedanin, PMC12808916]; (d) authoritative review consolidating Gα12/13-RhoA/ROCK-PKCγ dorsal-root demyelination, LPA-driven Cavα2δ1/ephrin-B1 upregulation and LPA1/3 positive-feedback amplification [Decoding lysophosphatidic acid signaling, Sig Transduct Target Ther 2025, DOI 10.1038/s41392-025-02423-4].
- **Relation to KB:** EC-0108 (RhoA activates LPA-RhoA-Demyelination Cascade via LPAR1) → **SUPPORTS**; items (a)+(b) add a Schwann-cell/SGC peripheral arm absent from the KB dossier (which frames LPA demyelination mainly at dorsal roots) → **EXTENDS** + **NEW_MECHANISM**; (b)/(c) → **NOVEL_TARGET** candidates.

### F5.7: ORL1/nociceptin receptor controls NgR1 maturation and surface expression
- **Evidence:** Trauma upregulates ORL1 in NgR1-deficient expression profiles; ORL1 co-immunoprecipitates with immature NgR1, enhances O-linked glycosylation and surface delivery; ORL1 antagonist J113397 promotes locomotor recovery additively with NgR1 deletion after spinal hemisection [Sekine et al., Sci Signal 2018;11(559):eaao4180, DOI 10.1126/scisignal.aao4180].
- **Relation to KB:** No equivalent edge exists → **NEW_MECHANISM**: an opioid-system receptor gating NgR1 abundance provides a pharmacological handle upstream of every NgR1-centered claim (EC-0106, EC-0095, EC-0151).

### F5.8: NgR1 necessity for myelin inhibition is contested — acute collapse yes, chronic inhibition no
- **Evidence:** NgR1-null or shRNA-knockdown neurons remain fully inhibited by substrate-bound MAG/OMgp/Nogo-66 while soluble ligand-induced growth-cone collapse is attenuated; PirB, NgR2/gangliosides and NgR1-independent pathways mediate chronic inhibition [Chivatakarn et al., J Neurosci 2007;27(27):7117; Zheng et al. 2005; commentary J Neurosci 2007;27(43):11451]. Triple knockouts show tract-specific, sometimes opposing effects (MAG deletion reduced CST sprouting) [reviewed in PMC4122599].
- **Relation to KB:** Dossier's convergence framing ("NgR1 complex is the convergence point for multiple myelin-derived inhibitors") holds for acute signaling but is qualified for chronic inhibition → **CONTRADICTS** (nuance) against an unqualified reading of EC-0106/EC-0095; supports the KB's own redundancy caution (NogoA-NgR1.md "Redundancy Among Myelin Inhibitors") → partial **SUPPORTS**.

### F5.9: Independent regulators of TRPV1 membrane content parallel the Nogo-A/NgR1 mechanism
- **Evidence:** AIBP (APOA1-binding protein) disrupts neuronal TLR4-lipid rafts, displacing TRPV1, reducing p-TRPV1 and capsaicin responses, reversing paclitaxel-CIPN allodynia [Brain Behav Immun 2023, PMC10182209]; ZDHHC4-mediated palmitoylation routes TRPV1 to lysosomal degradation (APT1 counteracts), setting the duration of inflammatory heat hypersensitivity [EMBO Rep 2024, DOI 10.1038/s44319-024-00317-0]; Tbx5/GATA4/Brd4 epigenetic activation of Trpv1 transcription in SNL allodynia [J Neurosci 2024;44(39)].
- **Relation to KB:** Dossier TRPV1-stabilization theme → **EXTENDS** (the same behavioral output is governed by raft-, palmitoylation-, and transcription-level mechanisms independent of NgR1); ZDHHC4/APT1 and AIBP → **NOVEL_TARGET** candidates complementary to NgR1 blockade.

### F5.10: New upstream activators of RhoA/ROCK in pain models
- **Evidence:** USP53 stabilizes FKBP51 (deubiquitination), activating FKBP51/RhoA/ROCK in CCI; USP53 knockdown reduces GTP-RhoA/ROCK1, TNF-α/IL-1β and allodynia [Acta Neurobiol Exp 2022;82]; CXCR4-SDF1 engages RhoA/ROCK2 in spinal neurons for bone-cancer pain [Mol Pain 2020, DOI 10.1177/1744806920919568]; LPAR1 over-expression alone suffices to reproduce allodynia with RhoA/p-MYPT1 activation [PMC12808916].
- **Relation to KB:** EC-0113 (TNF-α activates RhoA), EC-0078 (mitochondrial dysfunction → RhoA/ROCK), EphA/CXCR4 upstream entry in RhoA-ROCK.md §Upstream Regulators → **SUPPORTS** + **EXTENDS** (USP53/FKBP51 node is new).

## Contradictions & Contested Areas
1. **Clinical translation of myelin-inhibitor blockade is far weaker than the KB dossier tone:** NISCI primary endpoint negative (NG101, Lancet Neurol 2025) vs dossier text "NG101 improved neurological and functional recovery"; opicinumab failed SYNERGY, RENEW (ITT) and AFFINITY Part 1. The dossier should be softened to "well tolerated; subgroup/post-hoc signals only."
2. **NgR1-centricity:** NgR1 required for acute growth-cone collapse but dispensable for chronic substrate-bound myelin inhibition (Chivatakarn 2007; Zheng 2005); PirB/NgR2 redundancy. Any graph path implying NgR1 is the obligatory conduit for MAG/OMgp/Nogo-A pain signaling needs qualification.
3. **Claim-directionality anomalies flagged for curation:** EC-0106 ("RhoA GTPase --activates--> NgR1") inverts the canonical direction (ligand-NgR1 → RhoA-GTP; likely intended as functional interaction); EC-0094 sign convention reads "Nogo-A neutralizes NG101 (−1)" whereas biologically NG101 neutralizes Nogo-A.
4. **Biphasic/context effects of ROCK inhibition** (pronociceptive at low peripheral doses; pro-regenerative centrally) — already flagged in KB cautions; JOT 2024 adds a pro-remyeliating instance.
5. **No replication wave for NgR1–TRPV1 trafficking since 2021:** searches 2021-2026 returned adjacent TRPV1 literature but no new primary confirmation of the Nogo-A/LIMK/cofilin TRPV1-stabilization module; confidence rests on the 2014/2019/2021 lineage.

## Landmark Classics Reconfirmed
- **Yiu & He, Exp Neurol 2006;201(1):8-21** ("White matter inhibitors in CNS axon regeneration failure"): tripartite NgR1/p75-TROY/LINGO-1 complex, Nogo/MAG/OMgp convergence on RhoA-ROCK — reconfirmed verbatim by 2023-2024 reviews (NRR 2023; MDPI Biology 2024;13:703; IJMS 2023;24:4479). SUPPORTS EC-0095 context, EC-0107.
- **Tatsumi E et al., Glia 2015;63(2):216-228** (DOI 10.1002/glia.22745): RhoA/ROCK mediates p38-MAPK activation and microglial morphology downstream of P2Y12/13 — directly reconfirms the KB microglial cascade (EC-0107; RhoA-ROCK.md §2). Note: this is a microglial RhoA/ROCK landmark, not an NgR1/TRPV1 one as labeled in the sweep brief.
- **Inoue M et al., Cell 2004 (LPA initiates neuropathic pain)** — repeatedly reconfirmed within 2024-2025 LPA literature as the founding observation behind EC-0108/QC claims.
- **Kim JE et al. 2004 / Chivatakarn 2007 (NgR1 genetics)** — foundational, but their divergent outcomes are themselves the contested area (F5.8).
- **NgR1–TRPV1 lineage:** Li Y et al., Sci Signal 2014;7:ra61 (LIMK/cofilin→TRPV1) + Hu F et al., FASEB J 2019;33:668-682 (Nogo-A maintains TRPV1) stand as the de-facto landmarks for the dossier's TRPV1-stabilization claim; no "Tatsumi 2015 NgR1 pain" paper exists under that author-year combination.

## Sweep Limitations
- **Unresolved landmark:** no paper matching "Xie 2017 RhoA/ROCK pain" could be unambiguously identified; nearest lexical neighbors (Xie F et al., Neurochem Res 2016 — CXCR4 antagonist AMD3100 in SNL) concern a different target. Recommend the KB verify which Xie reference was originally intended.
- **Landmark mislabeling in brief:** "Tatsumi 2015 NgR1 pain" resolves to Tatsumi E et al., Glia 2015 (microglial RhoA/ROCK–p38); the NgR1–TRPV1 landmark set is Li 2014 / Hu 2019 / Chen 2021 instead.
- **Field dormancy:** no 2021-2026 primary study specifically replicating NgR1-dependent TRPV1 membrane trafficking was found; recent TRPV1-trafficking advances come from non-NgR1 mechanisms (F5.9).
- Unverified KB detail: sickle-cell-disease Nogo-A/NgR1/ROCK DRG claim (dossier ref:6) was not independently re-searched this sweep.
- Some sources are conference posters (UD-039/UD-059Na) or PMC records without full bibliographic fields retrieved; DOIs/PMIDs cited where available.
- Websearch snippets occasionally truncated; quantitative details quoted here reflect snippet-visible content only.
- One filter false positive (QC-0047, P2X7 ATP affinity) included in the 32 matches; it is unrelated to Theme 5 and was not classified.
